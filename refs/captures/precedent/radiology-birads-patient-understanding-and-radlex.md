- Wikipedia, "BI-RADS," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BI-RADS — status: SECONDARY/tertiary, retrieved 2026-09-14 (snippet only)

### What the snippets show (paraphrase — none of these were independently fetched in full; treat as leads for verbatim confirmation)
- A callback (Category 0) is repeatedly and explicitly reframed for patients as NOT a diagnosis: one patient-facing source states plainly "BI-RADS 0: Incomplete. This is not a diagnosis. It means the radiologist couldn't make a complete assessment from the images taken and needs more information," and stresses "it does not mean something is wrong."
- The pattern across multiple independent patient-education sources restating the same "this does not mean cancer" disclaimer is itself evidence that patient misunderstanding of Category 0 is a **recognized, recurring problem** significant enough that hospitals and advocacy sites maintain standing explainer content for it — i.e., the codified professional distinction (0 vs. 1/2) does not automatically transfer to lay comprehension, and requires active, repeated lay-facing translation.
- Per the Wikipedia snippet: the official BI-RADS documentation itself is scoped to "patient reports used by medical professionals," not the "lay reports" given directly to patients — i.e., the controlled vocabulary is a professional-to-professional standard; a *separate* translation layer (lay letters) is needed to carry the same distinction to the affected party. ACR does provide sample "lay letters" for this purpose per its own FAQ document (see `radiology-birads-categories.md` source, page 6: "Yes, the ACR has samples of lay letters for all of the BI-RADS® final assessment categories").

### Honest limitation
No peer-reviewed comprehension study (e.g., a survey measuring what fraction of patients correctly distinguish "Category 0" from "Category 1/2" after reading their report) was located in this pass. The claim "patients struggle with Category 0" is well-supported as a *professionally recognized concern* (multiple independent patient-facing orgs maintain standing explainer content for exactly this confusion) but is not backed here by a quantified study. Flag any number (e.g., "X% of patients misunderstand") as unverified if it appears elsewhere.

### Finding: could NOT verify specific RadLex terms/RIDs for "not visualized," "technically limited study," or "obscured by artifact"
This is an honest negative result, not an absence-of-effort result: search-engine synthesis of academic literature about RadLex repeatedly surfaces the *general* fact that RadLex has acknowledged coverage gaps for exactly this class of descriptive/qualifier language (as opposed to anatomic/pathologic nouns, which it covers well) — e.g., one study mapping real report language to RadLex found "123/160 (76.9%) items showed literal RadLex equivalents... 7/160 (4.4%) entities could not eventually be transferred adequately into the RadLex ontology," and a separate radiology-report-NLP paper notes some frequently used descriptive terms "are not present in the RadLex lexicon" and must be added manually from corpora. Neither source, nor the ontology browser itself, could be reached in this session to confirm or deny a specific RID for "not visualized" et al.

**Disposition: mark this as an open lead, not a finding.** Do not cite RadLex as having (or lacking) a specific controlled term for these visualization-limitation concepts without a direct ontology-browser lookup (retry bioportal.bioontology.org/ontologies/RADLEX or ncithesaurus/RadLex's own site, radlex.org, which was not reachable via WebFetch in this session either — both attempts 403'd, consistent with anti-bot protection rather than confirmed absence of content).

## Inference (ours)
If RadLex genuinely lacks a first-class controlled term cleanly separating "not visualized because out of field of view" from "not visualized because obscured by artifact/technical failure" — which the evidence above suggests but does not confirm — that would itself be a data point for arcviz: even radiology's own terminology-standardization project, decades in, has NOT fully closed the "why don't we know" sub-distinction at the controlled-vocabulary level, even though the reporting *practice* (BI-RADS Category 0, the ACR communication practice parameter's "potential limitations" clause) mandates that the fact of not-knowing be surfaced. This suggests arcviz's three-way distinction (withheld / missing referent / blinded-topology) may be asking for a finer grain than even mature medical-imaging standards have fully codified in vocabulary — though the *reporting obligation* to surface an unknown as distinct from a negative is very well established.
