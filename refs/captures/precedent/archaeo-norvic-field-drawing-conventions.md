## Verbatim line conventions (p.1)

> - Solid Line: Use for the edges of features or deposits
> - Dash-dot: Edge of excavation, edge of baulks, sections and sondages
> - Dash-dot-dot: Use for truncated features (such as where a deposit has been truncated by modern disturbance)
> - **Dashed Line: use for ephemeral edges, suggested continuation, removed features, unclear relationships and diffuse boundaries** (use sparingly, use a solid line where relationships are clear, always annotate dashed lines if necessary to communicate their meaning)
> - Bold Line: Use for Solid Structures and Pipe Trenches and annotate such features clearly

## Uncertain-feature labelling convention (example drawing, p.1)

The worked example plan labels one feature "**?DITCH**" — a leading question mark prefixed to the interpreted feature type, a common informal UK field convention for "probably a ditch, not certain," distinct from an unqualified label for a confidently identified feature.

## Relevance to arcviz

- **"Dash-dot: Edge of excavation"** is the direct analogue of "the limit of what has been investigated" — distinct from "dashed = inferred feature." This is the closest thing found in this research pass to the "not investigated" vs. "investigated, nothing found" distinction the assignment asked about: the edge-of-excavation line marks a boundary of INQUIRY (we stopped digging here), not a boundary of a FEATURE. Everything past that dash-dot line is simply outside the record's scope — neither asserted present nor absent.
- **"Suggested continuation"** under the dashed-line definition is a documented convention for "we believe this edge keeps going past what we can currently see" (e.g., a wall alignment projected into an unexcavated area based on the visible portion) — a partial analogue for arcviz's "known to exist, contents/extent withheld," though it requires the visible portion to establish the trajectory, unlike arcviz's case where nothing about the target node is visible at all.
- This document independently corroborates the Historic England four-way wall convention's dashed-for-uncertain pattern, suggesting solid-vs-dashed-for-certainty is genuinely a cross-organization UK convention, not one unit's idiosyncrasy — worth citing as convergent practice even though this specific document isn't independently authoritative.

## What was NOT found here

No convention in this document for: a feature referenced from elsewhere but entirely absent from this trench (arcviz's "missing referent"), or a relationship of known origin/multiplicity but unknown destination (arcviz's "blinded edge"). Same gap as noted in the Historic England capture — this document's scope is a single trench's own excavated ground, not a network of cross-referenced trenches/records.
