# FOIA exemption-marking requirement — capture

Status: PRIMARY (statute text, fetched directly from Cornell LII's codification of 5 U.S.C. § 552).
Retrieved: 2026-09-14.
URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/552

## The requirement (subsection (b), closing paragraph — the segregability/marking clause)

> "The amount of information deleted, and the exemption under which the deletion is made, shall be indicated on the released portion of the record, unless including that indication would harm an interest protected by the exemption in this subsection under which the deletion is made. If technically feasible, the amount of the information deleted, and the exemption under which the deletion is made, shall be indicated at the place in the record where such deletion is made."

Locator: 5 U.S.C. § 552(b) (closing sentences, added by the Electronic Freedom of Information Act Amendments of 1996, Pub. L. 104-231). This is the clause agencies rely on to produce the familiar "[REDACTED — (b)(5)]" or "(b)(1)" markings printed directly on released pages.

## A parallel, older marking requirement (subsection (a)(2) — "reading room" materials)

> "However, in each case the justification for the deletion shall be explained fully in writing, and the extent of such deletion shall be indicated on the portion of the record which is made available or published, unless including that indication would harm an interest protected by the exemption in subsection (b) under which the deletion is made. If technically feasible, the extent of the deletion shall be indicated at the place in the record where the deletion was made."

Locator: 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(2) (applies to opinions, policy statements, staff manuals, and other proactively-published/reading-room materials, as opposed to (b)'s general request-response marking rule).

## What this establishes (fact, not inference)

Both clauses require THREE things simultaneously wherever technically feasible: (1) the deletion is visually located ("at the place in the record where such deletion is made" — i.e., in situ, not just in a cover letter), (2) the *amount/extent* of what was deleted is indicated (so the redaction communicates its own size), and (3) the specific *category of reason* (the exemption code, e.g. (b)(1)–(b)(9)) is attached to that specific deletion, not asserted once for the whole document. Both carry an explicit safety-valve: the indication can be omitted only if making the indication itself "would harm an interest protected by the exemption" — i.e., the statute anticipates a case where even signaling "something is redacted here, and why" is itself unsafe. This safety-valve is the statutory ancestor of the Glomar problem (see redaction-glomar.md) — it is the point in the text where the law admits that even the *typed, in-situ marker* can leak too much.
