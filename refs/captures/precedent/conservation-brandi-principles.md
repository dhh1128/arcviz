# Cesare Brandi's Theory of Restoration — principles and direct quotes

Status: PRIMARY-adjacent. These are full-text captures (via pdftotext of fetched PDFs) of two
published scholarly essays that quote and translate Brandi's *Teoria del Restauro* (1963) and
his earlier ICR bulletin articles directly, with page citations to the Italian original. I could
not obtain the full 2005 ICR English translation of *Theory of Restoration* itself (not freely
available online), so these secondary essays — which quote Brandi verbatim in English
translation with page numbers — are the best available evidence short of buying the book.
Retrieved 2026-09-14.

## Source 1: Sebastiano Barassi, "Dreaming of a universal approach: Brandi's Theory of
Restoration and the conservation of contemporary art" (paper presented London, 24 Sept 2009)

URL: https://www.icom-cc.org/dlfile.aspx?file=https://www.icom-cc.org/docs/content/Sebastiano-Barassi-paper.pdf

Full text captured to /tmp (not re-saved here in full; key passage below is verbatim from the
pdftotext extraction).

Barassi states Brandi's Theory (1963) "provided the foundations for the 1972 Carta del restauro
(Charter of restoration), the official document guiding conservation in most Italian museums to
this day," and that its strength lies in "simple and unequivocal guidelines on what is ethically
acceptable, and unacceptable, in conservation." He then enumerates, verbatim:

> "1. The unacceptability of creative conservation. That is: a conservator must never attempt to
> substitute the artist, to imitate his/her style and to interpret the work subjectively,
> especially when it comes to filling in lacunas. ...
> 2. The imperative of preservation of the patina. ...
> 3. The complete reversibility of any conservation work.
> 4. The need ... to proceed always case by case ..."

This is the cleanest available secondary statement of Brandi's four core principles, with (1)
and (3) directly load-bearing for the "distinguishable, not invented" doctrine.

## Source 2: Chiara L. M. Occelli, "The tradition of Brandi" (trans. Mary Ann Macintosh),
published in Conversaciones / ICCROM

URL: https://www.iccrom.org/sites/default/files/publications/2020-05/the_tradition_of_brandi.pdf

Key verbatim passages (with Occelli's own citations to Brandi's original texts):

> "to keep any integration within the strictest limitations, with practical implementations that
> are recognizable to everyone, so as not to use the hypothetical reintegration to usurp the
> authenticity which belongs only to the ways in which it was created" (Brandi, 1949: 698)

> "restoration is not creation, and restorers are not artists – firstly they are critics, then
> technicians" (Brandi, 1949: 698)

> "whatever damage to that which is represented must be remedied" provided the intervention does
> not seek to make itself "independent of history" (Brandi, 1950a: 11) — "there is once again
> emphasis on ... the importance of the possibility of distinguishing between a new contribution
> and that which was pre-existent" (Brandi, 1950a: 11-12)

And — directly relevant to arcviz's "leave it visibly unknown" state (state 1/3, not filling at
all):

> "there are cases which 'either due to the extremely fragmentary state of the artwork, or to a
> prevalence for historical over aesthetic interest, the path chosen is not to complete
> anything' (Brandi, 1963a: 23). There are lacunae for which an intervention, even 'giving up the
> development of the residual figurativeness of the image' (Brandi, 1963a: 23) is necessary..."

Occelli's gloss: "it might be deduced that, since the object of restoration is the
re-establishment of the potential unity of the artwork, and the theme of the lacuna is outside
that object, this does not constitute a real action of 'primary restoration'."

**This is the strongest evidence for a graded/triage doctrine** (see finding #3 in final
report): Brandi's own theory recognizes (a) reintegration/fill (tratteggio, "hypothetical,"
bounded, recognizable), and (b) a distinct, legitimate choice to leave the lacuna
un-reintegrated entirely when evidence is too fragmentary or historical value outweighs
aesthetic unity — i.e., NOT a simple binary of "filled vs. not conservation-treated," but a
real fork in doctrine between "fill it (visibly)" and "leave the hole visible, on purpose."
