## The load-bearing structural fact: "potential limitations" is its own mandatory report component, separate from "findings"

Section II.B ("Components of the Report") lists the body of the report as five parts. Two of them are the ones that matter for the "not visualized ≠ normal" distinction, and ACR keeps them as **separate, independently-labeled subsections**, not folded into each other:

> "b. Findings: The report should use appropriate anatomic, pathologic, and radiologic terminology to describe the findings.
> c. Potential limitations: The report should, when appropriate, identify factors that may compromise the sensitivity and specificity of the examination.
> d. Clinical issues: The report should address or answer any specific clinical questions. **If there are factors that prevent answering the clinical question, these should be stated.**"
> — ACR Practice Parameter for Communication of Diagnostic Imaging Findings, Section II.B.3 ("Body of the report")

The sentence "If there are factors that prevent answering the clinical question, these should be stated" is the exact professional-liability analogue of arcviz's HP1: it is a standing obligation to say, in words distinguishable from a finding, that the study *could not answer the question*, as opposed to answering it in the negative.

## Framing principle (Section I, Introduction)

> "Effective communication is a critical component of diagnostic imaging. Quality patient care can only be achieved when study results are conveyed in a timely fashion to the patient and to those responsible for treatment decisions."

## What this document does NOT give us

It does not use the specific phrases "not visualized," "technically limited," or "obscured by artifact" verbatim — those are report-writing conventions taught in residency/education literature and used ad hoc by radiologists, not controlled vocabulary mandated by this practice parameter. The parameter mandates *that* limitations be disclosed, not the literal wording. (See `radiology-vague-report-liability-berlin.md` for why the wording itself is a documented liability battleground, and for the BI-RADS counter-example of a domain that DID standardize the wording.)

## Preamble caveat (own limitation, stated by ACR)

> "Practice Parameters and Technical Standards are not inflexible rules or requirements of practice and are not intended, nor should they be used, to establish a legal standard of care." (cites *Iowa Medical Society and Iowa Society of Anesthesiologists v. Iowa Board of Nursing*, 831 N.W.2d 826 (Iowa 2013))

So this document does not itself carry direct legal force — courts have held ACR standards are "useful in determining the duty owed" but do not themselves set the standard of care (citing a concurrence in *Stanley v. McCarver*, 63 P.3d 1076 (Ariz. App. 2003)). Note this nuance if citing the parameter as "the law."
