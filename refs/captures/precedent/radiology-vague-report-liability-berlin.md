## Quotes (as reproduced by the secondary source, attributed to Berlin)

On what "vague" means and why it's a problem in a diagnostic report:

> "Vague: Wandering, roaming, unsettled, uncertain...not definite in meaning, not explicit or precise, of indistinct ideas...absence of clear perception or understanding...meaningless." (Berlin, opening definitional framing)

On liability mechanism — vague language can fail to prompt needed action:

> "Did the language used in the radiology reports lull the referring physicians into inaction and lethargy...rather than alert them to the fact that prompt action must be taken?" (Berlin)

On the radiologist's own independent duty (i.e., blaming the referring physician's inaction doesn't excuse an ambiguous report):

> "Radiologists must remember that they have an independent duty to be accurate and concise, regardless of whether the actions of the referring physician are negligent." (Berlin)

**On BI-RADS specifically as the field's own fix for this exact problem — the load-bearing quote for this whole research assignment:**

> "By providing clear and succinct language for reports, BI-RADS has achieved its goal of making communication of mammographic findings more accurate and decisive. Ambiguity and vagueness have, for the most part, been eliminated in mammography." (Berlin)

A second web-search pass surfaced an equivalent paraphrase of this same point, independently, from the search engine's own synthesis of the primary/secondary literature (not a separate source, cross-check only):

> "...it has already been accomplished in the subspecialty of mammography via the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data Systems (BI-RADS), which has achieved its goal of making communication of mammographic findings more accurate and decisive, largely eliminating ambiguity and vagueness in that subspecialty."

## What this establishes, and what it doesn't

Established (well-supported, cross-corroborated across primary-adjacent sources): general diagnostic radiology (outside mammography) has a **recognized, published, named** problem of ambiguous report language causing missed/delayed diagnosis and malpractice exposure, and BI-RADS is cited by radiology's own malpractice-liability literature as the domain's counter-example — proof that codified, controlled-vocabulary reporting closes the ambiguity gap where free-text reporting does not.

Not established here (would need the AJR primary, or a citing article, to verify): the specific taxonomy of *which* vague phrases Berlin catalogued, and whether he specifically discussed "not visualized"/"technically limited" as a phrase pair (as opposed to hedge words like "if clinically warranted"). Treat any claim about Berlin's exact catalogue of vague terms beyond the ones quoted above as unverified.

## Inference (ours) — transfer to arcviz

The malpractice literature's core finding — that a vague report can read as "nothing to worry about" and thereby suppress the follow-up action a genuine unknown requires — is structurally identical to arcviz's HP1 risk: a partially-disclosed ACDC graph that renders an unknown region in a visually unremarkable way will be read by a verifier as "checked, nothing there," with the same class of downstream harm (a security/trust decision made on the false premise that absence was confirmed rather than uninvestigated). This is our inference, not a claim in the radiology literature itself — the radiology sources never mention graph visualization or credentials.
