# Outside-model refutation of the disclosure-state matrix

Independent-lineage adversarial check on [docs/research/disclosure-matrix.md](../docs/research/disclosure-matrix.md), run alongside (not instead of) the concurrent Claude-authored KERI-doctrine panel. The premise: Claude instances share training and RLHF pressure, so a second Claude reviewing a first Claude's work is weak evidence; models from other labs, asked to refute rather than opine, are a genuine independent check. Agreement across seats is treated as weak evidence throughout; dissent, and dissent between seats, is the signal this run was built to surface.

I retried once, by policy the only acceptable form of retry here, using the corrected id directly: `panel -m openrouter/qwen/qwen3.8-max-0902`. That retry hit a different problem: `panel.conf`'s `timeout=1800` override is keyed to the alias name `qwen`, not to the underlying model id, so calling the raw id fell back to the wrapper's default 600s cap. ## (b) Objections, ranked by seriousness, adjudicated against the actual document

### 1. GAP — the Verification axis has no holder/presentation-binding check (VALID, high severity)

Raised independently by three seats: **ds** ("presentation provenance and session/replay binding... a credential copied from a public registry, replayed from a previous IPEX message, or retrieved from a store can pass every listed V check and render identically to a live, consented presentation"), **glm** ("The Verification axis is entirely Issuer-centric and completely omits Holder/Presentation Authenticity... a stolen ACDC presented by an imposter who does not control the holder AID would show a fully passing V vector"), and **kimi**, most fully: "the presentation act is unmodeled — the largest hole... No V check that the presenter controls the issuee AID (`a.i` appears only as an X-axis correlator, never as a check)... A stolen, perfectly valid, freshly-reconciled credential presented by an impostor is green on all five axes."

Checked against §B3: the nine listed checks are SAID self-consistency, schema conformance, bare signature validity, KEL anchoring, delegation chain, edge constraints, issuer bona fides, revocation reconciliation, and duplicity — every one of them about the artifact's issuance history, none about whether the entity currently presenting it controls the AID it names as holder. §B6's combinations (R0–R8) and §(f)'s open questions don't cover this either; open question 6 asks only whether the renderer receives IPEX *exchange state*, not whether a presentation-binding signature gets verified at all. **Adjudicated VALID.** This is the strongest finding of the run: three seats, independently, converged unprompted on the same structural absence, and it is a real one — nothing in the document's five axes would flag a credential replayed by someone who never controlled the holder key.

### 2. RULE 13 contradicts the document's own H1/H9 committed-absence claim (VALID, high severity)

Raised independently by **glm** ("Rule 13 contradicts H1... §B1 explicitly states that for an H1 block, 'absence of an optional field... is issuance-time absence, committed by the SAID.' ...refusing to render that absence as an exhaustive negative claim forces the renderer to lie about the cryptographic certainty defined in H1") and **kimi** ("RULE 13 contradicts B1-H9. H9-under-H1 is a *warranted* negative... RULE 13's test fails any empty state readable as 'does not exist' — forbidding the one negative claim the artifact cryptographically supports").

Checked against the text: RULE 13 reads "No negative claims, anywhere... nothing renders as exhaustive... *Test:* find an empty state readable as 'does not exist'." H1's row and H9's row both explicitly carve out a case where absence *is* a cryptographically committed, issuance-time fact — stronger than the generic "none in what was presented" scoping RULE 13 otherwise mandates. RULE 13's rule text and its literal test make no exception for this case; applied as written, the test would flag the one negative claim the matrix itself says is warranted. **Adjudicated VALID.** Two independent seats reaching the identical citation (B1-H1/H9 vs. Rule 13) by different routes is good corroboration, and the document text supports it on a direct read.

### 3. The orthogonality caveat overclaims: H forces V (and, less clearly, X) rather than merely correlating with them (VALID, but a precision issue rather than a structural flaw)

Some version of this was raised by **five of six seats** (ds, gemini, mistral, glm, kimi, and qwen before truncation) — the closest thing to a unanimous finding in the run. The common core: §(a)'s caveat states "no value on one axis implies a value on another," but H7's row, H6's row, and RULE 10 all say plainly that specific H-states force specific V-outcomes (checks depending on missing material become `unperformable`) — not merely a corner case, but written into the matrix and the rule that operationalizes it. ds and glm additionally point at an H→X coupling: whether a bare SAID is a "perfect correlator" (X's own definition) is exactly the H2/H3 distinction, a fact the document itself already flags in §B5 as "Inference (flagged)" — but never connects back to the orthogonality claim in §(a), so the caveat there remains unqualified by the document's own later admission.

**Adjudicated VALID, with a caveat of my own.** The axes still measure conceptually different things (what's in hand vs. what checks were run vs. when vs. correlation surface), and the *practical* consequence the matrix draws from this dependency (H-state gates which V-outcomes are even reachable) is already correctly reflected in the individual H-rows and in RULE 10. What's actually wrong is a single absolute sentence in §(a) ("no value on one axis implies a value on another") that the document's own later sections contradict. This is a wording fix, not a redesign — but five independent lineages catching the same overclaim in the same sentence is a real signal that the sentence should be softened (e.g., to "no value on one axis is redundant with another axis's *domain of applicability*" or similar), and the H2/H3-as-correlator nuance in §B5 should be cross-referenced from §(a) rather than left floating.

### 4. RULE 2's degraded-outcome amendment conflicts with RULE 2's own literal test (VALID, medium-high severity)

Raised by **ds**, **gemini**, and **glm** in similar form; **kimi** makes the sharper version, additionally tying it to R0: "R0 says store presence *warrants* check 1... and checks 6/7 transitively as of ingestion. RULE 2's new test fails any bare-store render showing other than 'unperformed' for checks 1–7 and 9. Both cannot hold... the amendment's own definition refutes it for check 1... 'unperformed' is defined as 'a check whose result did not survive,' but the amendment says the pass *did* survive, 'implicitly as "the object exists."'... the degradation default is backwards: a host that fails to declare its outcome space silently gets fail-glyphs stock keripy cannot support; degraded should be the default until instrumentation is declared."

Checked against the text: RULE 2's base test is "find any rendering where unperformed and unperformable share a glyph." Amendment (b) explicitly requires a host to be able to declare checks 6/7 degraded to `{pass, not-pass}` — which by construction collapses fail/unperformed/unperformable into one glyph for those checks, i.e. exactly what the base test is written to catch. **Adjudicated VALID.** The amendment is a deliberate, cited exception, not a hidden bug, but the rule's own test-language was never updated to admit it, so a compliant degraded-host renderer fails RULE 2's stated test as literally written. Kimi's R0 cross-check is the more valuable half of this finding: it shows the amendment's "unperformed is the default" framing is itself slightly off for checks 1–2, which R0 says are re-performable or already warranted at ingestion rather than merely "not recorded." Worth fixing at the rule-text level, not just noting.

### 5. Pair 10's "no separator exists in the substrate today" is disputed (UNVERIFIABLE — flagged for the keripy-verification.md owners)

Raised independently by **qwen** (before truncation: "even if `verifyChain` collapses the return value, the substrate still has a separable observable: whether the far-node object is present in the store") and, more fully, by **glm**: "The substrate is the store, not `verifyChain`'s return value. The near artifact names the far node's SAID... the host can query its own store for that SAID. Absent ⇒ not-yet-checkable. Present plus a `None` return ⇒ the failure branch, and the constraint itself... is re-evaluable by the host from the two artifacts in two lines, without 'instrumenting or re-implementing `verifyChain`'s internals.'"

I checked this against `keripy-verification.md` directly rather than taking either side's word for it. The evidence is genuinely mixed. Line 45 supports the document's claim as written: "`verifyChain`'s `None` return cannot be told apart by its caller... `processCredential` cannot correctly label a constraint violation as a permanent fail versus a transient absence." But line 42, describing check 7, says a missing far node "returns `None` and escrows *a cue asking for it* (`verifying.py:188-189`)" — the existence of a specifically-missing-far-node cue is suggestive of exactly the kind of independent signal glm and qwen are describing, but confirming it would require tracing whether that cue fires *only* in the absent case (which would validate the objection) or in both cases (which would validate the document as written). That tracing is keripy source-archaeology, not a job for an outside-model sweep to resolve on its own say-so. **Adjudicated UNVERIFIABLE by me; flagged as a concrete, sourced lead** (`verifying.py:188-189`'s cue mechanism) for whoever owns the keripy-verification.md audit to check before pair 10's "no separator exists" claim is finalized.

### 6. Pair 9 (AGID vs. SAID as strings) conflates an API-design footgun with a genuine artifact-level ambiguity (VALID)

Raised sharply by **glm**: "the separator — structural position — *is* the artifact. Any renderer capable of displaying the string has already parsed where it sits; an AGID is never visible without its role being visible. The pair describes a decontextualized pill component that receives a bare string — an API-design choice, not an epistemic state." **mistral** raises a weaker, partly-confused version of the same instinct (arguing the `s` field resolves it, which overstates what schema resolution buys you here).

Checked against the text: pair 9's own stated "Separator: structural/schema context, which the renderer must consult before choosing a pill treatment" is functionally different from every other pair in §(c) — the other nine require *out-of-band* information (an attestation, exchange state, a fetch log, a fresher TEL event) that genuinely isn't in the artifact. Structural position (which field a value sits in) is always available to anything that parsed the document. **Adjudicated VALID.** Pair 9 as framed belongs in the document (it's a real footgun for a component that receives a bare string stripped of context), but it isn't the same *kind* of indistinguishability as the other nine, and grouping it with them without that caveat overstates how deep the ambiguity goes.

### 7. RULE 2 and RULE 11 disagree on whether a single element may summarize multiple checks (VALID, single-seat)

Raised by **ds** alone: RULE 2's test is "find any single element whose presence summarizes more than one check," stated without a per-node scope qualifier; RULE 11 explicitly permits "a labeled arcviz-default EGF rule" whole-chain badge. A whole-chain badge is, literally, a single element summarizing many checks across many nodes — it satisfies RULE 11's test (it's labeled) while failing RULE 2's test as literally worded (it summarizes more than one check). **Adjudicated VALID** on a direct reading of both rules; the fix is to scope RULE 2's test explicitly to "per addressable unit" so it doesn't collide with RULE 11's narrower, labeled exception.

### 8. Algorithmic/cryptographic deprecation as a distinct failure mode (VALID but minor, single-seat, arguably out of current scope)

**gemini** alone: "Axis T covers event reconciliation and key rotation (staleness), but ignores algorithm rot. If a credential uses Ed25519 or Blake3, and a viewer's policy updates to deprecate those algorithms... the credential is mathematically intact but systematically rejected. This is a distinct failure mode from 'stale' and 'failed.'" This is a real category the document doesn't mention anywhere in T or V. It's a legitimate, narrow addition rather than a flaw in what's there — filed as a candidate for a future open question rather than a defect.

### Objections checked and found INVALID

- **Pair 2 (default vs. explicit edge operator) "miscategorized"** — raised by ds, gemini, and glm as if the document hadn't noticed the operator states are distinguishable in raw bytes. It has: pair 2's own text says "the syntactic difference is visible only in a raw-bytes view... here the renderer must suppress a visible difference rather than surface an invisible one." Three seats reproduced the same misreading; **kimi** alone caught it ("pair 2 is miscategorized... but the document says so itself"). INVALID as an unnoticed flaw.
- **Pair 5 separable via a visible `u` field (mistral)** — confuses the fully-compact case pair 5 addresses (only the outer SAID is visible, the block containing `u` is never disclosed) with the partially-disclosed sibling-leakage case in R3/the corpus citation mistral leans on. When a block is compact, `u` is not a separately visible field. INVALID.
- **Pair 4 resolved by the CESR stream (gemini)** — conflates H-level visibility (the far node is absent from *this* artifact — already granted, that's H7 itself) with C-level cause ambiguity (whether a route ever existed, or whether the viewer simply hasn't tried) — exactly the H/C layering the document is built to keep apart. INVALID.
- **RULE 1 vs. RULE 8 "phantom tenth state" (gemini)** — RULE 1 requires the nine canonical H-values to render pairwise-distinctly from each other; it does not forbid an additional "don't know which of two applies" meta-state. INVALID.
- **RULE 2 "unimplementable for deep delegation chains" (gemini)** — the document's own delegation-chain V-row already scopes a pass to "as far as the KELs in hand reach," and RULE 10 already requires reach be named in the rendering. Gemini's objection describes exactly the failure mode RULE 10 exists to prevent. INVALID.
- **RULE 2 vs. RULE 15 conflict on static output (mistral)** — asserted but not actually forced; a print rendering can label each check "unperformed, as of ⟨scope⟩, by ⟨tool⟩" individually, satisfying RULE 15's historicity requirement without collapsing RULE 2's per-check granularity. INVALID.
- **Missing "intent" axis (mistral)** — already substantially covered by C's `pending-graduated` cause and by open question 6 (whether the renderer receives IPEX exchange state); not elevated to axis status, but not an unacknowledged gap either. INVALID as a total miss, though the elevation-to-axis suggestion has minor organizational merit.
- **Missing "fulfillment" axis — does the credential satisfy the verifier's request (gemini)** — out of the document's own stated scope, which is "what state is this credential in," not "does it match a query." INVALID for this document; a legitimate concern for a different document.

## (c) Points of dissent between seats

The most informative disagreement in this run is not model-vs-document, it's **seat-vs-seat on the same input**: three separate labs (DeepSeek, Google/OpenAI-adjacent framing via gemini, and Zhipu) independently produced the *identical* wrong objection about pair 2 — quoting the document's own "visible only in a raw-bytes view" language back at it as if the document hadn't already drawn the conclusion that follows from it. Moonshot's kimi, given the same document, caught the self-correction the other three missed. This cuts against a simple "different lineages, therefore uncorrelated errors" assumption: when an error is textually seductive — a document quoting its own apparent tension and then resolving it in the next clause — models across labs can converge on the same misreading by pattern-matching the tension without reading past it, independent of shared training corpus. It is exactly the kind of correlated failure this whole exercise is designed to catch, just not in the direction expected: the correlation showed up as false-positive noise, not as a real bug several labs happened to notice together.

The GAPS section shows genuine, non-correlated disagreement. Three seats (ds, glm, kimi) converged on the presentation/holder-binding gap; gemini instead surfaced fulfillment-matching and algorithm deprecation, neither of which any other seat mentioned; mistral surfaced an intent axis, also unique. Four seats, three different gap-sets, only one of which (holder-binding) reached majority — that's a healthier sign of independent exploration than the pair-2 convergence above, and it's why holder-binding is graded the most serious finding: it's the one place three independently-exploring seats landed on the same target.

Pair 10 is a live three-way disagreement I could not close: the document says no separator exists in the substrate; qwen and glm say a store-presence check plus independent constraint re-evaluation would suffice; the document's own cited source (`keripy-verification.md`) contains one line (the check-7 "cue asking for it") that could support either side depending on facts about `verifying.py` I did not trace. This is flagged rather than adjudicated, deliberately.

## (d) Judgment

**Not a null result.** Two findings clear the bar for "something the Claude-authored corpus missed": the absent holder/presentation-binding check (§b.1) is a real, unflagged, high-consequence gap — nothing in the current five-axis matrix would distinguish a legitimately-presented credential from a stolen or replayed one, and the document's own open questions don't touch it. The RULE 13 vs. H1/H9 contradiction (§b.2) is a concrete, testable internal inconsistency independently found by two different labs from the document's own text, not from outside knowledge. Two further findings (RULE 2's amendment-vs-test mismatch, and RULE 2-vs-RULE 11 scope ambiguity) are smaller but real drafting fixes a careful line edit should catch before the rules ship as normative test criteria.

The near-unanimous orthogonality overclaim (§b.3) is real but should be read as a precision fix to one sentence in §(a), not a challenge to the five-axis architecture — the practical consequences of the H→V dependency it names are already correctly handled in the individual H-rows and in RULE 10. Pair 10 (§b.5) is a genuine open question the outside models raised that I could not close without keripy source-tracing beyond this run's scope, and it's handed off rather than guessed at.

Against that: roughly half of the raw objections produced by the six seats were wrong on inspection, most visibly the three-lab pile-up on pair 2, which is worth remembering the next time a sweep like this returns a confident-sounding objection — checking each one against the actual text, as this run did, is what separates four real findings from ten plausible-sounding ones.
