# Protocol Security & Verifier Realist Review: the disclosure-state matrix (arcviz Phase 2B)

**Date:** 2026-09-05  **Target(s):** `WebOfTrust/keripy` @ `upstream/main` `e2a1e35c` (2.1.0-dev0); `trustoverip/kswg-acdc-specification` @ `upstream/main` `f0bd097` and the vendored checkout `651df33e`; `kswg-dossier-specification` @ `7669358f` (consulted, nothing in lens)  **Effort:** deep  **Objective lens:** survivability

## Executive summary

The document is sound in its central move and unusually well-grounded for a design deliverable: I re-anchored twenty-odd spec citations and every keripy `file:line` it names, and the load-bearing ones hold exactly, including the two hardest (the `verifyChain` conflation at `verifying.py:418-420,471-476,478-479` and the witness TEL-replay endpoint at `indirecting.py:104,1191`). The findings below are not attacks on the axis decomposition, which I think is right. They are six places where a rendering rule or a matrix cell makes a claim about machine behavior or spec text that the live sources do not support — in three cases over-claiming, in two cases under-claiming, and in one case contradicting a normative MUST the same document cites two sections earlier.

The single biggest is F1: the closed decision that unknown edge operators render as **unevaluated, never invalid** contradicts ACDC `spec-body.md:1197-1201`, which the document itself cites in check 6. The spec's default-injection rule is not a fallback for missing operators only — it fires whenever `o` "is present but does not include any of the `I2I`, `NI2I` or `DI2I` Operators", so an unknown operator produces a *determinate* I2I/NI2I verdict, which may be a genuine fail. Rendering that as "unevaluated" suppresses a determinate negative, which is the mirror image of the naive lie the document exists to forbid.

## Steelman

The strongest version of this proposal, in KERI's terms: a renderer is a verifier's mouth, and every claim it makes is a claim the verifier is on the hook for. KERI's characteristic properties are all *scoped* — duplicity-evident relative to the watchers consulted, not-revoked relative to the events in hand, authorized relative to the anchor's sn — and a UI that flattens a scoped claim into an unscoped one has manufactured authority the protocol declined to create. That is the same failure the doctrine names when a witness is rendered as a voucher or a registry is consulted at verification time; the document is applying it one layer up, to pixels, and it gets the shape right. Three moves in particular are correct and non-obvious:

The refusal to make "Form" an axis is well-argued and correctly grounded. `spec-body.md:1775` ("All the Graduated Disclosure mechanisms MAY be used in combination") and `:1822` ("a signature on any variant MAY be used to verify the Issuer's commitment to any other variant… This cross-variant Issuer commitment verifiability is an essential property") both re-anchor exactly. One Issuer commitment, many views: an axis whose values co-occur and do not change verification is a vocabulary, and saying so is the kind of correction that saves a downstream API from encoding a category error.

The vector-not-scalar treatment of verification is exactly the doctrine's authenticity/veracity split made operational, and the four-valued outcome with *unperformable* as a first-class value is the right way to render "incomplete evidence is a normal state, not an error state." The corpus says the same thing about detection being observer-dependent (`background.md` §6.4-6.6, via bible 02 §9); a renderer that cannot say "I could not perform this check with what I was given" will lie by omission on every real presentation.

The keripy audit folded in on 2026-09-05 is the strongest section, and its central claim survives adversarial re-reading. `Verifier.verifyChain` really does return bare `None` for a genuine I2I constraint failure (`verifying.py:478-479`), for far-node-absent (`:418-420`), for an untargeted far node (`:471-472`) and for a missing issuee index (`:475-476`); `processCredential` really does route all four to one `MissingChainError` and one escrow table (`:187-191`); and `_processEscrow` really does drop a stale entry with `db.rem(said)` and a log line and no persisted marker (`:318-361`, confirmed by `MissingChainError(KeriError)` at `kering.py:1104` not being a `ValidationError` subclass, so the stale `ValidationError` falls to the generic handler). Reaching that conclusion required reading the code rather than the prose, which is the master reflex of this lens, and the document did it.

## Top findings

### F1: "Unknown operators render as unevaluated, never invalid" contradicts the spec's default-injection MUST

- **Severity:** HIGH **Confidence:** CONFIRMED **dedupe_key:** `unknown-edge-operator-default-injection-ignored` **Objective function:** survivability **Layer:** acdc **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §f open question 5 (marked *Closed*), and §B3 check 6 which cites the same clause. Re-anchored: ACDC `spec-body.md:1191-1195` (the normative unary operator table: `I2I`, `NI2I`, `DI2I`, `NOT` — and nothing else), `:1197-1201` (default injection), keripy `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:41` and `:431-441`.
- **Finding.** The spec's default-injection rule reads: "When the Operator, `o`, field is missing **or empty or is present but does not include any of the `I2I`, `NI2I` or `DI2I` Operators** then… the `I2I` Operator MUST be appended to the Operator, `o`, field's effective list value" (`:1197-1199`; `NI2I` for an untargeted far node at `:1201`). An unknown operator is therefore *not* an unevaluated edge. It is an edge evaluated under an injected default, with a determinate pass-or-fail answer. The reference verifier implements exactly this: `ops = [cand for cand in ops if cand in self.UnaryOps]` then `if not ops: op = 'I2I' if creder.iseaid is not None else 'NI2I'` (`verifying.py:432,435-440`), and its own class comment says the quiet part out loud — tokens outside `UnaryOps` are "dropped and silently defaulting" (`:38-41`). So the decision recorded as *Closed* asks the renderer to report `unperformable` for a check that both the spec and the reference verifier say MUST be performed.
- **Why it matters.** Take an ACDC whose edge carries `o: "I1I"` (one of the #1515 proposed operators, not in the spec table and not in keripy's `UnaryOps`), pointing at a targeted far node whose issuee is *not* this ACDC's issuer. Per `:1197-1199` the effective operator is `I2I`; the constraint fails; keripy returns `None` at `:478-479` and `processCredential` raises `MissingChainError`. The credential is invalid. arcviz, following §f-5, renders check 6 as `unevaluated` — a state its own Rule 2 defines as "a fact about the evidence," not a verdict. A viewer reads "not checked" where the correct reading is "checked and failed." That inverts the document's own thesis: it is the naive lie run backwards, hiding a determinate negative behind a scoping caveat.
  The `E1E` case that motivated the decision now cuts three ways rather than two, which is worth knowing before the decision is treated as settled. `E1E` appears **zero times** in `spec-body.md` at both `f0bd097` and the vendored `651df33e`, but it *is* in keripy's `UnaryOps` at `verifying.py:41` and is implemented at `:454-463` as a near-issuee/far-issuee equality that deliberately does not constrain the issuer. So for the same guardianship artifact: keripy passes it under `E1E`; a spec-conformant verifier drops the token, injects `I2I` per `:1197-1199`, and *fails* it (issuer ≠ issuee is precisely the SEDI shape); and arcviz renders it as unevaluated. Three implementations, three answers, and arcviz's is the only one that is not a verdict.
- **Recommendation.** Split the decision by which of two things the token is. For a token the *host's verifier* does not recognize, the honest render is the effective-operator verdict plus a visible annotation that the declared operator was not the one evaluated — a fifth outcome, "passed/failed under a substituted constraint," or an annotation on the existing pass/fail cell. Reserve `unperformable` for the case where no verdict was reachable at all. Then add a test: a fixture whose edge names a token outside `{I2I, NI2I, DI2I, NOT}` and whose issuer ≠ far issuee must render check 6 as a fail carrying the substitution, not as unevaluated.

### F2: Rule 8's H2/H3 distinction is derived from an input whose integrity the host interface never requires

- **Severity:** HIGH **Confidence:** CONFIRMED **dedupe_key:** `schema-resolution-integrity-unspecified` **Objective function:** survivability **Layer:** acdc **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §c indistinguishable-pair 5 ("Host interface: schema resolution") and rendering Rule 8. Re-anchored: ACDC `spec-body.md:194-200` (dynamic schema references MUST NOT be used; the schema-revocation and semantic-malleability attacks; "all Schemas MUST be static, i.e., Schemas MUST be SADs and therefore verifiable against their SAIDs"), `:226` (dereferencing `$schema` "would be an attack vector"); keripy `src/keri/core/scheming.py:47-48` and `:170-174`.
- **Finding.** The document is right that H2 versus H3 is read from the schema, and right that Rule 8's three treatments are the gravest thing on the H axis — an H2 rendered with the lock treatment asserts protection the spec says does not exist (`:160`, re-anchored exact). But the host interface it derives names only one hazard of schema resolution, correlation: "resolving over the network is itself a correlating fetch." The integrity hazard is not named anywhere, and it is the one the spec devotes three paragraphs to. A schema is the *sole* input distinguishing "contents recoverable/guessable" from "computationally hidden." Whoever controls schema resolution controls which of those two arcviz asserts.
- **Why it matters.** Concretely: an attacker (or merely a mis-wired host that resolves `$id` as a URL rather than as a SAID) supplies, for the committed schema SAID, a schema whose attribute sub-block reserves a `u`. The unit's actual committed block has no `u`, so it is H2 and rainbow-attackable from SAID plus schema. arcviz reads the substituted schema, classifies the unit H3, and applies the lock treatment — asserting privacy protection that does not exist, on an artifact whose contents are in fact discoverable. This is the exact naive lie Rule 8 exists to forbid, reached by the input Rule 8 depends on. It is the spec's own semantic-malleability attack (`:198`) aimed at the renderer rather than at validation logic. Stock keripy is safe here — `CacheResolver.add` refuses to cache a schema whose SAID does not match the requested key (`scheming.py:47-48`) and `Schemer.load` verifies the schema's own `$id` SAID (`:170-174`) — which is precisely why the requirement must be written into arcviz's *host interface* rather than assumed: a browser-side or KERIA-side host has no such gate by construction.
- **Recommendation.** State it as a precondition on the pair-5 host interface: a resolved schema is admissible input only if it verifies against the committed schema SAID, and a host that cannot demonstrate that MUST supply the third treatment ("hidden; protection unknown") rather than a resolved one. Add the negative test to Rule 8: a fixture whose supplied schema does not verify against the committed SAID and which renders anything but "protection unknown" fails.

### F3: R0's "merely retrieved from the substrate's store" names two stores with different warrants, and keripy's own CLI reads the weaker one

- **Severity:** HIGH **Confidence:** CONFIRMED **dedupe_key:** `store-presence-conflates-validated-and-escrowed` **Objective function:** survivability **Layer:** acdc **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §B6 R0, and Rule 2 amendment (a) ("the honest claim store presence supports is R0's"). Re-anchored: keripy `src/keri/vdr/eventing.py:2316` (`self.creds`), `:2336` (`self.saved`), `:2478-2489` (`logCred`); `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:262-307` (all three escrow helpers call `logCred`), `:363-389` (`saveCredential`), `:318-361` (`_processEscrow`); `src/keri/cli/commands/vc/export.py:95`; `src/keri/cli/commands/vc/import_.py:87`; `src/keri/cli/commands/vc/list.py:114-128`.
- **Finding.** R0 states one warrant set for one state. In stock keripy there are two credential stores with materially different warrants, and nothing about a retrieved credential says which one it came from. `saveCredential` pins `reger.saved` and the `issus`/`subjs`/`schms` indices only after schema validation and every edge check has passed (`verifying.py:363-389`). But `escrowMRE`, `escrowMCE` and `escrowMSE` each call `reger.logCred` first (`:274`, `:289`, `:306`), and `logCred` writes the credential into `reger.creds` unconditionally (`eventing.py:2478-2489`). So a credential that failed schema resolution, failed the far-node edge schema, or failed `verifyChain` outright is written into `reger.creds` — and when its escrow later goes stale, `_processEscrow` removes the *escrow* entry (`verifying.py:353`, `:359`) and never removes the credential. `reger.creds` therefore accumulates, permanently and unmarked, credentials that failed the exact checks R0 says store presence warrants.
- **Why it matters.** This is not hypothetical wiring. `vc export` enumerates `self.rgy.reger.creds.getTopItemIter()` (`export.py:95`) — it exports the unvalidated table, chains and TELs and all. `vc import` prints "Credential successfully imported." the moment `reger.creds.get(said)` is non-None (`import_.py:87`), which escrow satisfies before any check passes. `vc list`, in the same directory, reads `issus`/`subjs`/`schms` (`list.py:114-119`) — the validated indices. Two commands in one CLI disagree about what "the store" is. A host that wires arcviz to the `vc export` path (the obvious choice for a renderer: it is the only bulk-enumerating surface) hands it credentials for which R0's schema-conformance and edge-constraint warrants are simply false, and R0 licenses the renderer to assert them. Store-retrievability read as a verification scalar is the naive lie R0 itself names; here the document commits a weaker form of it by treating one warrant set as unconditional.
  SAID self-consistency does survive on both paths, since `reger.creds` is a `SerderSuber` over `SerderACDC` and `Serder.__init__` defaults `verify=True` (`serdering.py:539`, check at `:621-628`) — so that leg of R0 is fine.
- **Recommendation.** Make store-identity part of the host interface rather than an assumption: R0 should be two cells, or one cell parameterized by a host-declared "validated store / ingestion log" flag, with the flag defaulting to the *weaker* reading. Concretely, presence in a validated index (`saved`/`issus`/`subjs`/`schms`) carries R0's warrants; presence in a raw credential log carries only SAID self-consistency, with checks 2, 6 and 7 rendering unperformed rather than transitively passed. Add the fixture: a credential written by `logCred` from a failed `escrowMCE` that renders check 6 as anything but unperformed fails.

### F4: R0 under-credits the substrate on the one check that is KERI's signature property — anchor-relative validation

- **Severity:** MEDIUM **Confidence:** CONFIRMED **dedupe_key:** `store-presence-warrants-anchor-check` **Objective function:** survivability **Layer:** acdc **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §B6 R0 ("It does *not* warrant signature/anchoring depth…") and Rule 2 amendment (a) ("a bare-store fixture rendering anything but unperformed for checks 1–7 and 9 … fails"). Re-anchored: keripy `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:130` (`state = self.tevers[regk].vcState(vcid)`; `None` ⇒ raise), `src/keri/vdr/eventing.py:1144-1192` (`Tever.vcState` reads `reger.tels`), `:1208-1240` (`logEvent` writes `reger.tels`), `:1045` and `:1117` (`if not self.verifyAnchor(...): escrow; raise MissingAnchorError` — the gate `logEvent` sits behind), `:1299-1344` (`verifyAnchor` body).
- **Finding.** `processCredential` cannot proceed unless `Tever.vcState(vcid)` returns non-`None` (`verifying.py:130-134`). `vcState` computes from `reger.tels` (`eventing.py:1155-1160`), which only `logEvent` writes (`:1237`), and `logEvent` is reached only after `verifyAnchor` returns True on the issuance path (`:1045`) or the revocation path (`:1117`). And `verifyAnchor` is invariant H1 in code: `self.db.kels.getLast(keys=self.pre, on=seqner.sn)`, fetch that event, require `eserder.said == saider.qb64`, then require the seal triple to match the TEL event (`:1317-1344`). So presence in the validated store transitively warrants that this credential's TEL issuance event was anchored in the issuer's KEL **at exactly that sequence number** — check 4, performed and passed, with a real as-of scope (the anchoring event's sn) already in hand.
- **Why it matters.** Rule 2's test as written *fails* a renderer that correctly reports the one thing the substrate genuinely establishes, and the state it mandates instead erases the property that most distinguishes an ACDC from a signed envelope. The document's own check-3 naive lie is the "pyrrhic victory" of conflating valid-now with valid-then; check 4 is the resolution of that, and R0 currently tells the renderer to show it as unperformed. There is a second cost: `verifyAnchor` hands the seal's `sn` back through `vcState`'s `a=dict(s=seqner.sn, d=saider.qb64)` (`eventing.py:1190`), so the as-of scope Rule 3 demands for check 4 is already available from the substrate and would otherwise be discarded. (I read this as an under-claim, not a hole — under-claiming assurance fails safe — but it is a correctness defect in a document whose product *is* the warrant boundary, and Phase 2C would bake the wrong expected outputs into fixtures.)
- **Recommendation.** Move check 4 out of R0's not-warranted list into its warranted list, scoped: "the credential's TEL issuance event was anchored in the issuer's KEL as of the seal's sn," with the sn as the rendered as-of. Amend Rule 2's bare-store test to checks 1–3, 5, 7 and 9 (and 6 per F3's split). Leave the unperformed default for everything else; the point is only that one cell has a real, re-queryable answer, which makes it the second exception alongside revocation.

### F5: H6 puts the Issuer AID in the "knows" column when it is a Discloser claim with no issuer commitment behind it

- **Severity:** MEDIUM **Confidence:** CONFIRMED **dedupe_key:** `metadata-acdc-issuer-unattested` **Objective function:** survivability **Layer:** acdc **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §B1 H6 ("Knows: Issuer, Schema, Edges, Rules…"), rendering Rule 12. Re-anchored: ACDC `spec-body.md:166-168` (metadata ACDC definition; its `d` "will necessarily be different"), `:170` ("the potential Disclosee can verify the Issuer, the Schema, the provenance of the Edges…"), `:172` ("only cryptographic commitments from the Discloser are attached, not commitments from the Issuer"), `:174` ("The Disclosee is protected from a forged Discloser because, ultimately, verification of the disclosed ACDC will fail if the Discloser does not eventually provide verifiable Issuer commitments").
- **Finding.** The matrix's three-column discipline is "knows = the warranted claim." On a metadata ACDC the Issuer AID is not warranted by anything at render time. `:172` is unambiguous that no Issuer commitment is attached, and `:174` states the protection explicitly as *deferred* — the forged-Discloser case is caught "ultimately," when the real disclosure fails to produce Issuer commitments. The metadata ACDC is by construction the one artifact class where the issuer field is entirely Discloser-chosen and nothing in hand contradicts a lie about it. The document sees this — "the Issuer stands behind nothing yet" is in the same cell — but it books the consequence as an artifact-level naive lie ("looks issued") and leaves Issuer sitting in the knows column, which licenses a renderer to give the Issuer AID the same field-level treatment it uses on an H1 issuer that a KEL walk confirmed.
- **Why it matters.** The whole purpose of a metadata ACDC is to get the Disclosee to agree to the Rules *before* real disclosure (`:168`, `:170`). That agreement is the decision the render informs, and it is made on an unverified issuer identity. Mallory offers a metadata ACDC naming GLEIF's AID as Issuer, with a plausible schema and rules; arcviz renders the artifact-level "issuer-uncommitted" marker per Rule 12, and renders `Issuer: GLEIF` as a known field. The user agrees to terms they would have refused from an unknown party. Rule 12's marker is necessary and not sufficient: it scopes the *artifact* and leaves the *field* unscoped, and users read fields.
- **Recommendation.** Move Issuer (and, by the same argument, the Edges' far-node identities) from H6's knows column to "attested by the Discloser, unverified," and give Rule 12 a field-level clause: on an H6 artifact, every identifier rendered must carry the Discloser-asserted treatment, distinct from the treatment used for an identifier confirmed against a KEL. Test: an H6 fixture rendering the issuer AID identically to an H1 fixture's issuer AID fails.

### F6: The claimed {pass, not-pass} outcome space for checks 6 and 7 erases the one case where stock keripy does report unperformable

- **Severity:** MEDIUM **Confidence:** CONFIRMED **dedupe_key:** `edge-check-outcome-space-overbroad` **Objective function:** survivability **Layer:** acdc **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §c pair 10 ("**Separator: none exists in the substrate today**; a host could create one only by instrumenting or re-implementing `verifyChain`'s internals. Until then the honest outcome space for checks 6 and 7 is {pass, not-pass}") and Rule 2 amendment (b). Re-anchored: keripy `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:442-452` (`NOT` and `DI2I` raise `ValidationError`, with the comment "Deliberately not a MissingChainError: the chain is present and retrying cannot help, so escrowing would promise a retry that can never succeed"), `:179-186` (the caller re-raises `ValidationError`, distinct from the `state is None` path at `:187-191`), `src/keri/kering.py:1104` (`MissingChainError(KeriError)` — not a `ValidationError` subclass, so the two are type-disjoint at the caller).
- **Finding.** Pair 10 is right about the `None`-returning branches and I confirmed all four of them. It is over-broad about the *operator* dimension. For two of the five tokens keripy recognizes — `DI2I` and `NOT`, both of which are in the spec's normative table at `spec-body.md:1193,1195` — `verifyChain` does not return `None`. It raises a `ValidationError` naming the operator and the far node, deliberately, and the code comment states the reasoning in exactly the document's own vocabulary: the chain is present, retrying cannot help. That is `unperformable`, distinguished from `not-pass`, at the raise site, by exception type alone. No instrumentation of `verifyChain`'s internals is needed — a host wrapping `processCredential` in a two-arm `except` already has it.
- **Why it matters.** Rule 2 amendment (b) tells a host to declare checks 6 and 7 degraded to {pass, not-pass} on stock keripy. A host that does so loses the ability to render `unperformable` for the exact case the document elsewhere calls the normal one: a check the verifier cannot perform with the material and the code in hand. An ACDC with a `DI2I` edge would then render as "not-pass" — indistinguishable from a genuine constraint failure — when the truthful state is that this verifier does not implement the operator. That inverts §f-5's own instinct (unimplemented ≠ invalid) inside the rule that is supposed to encode it, and it makes a `DI2I` credential look broken rather than unevaluated.
- **Recommendation.** Narrow the degradation claim to its actual scope: for checks 6 and 7 against stock keripy the outcome space is {pass, not-pass} **for the `I2I`/`NI2I`/`E1E` and far-node-absent branches**, and {unperformable} for `DI2I` and `NOT`. State the separator that does exist — the exception type at the `processCredential` boundary — so the host interface asks for it rather than declaring it unavailable. Test: a `DI2I` fixture rendering check 6 as "not-pass" rather than "unperformable" fails.

## Additional patterns noted

**The citation audit holds where it matters.** Everything I sampled re-anchored: `:91`, `:160`, `:164`, `:166-168`, `:172`, `:661`, `:694`, `:712-720`, `:1112`, `:1165`, `:1197-1201`, `:1747-1775`, `:1777`, `:1822` all resolve to the quoted text in the vendored checkout. The "byte-identical to upstream `main` for every section cited" assertion also holds for the cited range specifically: the vendored `651df33e` and upstream `f0bd097` differ by 263 lines in `spec-body.md`, but every hunk before line 2807 is size-preserving and the 255-line deletion sits at line 4849 — well past everything cited. The assertion is true; it is true by luck of hunk placement rather than by construction, so it should be re-checked, not inherited, at the next revision.

**The B4 correction is right.** `QueryEnd` is at `src/keri/app/indirecting.py:1191`, `app.add_route("/query", queryEnd)` is at `:104` inside `setupWitness` (`:42`), and the `typ=tel` branch serves `self.reger.clonePreIter(pre=regk)` / `(pre=vcid)` at `:1269` and `:1274`. Genuine event stream, live on `upstream/main`. The scoping caveat — that it is a witness surface and not the KERIA agent surface — is the right caveat and is the one that generalizes: F3 above is the same observation applied to the credential store, and the two should probably be one principle in §c rather than two instances.

**A small internal inconsistency.** §B3 says "Eight of the nine checks in the reference implementation are verify-or-throw gates," with revocation the single exception. R0 then says duplicity is "never run at ingestion." Both cannot be true of check 9; there is no watcher anywhere on the `processCredential` path. R0 is the correct one. Not worth a finding, but the "eight of nine" phrasing will get quoted.

**Nothing in my lens on reintroduced authority**, which is the thing I most expected to find. The document consistently frames revocation as reconciliation with a horizon rather than a status query, frames the witness as a source of events rather than a voucher, frames the watcher scope explicitly in check 9's warrant, and explicitly notes in §c pair 4 that fetching is itself a disclosure to third parties. Rule 4's "appears without user action" is the one place I looked hard for a design pressure toward verification-time fetching, and it is not there: the rule renders the horizon, it does not go get a fresher one. That is the correct KERI posture and it is unusual to see it held consistently across a whole document.
