## Executive summary

The matrix is sound in my lens as a decomposition, and its citation discipline holds up: I re-anchored every load-bearing ACDC-spec line the document cites (`:91`, `:160`, `:164`, `:166-168`, `:172`, `:661`, `:694`, `:712-720`, `:1112`, `:1165`, `:1197-1201`, `:1747-1780`, `:1822`) and each is live and quoted accurately. My findings are all in one place: the **edge-operator surface**, where three of the fifteen rules are not decidable by two independent implementers from the material the document says a renderer has, and where the document's own §f-5 closure is contradicted by the reference verifier.

The single biggest finding is Rule 9 together with §c pair 2. "Default and explicit `I2I` render identically" is not a spec fact; it is conditionally true. The spec's default-injection rule (`spec-body.md:1197-1201`) resolves an omitted `o` to `I2I` **iff the far node is targeted** and to `NI2I` otherwise — and the effective operator is therefore a function of a far node that the document's own H7 / R1 states say is routinely absent. A renderer that obeys Rule 9 in the state the document calls normal asserts a constraint that may never have been checked. That is the silent-mis-frame shape, not a cosmetic one.

Nothing else in the fifteen rules trips my lens. The H/C/V/T/X decomposition is orthogonal in the sense the author claims, "Form is not an axis" is well argued from `:1775` and `:1822`, and the four-valued V outcome plus the host-declarable degradation in Rule 2b is a conformance-testable design.

## Steelman

The document is doing what a spec for a renderer has to do before anyone writes rendering code: it separates what an artifact *is* from what a viewer *knows*, and it refuses to let the second masquerade as the first. Reading in KERI's terms, that is exactly the discipline doctrine claim 12 demands — authenticity is mechanizable, veracity is not, and a green checkmark is not a truth oracle — pushed down to per-unit granularity. The V axis as a vector rather than a scalar is the doctrine's own posture applied to UI: KERI is duplicity-*evident*, so "no duplicity detected by the watchers consulted" is the only claim a renderer may make (doctrine claim 3), and a witness "simply stores and serves events" (doctrine shibboleth), so a witness count is not endorsement. The refusal to name a cause for a bare digest is the correct reading of `:160`/`:164`: the format commits to content, not to reasons. And the R0 amendment — that a credential retrieved from the store warrants only what ingestion actually checked, and that `processCredential` deliberately keeps revoked credentials (`verifying.py:142-145`) — is precisely the doctrine-claim-9 move of verifying against the code rather than the prose. I would adopt fourteen of the fifteen rules as written.

## Top findings

### F1: Rule 9 and §c pair 2 assert a semantic identity that holds only when the far node is in hand

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `edge-operator-default-inference-underspecified`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §d rule 9 ("The badge shows the *effective* operator per the default-injection rule") and §c pair 2 ("Separator: none needed; the states are semantically identical"). Re-anchored: `kswg-acdc-specification/spec/spec-body.md:1197-1201`; `keripy/src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:436-440` and `:418-420`.
- **Finding.** The spec's default-injection rule is conditional on the far node, not on the near artifact: "If the node pointed to by the Edge is a targeted ACDC, i.e., has an Issuee, then the `I2I` Operator MUST be appended… If the node pointed to by the Edge block is an Untargeted ACDC… then the `NI2I` Operator MUST be appended" (`spec-body.md:1199,1201`). keripy realizes exactly that: `op = 'I2I' if creder.iseaid is not None else 'NI2I'` (`verifying.py:440`), where `creder` is the *far* credential fetched at `:422`. So an omitted `o` and an explicit `o: I2I` are semantically identical only on the branch where the far node is targeted. Where the far node is untargeted they are opposites — omitted yields `NI2I`, which by the same spec table "removes or nullifies any requirement expressed by the `I2I` Operator", while explicit `I2I` yields a constraint that cannot be satisfied (keripy returns `None` at `verifying.py:471-472` when `farIssuee is None`). Worse for a renderer: when the far node is absent — H7, which the document itself calls the *normal* case in R1 — the effective operator is not computable at all. keripy does not even reach the default rule: `said = self.reger.saved.get(keys=nodeSaid); if said is None: return None` (`verifying.py:418-420`).
- **Why it matters.** Two implementers reading Rule 9 build different renderers from the same vLEI capture. Implementer A resolves "effective operator" from the near artifact plus the schema and paints `I2I` on the dangling edge; implementer B holds that the operator is undetermined and paints it unresolved. A's output tells the viewer a same-holder binding was established when in fact no constraint was evaluated — the exact class of lie the matrix exists to forbid, produced by a rule inside the matrix. The document compounds this by listing pair 2 as the one pair whose resolution is to *suppress* a visible difference; suppression is safe only under a precondition the document does not state.
- **Recommendation.** Make Rule 9 conditional and state the precondition: suppress the syntactic `o`/no-`o` difference only when the far node is in hand and its targetedness is established; otherwise render the operator as undetermined and mark check 6 unperformable with the far SAID named, which Rule 10 already provides for. Restate pair 2 as "semantically identical *when the far node is targeted*" and add the untargeted branch, where the two are not identical, as a separate pair whose separator is the far node itself.
- **Bucket:** deployment-gap (the divergence is realized in the reference verifier's control flow, not merely inferable from prose). Handoff: SEC under the same key if the "no constraint was evaluated but the chain passed" outcome is judged an enforcement finding.

### F2: the V axis has no cell for an unrecognized operator, and §f-5's closure is contradicted by the verifier

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `unknown-edge-operator-silent-downgrade`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §f open question 5 ("*Closed:* unknown operators render as **unevaluated, never invalid** — the `unperformable` outcome") and §B3 check 6. Re-anchored: `keripy/src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:41`, `:429-431`, `:436-440`, `:446-452`.
- **Finding.** Stock keripy today (`upstream/main`, byte-identical to the working tree for this file) splits unknown-operator handling three ways, and none of the three is "unevaluated":
  1. **Genuinely unrecognized tokens are dropped**, with the comment saying so — "Tokens this verifier does not recognize are skipped" — implemented as `ops = [cand for cand in ops if cand in self.UnaryOps]` (`:430-431`). If the drop empties the list, `if not ops:` falls through to the default rule at `:436-440`. An edge carrying `o: "X9X"` against an untargeted far node therefore resolves to `NI2I`, skips the constraint block at `:465`, and `verifyChain` returns a TEL state — a **pass**. The declared operator was not evaluated *and the check did not report that*; a weaker constraint was silently substituted.
  2. **Recognized-but-unimplemented tokens fail the whole credential.** `NOT` and `DI2I` raise `ValidationError` (`:446-452`), re-raised by `processCredential` with the near SAID attached at `:180-186`. That is neither pass, fail, unperformed, nor unperformable in the document's four-valued space — it aborts ingestion, so the credential never reaches R0 and there is nothing to render.
  3. **`E1E` is no longer unknown.** It is in `UnaryOps` (`:41`) and enforced at `:454-464`, while appearing zero times in `spec/spec-body.md` (the normative operator table at `:1190-1195` lists only `I2I`, `NI2I`, `DI2I`, `NOT`). So on a keripy host, `E1E` renders "unevaluated" under §f-5 while the substrate actually enforced it — under-reporting a check that ran, the mirror of case 1's over-reporting.
- **Why it matters.** Case 1 is the silent-downgrade shape doctrine claim 9 and bible 04 §5c-5d treat as the worst failure mode: a wrong input does not throw, it mis-frames. The renderer inherits it. The matrix's whole value proposition is that it enumerates what a viewer can be lied to about; an operator token the verifier silently replaced is a lie with no cell. Note also that this is a *re-anchoring correction* to the doctrine itself — the doctrine's shibboleth row cites `verifying.py` ≈L354's `op = 'I2I' if 'i' in creder.attrib else 'NI2I'` coercion, which no longer exists in that form; the coercion now runs on far-node targetedness via `.iseaid` and skips unknown tokens explicitly.
- **Recommendation.** Add a fifth V outcome, or an H-independent annotation on check 6: **substituted** — "the declared operator was not recognized by the verifying host; a different operator was applied; here is which." Re-open §f-5 with three sub-answers (unrecognized-and-substituted, recognized-but-unimplemented-and-rejected, implemented-but-not-in-the-spec) rather than one. State the host-interface requirement that follows: a host must be able to report the operator it *applied* alongside the operator the artifact *declared*, because on stock keripy those differ silently.
- **Bucket:** deployment-gap. Handoff: SEC (`unknown-edge-operator-silent-downgrade`) for whether the substitution is itself an authority downgrade; SKP if the answer is that arcviz should refuse to render such an edge at all.

### F3: Rule 8's H2-vs-H3 decision procedure is undecidable for the block classes where the spec makes `u` optional

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `optional-uuid-blinding-state-undecidable`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §B1 H2/H3 ("bare SAID for a block whose schema reserves no `u`" / "committed block carries high-entropy `u` not disclosed"), §d rule 8, §c pair 5, R4. Re-anchored: `spec-body.md:91`, `:1093`, `:1162`, `:1275`, `:1311`; the worked `oneOf` at `:346-400`.
- **Finding.** Rule 8 assumes the H2/H3 split is a two-valued function of the resolved schema. It is not, because the spec makes `u` *permissive*, not schema-determined: "A UUID, `u` field, MAY optionally appear in any block (field map) at any level of an ACDC" (`:91` — a line the document itself cites, for the uniform `d`+`u` mechanism, without noticing the "MAY optionally"). For Edge blocks, Edge-groups, Rule blocks and Rule-groups the spec says so normatively and repeatedly: "The UUID, `u` field is optional, but when it appears, it MUST appear as the second field in the Edge block" (`:1162`; identically at `:1093`, `:1275`, `:1311`). A conformant schema for such a block may therefore list `u` under `properties` without listing it under `required` — the worked attribute example at `:355-360` happens to require it, but nothing obliges an edge schema to. Against such a schema, one compact edge in hand, the artifact is consistent with both H2 and H3 and the schema does not decide: whether a `u` was present at issuance is exactly what the compaction hides.
- **Why it matters.** Rule 8's three-fixture conformance test ("three fixtures, fewer than three distinct treatments fails") is decidable only for required-`u` and forbidden-`u` schemas. For an optional-`u` schema two implementers pick opposite defaults and both can claim conformance: A treats optional-as-absent and paints "contents recoverable/guessable" (over-warning, and wrong whenever the issuer did include `u`); B treats optional-as-present and paints the lock — which is the naive lie the document itself calls "the gravest on this axis," reintroduced by the rule meant to prevent it. The document's own third treatment, "hidden; protection unknown," is reserved by pair 5 for a *different* condition (schema unresolvable), so a renderer following the text has no sanctioned state for this one. This bites hardest on H8 (Compact Private Edge, `:1165`), which the document already tiers as spec-only and untested.
- **Recommendation.** Split the schema-reservation input three ways — `u` required, `u` forbidden, `u` optional — and route both "optional" and "schema unresolvable" to the protection-unknown treatment while keeping them distinguishable in the detail view, since their host-interface remedies differ (the second is fixed by schema resolution; the first cannot be fixed by any fetch). Update Rule 8's test to four fixtures. Add "protection undecidable from a permissive schema" to the §c indistinguishable-states list; it belongs there and is currently missing.
- **Bucket:** proof-scope (the document's decision procedure is narrower than the spec's permission surface).

### F4: pair 3's separator, and the §e tiering that supports it, rest on `dp`, which exists in no published spec and in no implementation

- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `disclosure-path-reference-unresolvable`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance
- **Location:** proposal §c pair 3 ("Separator: IPEX position and the offer's disclosure paths (`dp`)") and §e ("Worked-example-attested (keripy tests…): IPEX graduated exchange and `dp` grammar").
- **Finding.** `dp` occurs zero times in `kswg-acdc-specification/spec/` and zero times anywhere under `keripy/src/keri/` or `keripy/tests/`. Its only presence in the working environment is an untracked PR-authoring note in the spec repo, `pr-1549-dp-disclosure-paths.md`, which is explicit that the construct is unmerged and *stacked on another unmerged PR* ("Stacked on #202 — merge that first"), that it implements a design from keripy **discussion** #1549, and that the gap it closes is precisely that the construct "was [not] defined anywhere in the spec, in keripy, or in the archived IPEX spec." Under the tier discipline bible 08 §0 imposes, a keripy discussion is `[P]`, not `[K]` and certainly not a worked example. The §e claim that the `dp` grammar is worked-example-attested is therefore wrong by two tiers, and pair 3's separator is an unresolvable "as defined by X": there is nothing a conformance vector can pin.
- **Why it matters.** §c is billed as the derivation of arcviz's host interface. An interface item whose payload format is an unmerged construct on a stacked branch will change shape before it lands, and the document's tiering tells a Phase 3/4 reader it is settled. This is a small error with a downstream cost: pair 3 is one of only two pairs whose separator is claimed to be fully available, and it is the one that gates R5's metadata-offer rendering.
- **Recommendation.** Retier `dp` to spec-only-and-in-flight, cite the discussion and the PR by number rather than the token, and restate pair 3's host interface as "negotiation context, shape TBD pending ACDC v1.1" — which is what §f-6 already says and §c contradicts. Keep the IPEX-position half of the separator, which is grounded in `spec-body.md:1810-1818`.
- **Bucket:** proof-scope. Handoff: GOV under the same key for who arbitrates the construct's landing.

## Additional patterns noted

Two things I checked and will not raise as findings, recorded so a later reader does not re-derive them.

The "Form is not an axis" argument survives my orthogonality test. `:1775` ("All the Graduated Disclosure mechanisms MAY be used in combination") and `:1822` ("a signature on any variant MAY be used to verify the Issuer's commitment to any other variant… This cross-variant Issuer commitment verifiability is an essential property") are both live and both say what the document says they say, and a set of freely co-occurring values is a vocabulary rather than an axis. That is a correct spec reading, not a preference.

The `a`/`A` leakage asymmetry (`:1777`, and the label-blinding at `:694` with the order-decorrelation at `:696`) is quoted accurately; the document attributes the "prevents inference based on location" sentence to `:694` where it is at `:696`, which is an elision across a line boundary inside one paragraph and not worth a finding.

## Residual unknowns

I could not close whether the `A`-aggregate element case has the same optional-`u` hole as F3 describes for edges and rules. The blinded-attribute-block prose at `:700` presumes `u` is present rather than stating it MUST be, and the worked `anyOf`/`oneOf` schema at `:757-800` requires it in the detail branch, so H4 is probably safe — but I did not find a normative MUST for `u` in an aggregate element, and if there is none, F3's remedy should extend to H4 as well.

I did not evaluate whether the R0 store-presence guarantees hold on KERIA/signify-ts as opposed to keripy. The document scopes them to keripy and flags the surface dependence in §B4, which is the right shape; confirming parity across the four implementations is a CSR/GOV question, not mine.
