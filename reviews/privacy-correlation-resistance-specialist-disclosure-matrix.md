# Privacy & Correlation-Resistance Review: the disclosure-state matrix (arcviz Phase 2B)

**Date:** 2026-09-05  **Target(s):** `kswg-acdc-specification` @`651df33e`, `keripy` @`bccea7860`, `kswg-dossier-specification`  **Effort:** medium  **Objective lens:** survivability
**Sources used:** keri-doctrine, review-house-style, orchestrating-reviews, bible 05 §4/§7-9, 06 §3-4, 07 §10, 10 §3/§8; live re-anchors in `spec/spec-body.md` and `src/keri/app/indirecting.py`

## Executive summary

The matrix is the best-aimed privacy artifact I have reviewed in this panel: it gets the H2/H3 distinction right (compact is not private), it refuses to let selective disclosure be rendered as achieved privacy, and it correctly locates the AGID as not-a-SAID. It is sound in my lens as far as it goes. What it does not do is turn its lens on **arcviz itself**. Every axis measures what *the viewer* knows about *someone else's* disclosure decisions; nothing in the fifteen rules constrains what arcviz does as a party in the disclosure chain — it never renders the Chain-Link Confidentiality terms that bind its own reader, never bounds its render-time network fetches, and treats cross-presentation correlation as a UI affordance to depict honestly rather than an act it might decline. Alongside that, one substantive cell is understated: expanding an `a` section forces disclosure of the Issuee AID, the single strongest correlator in the artifact, and the matrix records only label leakage.

## Steelman

The strongest version of this document, in KERI's terms: correlation-resistance is a three-stratum stack (bible `06` §4), and the technical stratum's work is undone at the last inch if the surface that displays a disclosure lies about it. The spec spends thousands of words making a compact block indistinguishable from a private one *only when `u` is present* (`spec-body.md:160` vs `:164`), and blinds `A`-element labels and positions on purpose (`:694`) — and every shipped wallet then collapses the whole thing into one green check. The matrix's central move is to refuse the scalar: `H` is per-unit, `V` is a vector of independently-scoped checks, `T` scopes every `V` claim, and `X` carries the residual correlators the disclosure machinery cannot remove. That decomposition is right, and dropping "Form" as an axis is right for exactly the reason given — `:1775` says the mechanisms combine freely and `:1822` says one Issuer commitment covers every variant, so the seven variants are coordinates, not states.

The privacy-specific wins are real and re-anchor cleanly. H2's "gravest naive lie" is the doctrine's own §10.2 anti-pattern stated as a rendering rule, grounded in the correct sentence (`:160`, "SHOULD be considered a public (non-confidential) ACDC"). Rule 6's `a`/`A` asymmetry is `:1777` correctly read. H4's ban on schema-derived candidate labels and on positional alignment is `:694` correctly read, and it is the kind of thing a renderer gets wrong by default. B5's refusal to call a bulk instance "anonymous," and its naming of the registry SAID and aggregate `B` as surviving join keys, matches `:2773` precisely. This is a document written by someone who read the disclosure sections rather than the summaries of them.

## Top findings

### F1: Expanding any `a` sub-block force-discloses the Issuee AID; the matrix records only label leakage

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `attribute-partial-disclosure-forces-issuee-aid`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc  **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §B1 leakage-asymmetry note; §B6 R3; §d Rule 6; §f open question 7. Re-anchored: `spec-body.md:264-270` (Attribute-section reserved labels `d`,`u`,`i`,`rd`,`dt`), `:344`, `:1777`.
- **Finding:** The matrix draws the `a`/`A` asymmetry entirely in terms of **labels** — "label known, value hidden" for hidden siblings — citing `:1777`, which says a partially disclosed block "exposes, at the very least, the labels of other fields in its enclosing block." The "at the very least" is doing work the document does not collect. The enclosing block of any `a` sub-block is the attribute section itself, whose reserved fields are not ordinary siblings: `i` is the Issuee AID and `dt` the issuance datetime, and both appear with **values**, not labels, in any uncompacted `a`. The spec is explicit about why this matters: "Because the Issuee AID is nested in the attribute block as that block's top-level, Issuee, `i`, field, a presentation exchange (disclosure) could be initiated on behalf of a different AID... and then only correlated to the Issuee AID after the Disclosee has agreed to the Chain-Link Confidentiality provisions" (`:344`). The Issuee AID's disclosure is the CLC-gated event of the whole exchange. The document names `a.i` once, in B5, as "pseudonymous but linkable" — as a static property of the vLEI leaf — and never connects it to H: nothing in the H-axis, in R3, or in Rule 6 says that opening one `a` field compulsorily moves `a.i` and `a.dt` to H1. §f question 7 parks the pre-presentation warning as being about "`a`-block label leakage," which is the weaker half of the problem.
- **Why it matters:** Two verifiers who each receive a different single `a` field from the same credential can join on `a.i` with an equality test — the perfect-correlator case the matrix's own X axis defines. A holder deciding what to disclose, looking at an arcviz render built to these rules, sees "one field shown, five hidden, their labels leaked" and does not see that the strongest identifier in the credential went out with the first field and cannot be withheld. That is precisely the state the spec's `a`-vs-`A` design exists to let a schema author avoid ("an aggregate can disclose one element with no issuee," bible `05` §4). A matrix whose stated job is "what a viewer would be lied to about" understates the largest correlation event in the attributive path.
- **Recommendation:** Add the attribute section's reserved fields as a named H consequence: disclosing any `a` sub-block moves `a.d`, `a.u` (if disclosed), `a.i` and `a.dt` to H1 as a *forced* transition, not an independent one. State it as an explicit exception to the axis's per-unit independence caveat, extend Rule 6 with a test ("an R3 fixture that renders `a.i` with the same treatment as a voluntarily disclosed field fails"), and widen §f question 7 to Issuee-AID disclosure rather than label leakage.

### F2: The rules section's terms bind the render's reader, and no rendering rule carries them

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `clc-terms-not-carried-to-downstream-disclosee`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** governance  **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §d Rules 1-15 (the `r` section appears only as an H addressable unit); §d Rule 15; §e (CLC listed only as a fixture-coverage item). Re-anchored: `spec-body.md:1790`, `:256`, `:1783-1792`.
- **Finding:** Chain-Link Confidentiality is normative and it is normative about *propagation*: "Chain-link confidentiality... chains together a sequence of Disclosees. Each Disclosee in the sequence, in turn, is the Discloser to the next Disclosee. The terms-of-use of the original disclosure as applied to the original Disclosee MUST be applied by each subsequent Discloser to each subsequent Disclosee" (`:1790`). The person reading an arcviz render is a Disclosee. Rule 15 then *designs the re-disclosure act* — print, screenshot, export — and specifies that the export carry "what a re-verifier needs," which is cryptographic material, and says nothing about the terms under which the data may be held or passed on. The matrix decomposes `r` into addressable units for the H axis and stops; there is no rule that the terms of use are rendered at all, no "naive lie" cell for displaying encumbered data as unencumbered, and no requirement that an export carry the `r`-section SAID and text with it. §e mentions CLC only to note that keripy has an example of it.
- **Why it matters:** The governance stratum is the only one that reaches a second-party Verifier at all — cryptography does not, by the spec's own admission (`:2911`, and bible `06` §4). Its entire enforcement mechanism is that the recipient knew and accepted the terms. A renderer that displays SEDI or vLEI attributes with no visible `noSurveillance` / `chainLinkConfidentiality` obligation, and then exports them into a file with no terms attached, is the point at which the chain silently breaks — and it breaks invisibly, because the export still verifies. This is the layer-composition seam the doctrine tells reviewers to test, not a GDPR import: the duty is contractual, the artifact carries it, and the rendering drops it.
- **Recommendation:** Add a sixteenth rule: any unit whose enclosing ACDC carries a non-empty `r` section renders with a persistent, non-dismissable indication that terms of use attach, with the terms reachable and the `r` SAID shown; and Rule 15's static output MUST carry the rules section (or its SAID plus a resolution hint) alongside the re-verification material. Add a "terms attached but not shown" naive-lie cell to §B. This also gives §c pair 1 (attested erasure) a natural home, since the dossier Annotation Edge mechanism lives in the same governance stratum.

### F3: Render-time resolution is a phone-home, and the surface comparison is capability-only

- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `render-time-fetch-is-point-of-validation-leak`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** deployment  **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §B4 correction (2026-09-05); §c pairs 4, 5, 6. Re-anchored: `spec-body.md:1693`; `keripy src/keri/app/indirecting.py:1191` (`QueryEnd`), `:1260-1274` (the `typ=tel` branch serving `reger.clonePreIter`), `:103-104` (mounted at `/query` in `setupWitness`).
- **Finding:** The keripy claim checks out exactly as stated — the witness does serve TEL event history at `GET /query?typ=tel`. But the document evaluates the two candidate surfaces (witness vs KERIA agent) on **capability alone**: "whether the Time axis is servable depends on which surface the host talks to." A witness is a controller-designated component operating on the Issuer/controller side. The spec puts a Validator's point-of-validation query at a **verifier-controlled Observer** precisely so the Issuer side cannot see it: "A validator queries its Observer, not the Registrar, at a point of validation... This protects against forced validator-to-issuer correlation of ACDC usage, i.e., no forced phone home validation" (`:1693`). A renderer that reaches the witness when a card is opened re-creates the OCSP leak the split exists to delete, and it does so at the worst possible granularity, since a render is one-to-one with a human looking at a credential. The same applies, more weakly, to §c pair 5 (schema resolution) and pair 4 (far-node fetch): the document flags both as "itself a correlating fetch" in prose, which is right, but neither hazard survives into §d, and §d is the only part with fixtures and failure tests behind it. The Observer/Registrar split is also, per bible `05` §9, architecturally committed and operationally unbuilt — so "fetch from the witness" will be the path of least resistance unless a rule forbids it.
- **Why it matters:** Two verifiers using arcviz against the same witness hand the Issuer's infrastructure a usage timeline neither of them intended to disclose; the honesty the matrix buys in the render is paid for out of the holder's correlation budget. Worse, this leak is invisible in the rendering the rules produce: nothing on the card says "displaying this told the issuer's witness you were looking at it."
- **Recommendation:** State a rule that arcviz performs **no network resolution as a side effect of rendering** — all material arrives from the host, and an unresolved unit renders as H7/unperformable rather than triggering a fetch. Where the host does fetch, §c pair 6's interface should name the surface class (verifier-controlled Observer vs issuer-side Registrar/witness) as part of the freshness input, so a render can scope its claim to a horizon whose acquisition cost is known. Add the surface's correlation cost to §B4 alongside its capability.

### F4: Cross-presentation correlation is treated as a depiction problem, never as an act arcviz might decline

- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** LIKELY  **dedupe_key:** `renderer-is-itself-a-correlation-surface`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** deployment  **Bucket:** deployment-gap
- **Location:** proposal §d Rules 14 and 15; §B5. Ground: bible `06` §4 (deployment stratum: per-facet AIDs, un-logged one-time presentations); bible `10` §3 ("Makes the presentation less ephemeral").
- **Finding:** The default adversary in this lens is two verifiers who compare notes. Rule 14 makes comparing notes a feature of one program: separately-loaded presentations may be joined, provided the join is explicit, distinctly styled, and **logged**. Rule 15 makes a durable artifact of every render. Both rules are about honest depiction, and on that axis they are well drawn. Neither asks the prior question the deployment stratum asks — whether arcviz should hold two presentations in a joinable state at all, and whether the join log and the export are themselves a correlation store with a lifetime nobody has bounded. The corpus treats un-logged, ephemeral presentation as one of the three legs correlation-resistance stands on, and treats added durability as a cost paid deliberately (bible `10` §3). arcviz is the viewer-side deployment stratum; the matrix's five axes describe what the viewer knows, and never what the viewer accumulates.
- **Why it matters:** A holder's technical protections can be intact — blinded blocks, bulk instances, per-facet AIDs — and be defeated by a viewer that quietly retains three presentations and offers to join them, or by an exported PNG carrying `a.i` into a shared drive. The failure is silent and its evidence lives only on the verifier's disk. I mark this LIKELY rather than CONFIRMED because it is an omission argument: nothing in the document is wrong here, and a reviewer could reasonably answer that retention is a Phase 3/4 host concern. My position is that it is not, because Rules 14 and 15 are the rules that *create* the artifact, and a rule that mandates an export while leaving its lifetime to a later phase has already made the decision.
- **Recommendation:** Add retention and join scope to the §c host interface as first-class inputs: how long a loaded presentation remains joinable, whether a join log persists across sessions, and whether export is permitted at all for a given credential (a natural place for an EGF or `r`-section term to say no — which links this to F2). At minimum, state as a non-goal that arcviz does not persist presentations across sessions by default, so the absence is a decision rather than an omission.

## Additional patterns noted

- **The `B` and registry-SAID join keys in B5 are correctly identified** — `:2773` names both the REGID from `rd` and the aggregate `B` as points of correlation for a bulk-issued set, disclosable only behind contractual protection. The document's "shared registry SAID and `B` remain join keys in the simplest form" is exact. The three topologies it references (basic, independent-AID `:2891`, independent-registry `:2899`) also line up.
- **Layer diagnosis, offered because the panel's synthesis will want it:** none of the four findings faults KERI-core. F1 is a disclosure-mechanism (technical stratum) reading gap; F2 is governance-stratum propagation; F3 and F4 are deployment-stratum. The matrix's own layer discipline is good — it never asks KERI-core for unlinkability, and B5's framing of correlators as "an equality test, not an inference" is the right register.
- **H6 / Rule 12 are right but interact with Rule 15.** The metadata ACDC exists so that "only cryptographic commitments from the Discloser are attached, not commitments from the Issuer" (`:172`), keeping anything durable out of the Disclosee's hands until the Rules are accepted. Rule 15 would export a pre-agreement offer as a record. That is the same underlying issue as F4 and is keyed there rather than restated.
