**Date:** 2026-09-05  **Target(s):** `/home/daniel/code/wot/keripy` @`bccea786` (branch `feat-indep-registry-bulk-issuance`), `/home/daniel/code/me/kswg-acdc-specification` @`651df33e` (branch `fix-worked-examples-schema-1520`; diffed against `upstream/main` @`f0bd097`)  **Effort:** deep  **Objective lens:** survivability

## Executive summary

The decomposition is the right shape and the "Form is not an axis" argument is correct, for two of the three reasons the author gives. What does not hold up is the treatment of the edge layer. The document's own subject — that a rendering must never assert a relation the material does not determine — is violated by Rule 9, which instructs the renderer to display the *effective* edge operator computed by the spec's default-injection rule; that rule's output is a function of the far node's disclosure state, so the same edge yields I2I or NI2I depending on how much of a *different* artifact was disclosed, and is undeterminable at all when the far node is at H7 or H8. Two structural problems sit behind it: the state space in §B6 is formed as a product over axes whose index domains disagree (per-unit, per-check, per-artifact), and the §f5 decision that unknown operators "render as unevaluated, never invalid" is contradicted by the reference verifier in both directions.

Nothing here attacks the axes themselves. H/C/V/T/X measure genuinely different things and the flattening the author is correcting is real. The findings are all in one place: the edge relation, which is the one part of the matrix that is not a labeling but an algebra.

## Steelman

The strongest reading, in KERI's terms. ACDC is "a fragment of a globally distributed property graph" (bible 05 §1; ACDC spec §ACDCs as secure graph fragments), and a presentation is a DAG normalized to one origin (bible 08 §2). A renderer of such a thing has a harder job than a credential-card designer, because the object it renders is partial *by design* — graduated disclosure means the artifact in hand is a view, and the spec is explicit that "All the Graduated Disclosure mechanisms MAY be used in combination" (`spec-body.md:1775`, re-anchored). The author's move is to stop treating "what state is this credential in?" as a single enumeration and to factor it into what-bytes-are-held, why-they-are-not, what-checks-ran, when, and what-correlates. That factoring is correct, and the argument that "Form" is not an axis is correct: the seven variants co-occur freely (`:1775`), and two of them (metadata `:166-172`, bulk-issued `:1773`) are not visibility degrees at all. Naming the seven variants as *coordinates* rather than dropping them is the right disposition.

The document is also right where it matters most doctrinally: it refuses the scalar "verified" badge, it refuses a chain-level roll-up on the grounds that validity logic is EGF-dependent ("The actual logic for interpreting the validity of a set of chained or treed ACDCs is EGF-dependent", `:1112`, re-anchored — bible 06 §1's two-layer split, exactly), and it distinguishes *unperformable* from *fail*, which is the honest reading of a verifier whose evidence is incomplete. R0 is a genuinely good addition: it states what store-retrievability warrants and refuses to read it as a verification scalar, and its claim that edge constraints hold "transitively as of each link's own ingestion" matches the code — `verifyChain` checks only that the far node is `saved` (`src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:418-420`) and does not re-walk it, so the chain is a closure assembled across separate ingestion moments, not a computation run at presentation. Most reviewers of a renderer would not have found that.

## Top findings

### F1: Rule 9 renders a derived edge relation that the disclosure state does not determine

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `effective-edge-operator-not-determined-by-disclosed-material`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §d Rule 9, §c pair 2, §B1 H7/H8; ACDC `spec/spec-body.md:1197-1201` (re-anchored, vendored `651df33e`, identical in `upstream/main` `f0bd097`); keripy `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:435-440`, `src/keri/core/serdering.py:2790-2807`.
- **Finding.** Rule 9 says "The badge shows the *effective* operator per the default-injection rule; syntactic presence of `o` is visible only in the raw view," and §c pair 2 justifies suppressing the difference by asserting that default and explicit `I2I` are "semantically identical." Both hold only when the far node is fully in hand. The spec's rule is: "If the node pointed to by the Edge is a targeted ACDC, i.e., has an Issuee, then the `I2I` Operator MUST be appended… If the node pointed to by the Edge block is an Untargeted ACDC i.e., does not have an Issuee, then the `NI2I` Operator MUST be appended" (`:1198-1201`). The injected value is therefore a function of far-node *content*, and three separate disclosure states make it undeterminable or unstable:

  1. **Far node compact.** keripy resolves targetedness through `SerderACDC.iseaid`, which reads `.sad["a"]["i"]` or `.sad["A"][1]["i"]` (`serdering.py:2790-2807`) inside a bare `try/except` returning `None`. A far node presented with its `a` section compacted to a bare SAID, or its `A` section compacted to the AGID, has no reachable `i`, so `iseaid` is `None` and `verifying.py:440` injects `NI2I` — no issuer/issuee binding. The same far node expanded injects `I2I`. One edge, one issuer, two different relations, selected by the disclosure state of a *different* artifact. This is not a keripy quirk; it is what the spec's own text says when read against a compact far node.
  2. **Far node at H7/H8.** With no far node at all, or a Compact Private Edge that hides even the far node's SAID (`:1165`, re-anchored), the effective operator is not computable by anyone. `verifyChain` returns `None` at `verifying.py:418-420` before it ever reaches the default rule.
  3. **Non-delegative `o` list.** For `o: ["E1E"]` the spec requires the default to be appended anyway (`:1197` triggers on "is present but does not include any of the `I2I`, `NI2I` or `DI2I` Operators"). keripy's guard is `if not ops:` (`verifying.py:435`), which is false once `E1E` survives the filter, so no default is injected and the targeted branch at `verifying.py:465` is skipped. Spec-effective list `["E1E","I2I"]`; keripy-effective list `["E1E"]`.

  So "the effective operator" is not one value. It is one value per authority (spec rule vs this host's rule) and per disclosure state, and in two of the nine H states it has none. Rule 9 mints a badge for it with no undetermined state and no statement of whose rule produced it.
- **Why it matters.** R1 — a chain with a dangling referent — is the state the document itself calls the normal case, and it is exactly where Rule 9 has no answer. Worse, case 1 is silent: a renderer showing "I2I (default)" on an edge whose far node arrived compact asserts a same-holder binding that neither the spec's rule nor the host computed. That is the document's own definition of a naive lie, committed by one of its rules, on the one relation in ACDC that distinguishes "Alice presenting *her own* credentials" from "two credentials that merely share an AID" (bible 05 §5.2). The document is a Phase 2C fixture contract; a fixture built on Rule 9 as written encodes the error into every downstream test.
- **Recommendation.** Give the effective operator its own undetermined state and make Rule 9 render it as *derived*, naming the input it was derived from. Concretely: derive targetedness from the far node's **schema** (which the edge may pin via `s`, `:1176`) rather than from its disclosed bytes, render "operator undetermined — far node unresolved" for H7/H8 and for a far node whose targetedness the schema leaves optional, and treat any host-computed effective operator as host-attested rather than artifact-derived. §c pair 2's suppression rule should be narrowed to "identical *when the far node is fully in hand*," which is the case the vLEI corpus actually attests.

### F2: The state space is formed as a product over axes whose index domains disagree

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `disclosure-state-axes-index-domain-mismatch`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §a (axis definitions), §B6 opening sentence, §d Rules 2 and 10; keripy `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:171-241`.
- **Finding.** The axes are declared over three different index domains: H "per addressable unit"; C "per unit not fully in hand"; V "per check per artifact"; T scoping "every V claim"; X "per artifact." §B6 then forms the state as "H-vectors × C × V-vectors × T × X" — C, T and X as scalars against H and V as vectors. That is malformed on the document's own definitions: C is per-unit by its own axis text, so it is a vector over the same index as H; T is per-check by its own axis text, so it is a vector over the same index as V.

  The V side is the load-bearing half. Checks 5, 6, 7 and 8 are not per-artifact predicates. Edge constraints are evaluated per edge — `for edge in edges: for label, node in edge.items():` with a separate `verifyChain` call and a separate far-node schema check per edge (`verifying.py:171-241`) — and delegation is evaluated per KEL tier. Rule 2 nonetheless fixes the vector at nine cells with "any single element summarizing more than one check fails," while Rule 10 requires that "every check it blocks reports unperformable **with the blocking SAID named**," which presupposes per-edge cells. The two rules cannot both be satisfied by a nine-cell per-artifact vector: naming a blocking SAID is naming a coordinate the vector does not have.
- **Why it matters.** A near ACDC with two edges, one far node in the payload and one at H7, has check 6 = pass on `e1` and unperformable on `e2`. A single cell must pick one, and either choice is the scalar collapse the document's own axis-level naive lie forbids — one level down from where it forbids it. The Phase 3/4 host API is shaped by these arities: an API that accepts a nine-element vector cannot express what a host that instruments its own edge walk actually knows, and cannot express the reach annotation Rule 10 demands.
- **Recommendation.** State each axis's index explicitly and form the space accordingly: H and C indexed by unit, V and T indexed by (check, subject) where subject is the unit the check is *about* (the artifact for 1–4 and 9, the edge for 6, the KEL tier for 5, the registry for 8), X by artifact. Rule 2's "one indicator per check" then becomes "one indicator per (check, subject)," which is what Rule 10 already assumes.

### F3: The §f5 decision on unknown operators contradicts the reference verifier in both directions

- **Severity:** HIGH  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `unknown-edge-operator-outcome-misreported`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §f open question 5 (marked *Closed*), §d Rule 2b; keripy `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:36-47`, `431-433`, `435-440`, `446-452`; commit `4da60571` ("Add E1E identity edge operator… (#1527)"), commit `100172ff3` ("Make edge-operator dispatch spec-conformant and diagnosable").
- **Finding.** §f5 closes with "unknown operators render as **unevaluated, never invalid** — the `unperformable` outcome; keripy's coerce-and-reject behavior is the naive alternative those worked examples criticize." Against the live verifier there are three distinct outcome classes, and "unevaluated" is wrong for two of them:

  - **Token outside `UnaryOps`** (`('I2I','NI2I','DI2I','E1E','NOT')`, `verifying.py:41`) is *silently dropped* by the filter at `verifying.py:432`, and if nothing recognized remains, the default rule at `435-440` injects `I2I`/`NI2I` and the edge is evaluated to pass or fail. The code says so in terms: "A token outside this set is not an operator to this verifier and is skipped" (`verifying.py:36-37`). The host produces a verdict, not an unperformable. Rendering "unevaluated" both misreports it and conceals the more serious fact — that the host substituted a relation the issuer did not write.
  - **`DI2I` and `NOT`** are recognized and *fail closed* with `ValidationError` (`verifying.py:446-452`), deliberately not escrowed: "the chain is present and retrying cannot help, so escrowing would promise a retry that can never succeed." The host's verdict is invalid. "Never invalid" contradicts it.
  - **`E1E`** is implemented (`verifying.py:454-463`, added in #1527). The premise that a keripy verifier "wrongly rejects" it is stale for anything after that commit.
- **Why it matters.** This is filed as a *closed decision*, so Phase 2C fixtures and the Phase 3/4 API will be built on it. A renderer that shows "unevaluated" for an edge the host rejected as invalid inverts the host's verdict — the mirror of the sin Rule 2b was written to prevent ("a renderer must not claim to distinguish fail from unperformed where its host's substrate cannot"). And the silent-coercion case is the one a disclosure-honest renderer is uniquely placed to surface: the issuer wrote operator X, the verifier evaluated `I2I`, and nothing in the artifact records the substitution.
- **Recommendation.** Split the decision three ways to match the substrate: unrecognized-and-coerced (render the written token *and* the operator actually evaluated, flagged as a substitution), recognized-but-unimplemented (render the host's verdict, which may be invalid), and genuinely un-evaluated. Drop the "keripy coerce-and-reject" characterization or re-date it; the current dispatch is deliberate and documented in-line, and #1527 landed E1E.

### F4: Checks 6 and 7 are one relation at two depths, and check 7 is the roll-up Rule 11 forbids

- **Severity:** MEDIUM  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `verification-vector-coordinates-not-independent`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §B3 checks 5/6/7, §c pair 10, §d Rules 2 and 11; keripy `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:171-241`.
- **Finding.** The V axis is framed as "a vector of independent checks." Coordinates 6 and 7 are not independent. "Issuer bona fides (the qualifying chain, e.g. the QVI credential behind an LE credential)" is check 6 applied one hop further out — the same `verifyChain` call in the same loop, run when the far node was itself ingested. The document says as much in §c pair 10: "The same applies to issuer bona fides, implemented as a special case of the same code path." Doctrine agrees at the design level, not just the implementation level: multi-hop authority "is an emergent property of the recursive edge walk, not of any operator" (bible 05 §5.1; 07 §10.5).

  So check 7 is an aggregate over check 6 at depth ≥ 2 — which is precisely a chain-level roll-up, and Rule 11 says "Any aggregate view labels itself as arcviz's own EGF-default rule, not spec-derived, and stays host-overridable." Cell 7 carries no such label. The same phenomenon — depth of a recursive walk — is meanwhile encoded a *second*, different way for check 5, as a reach annotation ("authority derivation *as far as the KELs in hand reach*", Rule 10's "delegation ✓ without reach fails"). One phenomenon, two encodings, one of them exempt from the rule that governs it.
- **Why it matters.** A host cannot report cell 7 without an EGF rule for what "the chain is good" means, which is exactly what `:1112` refuses to define ("EGF-dependent"). An unlabeled cell 7 therefore ships arcviz's own validity-propagation default under the appearance of a spec-derived check — the thing Rule 11 exists to stop, at the one coordinate Rule 11 does not cover.
- **Recommendation.** Fold 7 into 6 as a reach parameter, matching check 5's treatment; or keep it and bring it under Rule 11 explicitly, labelled as arcviz's EGF default and host-overridable.

### F5: "Form does not change verification" is false as stated; the conclusion survives on a different argument

- **Severity:** LOW  **Confidence:** CONFIRMED  **dedupe_key:** `form-not-an-axis-argument-overreaches`  **Objective function:** survivability  **Layer:** acdc
- **Location:** proposal §a argument 2; ACDC `spec/spec-body.md:1822` (re-anchored).
- **Finding.** Argument 2 reads: "Form does not change verification — 'a signature on any variant MAY be used to verify the Issuer's commitment to any other variant… This cross-variant Issuer commitment verifiability is an essential property' (`:1822`). One Issuer commitment; every variant is a view of it." The quote is accurate and the section is titled "Commitments via SAID," but it establishes only that *issuer-commitment verification* is form-invariant, on a section-by-section basis. It does not establish that verification generally is form-invariant, and plainly it is not: at compact form, schema conformance of the expanded attribute section, edge constraints (no `n` value in hand), and issuer-bona-fides traversal are all unperformable. The document's own H axis says so.
- **Why it matters.** The correct argument is stronger and is the one the rest of the document actually uses: form's effect on which checks are performable *factors entirely through* which bytes are in hand, i.e. through H, so form contributes no independent dimension. As written, argument 2 is a false claim in a load-bearing passage that will be quoted, and it sits next to two arguments that are sound.
- **Recommendation.** Replace "Form does not change verification" with "Form changes verification only through H." Keep the `:1822` cite where it belongs — supporting the narrower and genuinely striking point that one Issuer commitment covers every variant.

## Additional patterns noted

**H is not a per-unit reading; it is a dependent one.** Two of the nine H states are defined by reference to a *different* unit's state. H2 vs H3 turns on whether the schema reserves a `u` for the block, so it is a function of H(schema) — §c pair 5 records this as an indistinguishability but not as a structural dependency, and the axis text ("Readable from the artifact given the schema") quietly carries the whole load in four words. F1 is the same shape at the edge layer. The document would be more robust if it said plainly that H is computed over a dependency graph of units, with the schema and the far node as the two dependency kinds, and named the value it takes when a dependency is unresolved. Termination is not at risk — content-addressing makes the edge relation acyclic by construction, since a far node's SAID must exist before the near node's SAID can be computed — so the fixpoint is well-founded and cheap to state.

**The unit index is ambiguous between path and content-address.** "Addressable unit" mixes path-addressed things (a section, a nested sub-block, an `A` element, an Edge) with content-addressed things (a referenced far-node ACDC, a registry TEL, a schema). Rule 14 keys identity by SAID within the rendered material; Rule 1 keys the H-cell inventory by unit. In a DAG where one far node is reachable by two paths, these give different cell counts. It does not appear to break anything today — holdings really are a property of the SAID — but Phase 2C needs one answer before it writes fixtures, and it is worth an explicit sentence.

**Nothing to say against the axes themselves.** H, C, V, T, X measure different things, and the claim of orthogonality is stated with the right caveat (C conditioned on H, T scoping V). I probed for a sixth axis hiding inside X or for a collapse between C and V and found neither.
