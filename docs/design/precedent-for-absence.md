# Precedent for absence: what other disciplines do when they must draw a hole without lying about it

This document answers one question: outside computer science, who has already solved a version of HP1 (see [shape-catalog.md](shape-catalog.md), §HP1) — drawing something known to exist, whose contents are unknown, so it reads as a hole rather than as absence-of-data or as "fine" — and what principle makes their solution work? It does not propose an arcviz treatment. It reports precedent and the reasoning behind it; the charrette does the designing.

**Scope reminder, from the brief.** arcviz needs at least three pairwise-distinguishable states, none of which may read as "nothing here" or as "fine": (1) a node known to exist, contents withheld; (2) a missing referent, where an edge names a far node we do not have; (3) a blinded edge, where a relationship was committed to but its destination cannot be seen, so the topology itself — not just the content — is unknown. The graph-visualization literature was searched first and came up empty; the nearest thing found, [maceachren2012semiotics] (cited in [literature.md](../research/prior-art/literature.md) cluster 3), is uncertainty visualization for point symbols on maps, offered there explicitly as analogy rather than a hit. This sweep looked instead at disciplines that have carried a version of this problem for decades or centuries, under a constraint arcviz shares: the artifact must not lie about what is known.

**Citation discipline.** Every substantive claim below carries a key into [sources.precedent.yaml](../../refs/sources.precedent.yaml), with the capture and quote it rests on saved under [refs/captures/precedent/](../../refs/captures/precedent/). Per [EVIDENCE.md](../research/EVIDENCE.md) rule 6, a source not fetched is a lead, and several capture files record leads that could not be verified this pass (a paywalled AJR original, a bot-blocked NGA server, a Wikipedia passage that didn't corroborate a widely-repeated anecdote); those are named in the relevant capture file and not promoted to claims here. Every claim about whether a convention *transfers* to arcviz is this document's own reasoning, marked **Inference (ours)** — none of the source disciplines say anything about graphs, credentials, or ACDCs.

## 1. Textual scholarship and epigraphy: the Leiden Conventions

This is the closest match found in any discipline, and it is closer than expected: not a binary present/absent notation but a **sixteen-way** epistemic vocabulary, built over nearly a century specifically to keep an editor's guess from being mistaken for what a stone or papyrus actually preserves.

**The convention.** The 1931 Leiden system (restated and critically examined in [dow1969leiden]) assigns a distinct mark to each of: text certainly present; text damaged but legible with certainty; text damaged and doubtful at the level of the whole letter (subscript dot) versus at the level of a single stroke; a lacuna of known length (a bracketed, counted gap); a lacuna of estimated length (`[- ca. 5 -]`); a lacuna of unknown length (`[- - -]`); a space deliberately left blank by the original letterer, called *vacat* — kept typographically distinct from a lacuna specifically so "nothing was ever here" cannot be mistaken for "something was here and is now lost"; an ancient erasure still legible (`⟦ ⟧` with letters shown); an ancient erasure illegible, where the editor restores nothing (`⟦- - -⟧`); an ancient erasure the editor conjecturally restores (`⟦[...]⟧`); a letter certainly read but uninterpretable in context (plain capitals); a stroke visible but the letter unidentifiable (broken capitals); a reading attested by an earlier editor but now itself lost from the object (underlining — a second-hand attestation, directly observed once but not now); and three distinct editorial interventions on the current text — supplying an omission, substituting a correction, and deleting an error — historically all sharing one glyph, `⟨ ⟩` or `{ }`, a conflation the system's own codifier calls a defect (see §5 below).

**The principle.** [dow1969leiden] states the objective directly: "The aim of good editing is to set forth in print, by use of regular, understood, agreed-upon conventions... a clear and correct representation of the original text" — and the rules exist to police the boundary between *attested* and *conjectured* at the level of the individual letter, not the line or the document. Two rules make this explicit rather than incidental. First: "No detectable stroke of a letter should be enclosed within square brackets" — a restoration bracket may never contain something actually visible. Second, and this is the rule most directly on point for arcviz's own risk: "No letter between brackets... should be dotted. A dotted letter within square brackets would naturally be taken to mean not 'reading doubtful' but 'restoration doubtful'" ([dow1969leiden], quoted in full with locators in `refs/captures/precedent/leiden-symbol-table.md`). The convention was designed, in 1931, against exactly the failure mode HP1 is worried about: one notation doing double duty across two different epistemic situations.

**Who codifies it today.** There is no ISO-style body; stewardship is de facto and distributed. [epidoc-guidelines-intro] is the live, versioned, machine-checkable descendant — a TEI-XML schema that states plainly it is meant to "complement the Leiden Conventions... which have been in use in epigraphy and papyrology for over 80 years." Its `<gap>` element moves the print convention's length-confidence distinctions into structured attributes: `quantity`+`unit` when the missing amount is known, `extent="unknown"` when it is not, `atLeast`/`atMost` for a bounded range — i.e. "we know exactly what's missing," "we know roughly how much," and "we don't know the extent" are three separately encodable states at the schema level, not three typographic habits a renderer has to interpret. The Duke Databank / papyri.info's "Leiden+" transcription syntax is a second, parallel operational tool, though its own documentation page could not be fetched this pass and is a lead, not a verified source.

**Known limits — sourced, not hypothetical.** [tei-mahoney-epigraphy] states plainly that "the Leiden convention is neither universal nor flawless... any given book may or may not include a key to its particular markup system," and that "because a fully-marked text can be cumbersome to read, some publications omit the more complicated markings — especially in texts intended for beginners and students." That second sentence is a directly documented instance of "simplify the UI and lose the distinction that mattered," not a hypothetical risk. And [dow1969leiden] himself documents that the single glyph `⟨ ⟩` did at least three different jobs across sub-disciplines and decades — add, substitute, delete — disambiguated only by the editor's surrounding prose, never by the mark alone; his own verdict is that this is a defect the notation itself cannot fix. Every one of these limits assumes a reader who has been taught the code and still has to consult a per-book key. arcviz has no comparable luxury: a verifier is not a papyrologist, and there is no preface to read first.

## 2. Archaeology and architectural reconstruction

**The Venice Charter.** [venice-charter-1964] is the internationally codified document (ICOMOS, 1964/65) governing conservation and restoration of monuments, and it states the honesty-to-a-future-observer rationale more explicitly than any other source in this sweep: "Replacements of missing parts must integrate harmoniously with the whole, but at the same time must be distinguishable from the original **so that restoration does not falsify the artistic or historic evidence**" (Article 12). The load-bearing word is *falsify*: an indistinguishable repair does not merely look inauthentic to today's visitor, it corrupts the physical evidence for everyone who encounters the object afterward, with or without access to the excavation record. Article 9 draws a hard line — restoration "must stop at the point where conjecture begins," and anything past that line must be "distinct from the architectural composition" and "bear a contemporary stamp," i.e. deliberately of its own time rather than a stylistic forgery. Article 15 permits only *anastylosis* — reassembling a monument's own dismembered original parts — never invented reconstruction, and requires added integration material to be "always recognizable."

**A genuine three-way distinction, nationally codified.** [historic-england-2016-recording], the UK's statutory heritage-recording standard, gives a worked example (the Waterloo House survey) with an explicit three-symbol legend for the same feature class — structural timber — keyed to evidential basis rather than presence/absence: solid black square for **original timber in situ**; hatched/crossed square for **timber deduced from peg evidence** (nothing survives, but a trace — a peg hole — elsewhere licenses the inference); open outline square for **inserted (modern) timber**. Separately, the same standard's four-way wall key includes "**Wall of unknown thickness**" (dashed outline where the plinth would be) — existence and rough position asserted, a specific attribute explicitly marked unknown, rather than either omitting the wall or drawing it as fully known. And its "**Site of** [doorway/window/stair]" labeling convention names a feature known to have existed, of which nothing physically survives, without ever silently dropping it from the drawing.

**The field names arcviz's own failure mode, unresolved, as of 2001.** [ifa-hodgson2001-reconstruction], a joint professional-body technical paper, states the temptation directly: "the artist can arrange for [hidden] areas to coincide with those areas that are doubtful or missing... it seems that artists usually render every surface as if full information were available. This may well give a false impression of the extent of the data." The paper floats grey or "insubstantial" rendering as a fix and reports that practitioners don't use it — industry practice, as of that paper, defaults to solidity everywhere, i.e. to "fine." It also states that a standardized notation for grading reconstruction *probability* did not exist and was aspirational ("if this can be achieved") — corroborating, from inside the discipline, that HP1's general shape (not arcviz's specific three-way version) was recognized and unsolved here too.

**A structural gap, not a solvable omission.** Both [historic-england-2016-recording] and the corroborating site-level practice in [norvic-romfa-drawing] (whose "edge of excavation" dash-dot line marks a boundary of *inquiry*, distinct from a dashed line marking an inferred *feature* — the closest analogue found anywhere to "not investigated" versus "investigated, found nothing") were checked explicitly against arcviz's states 2 and 3, and neither has anything. **Inference (ours):** every uncertainty in this body of convention is uncertainty about an *attribute* of a physically located thing — thickness, material, survival — never about the *destination of a pointer*. A building has no structural counterpart to a directed edge whose far end is unresolved; the trace and the vanished feature are always co-located. This is plausibly the actual reason no prior art exists for arcviz's full three-state problem: none of these disciplines has first-class referential edges with their own uncertain resolution.

## 3. Cartography

**CATZOC — the strongest single find in this sweep.** [iho-s68-catzoc] defines Category of Zone of Confidence: six grades (A1, A2, B, C, D, U) baked into the nautical-chart data model itself, not applied as a rendering afterthought. "CATZOC for assessed data (A1, A2, B, C and D) and a sixth category (U) for data which has not been assessed... The attribute CATZOC is a **mandatory** attribute in the S-57 Meta Object class M_QUAL... CATZOC sectors may never overlap and/or have gaps between them." Every charted area carrying depth data must be graded — there is no "unmarked" default a chart-maker can fall back to, and "we haven't assessed this" (U) is not a rare edge case: [iho-s67-mariners-guide] documents that a quarter of the world's surveyed coastal ENC area (25.4%, per a cited 2015 survey) carries the U grade. The successor standard, S-101's Quality of Bathymetric Data, goes further still, tracking position accuracy, depth accuracy, and coverage as **independent axes** rather than one scalar letter.

**The grade is drawn as a shape change, not just a count.** [iho-s67-mariners-guide]: A1/A2/B render as star-counts inside a triangle; C/D render as star-counts inside a horizontal bar. The container shape itself steps down at the "full area search not achieved" threshold — the symbol family carries two bits of information (how many stars, which container) rather than one.

**A second, independently strong find: provenance kept as a separate, queryable channel from the value.** [lpdaac-srtm-userguide-v3] documents that every SRTM elevation pixel has a companion "NUM" file recording which of roughly thirteen named sources produced that exact value — genuine radar measurement, or one of several distinct interpolation/fill methods, down to which secondary dataset was used. The displayed elevation number renders identically regardless of source; the provenance tag is a separate lookup, never merged into or inferred from the primary display. And a fill that fails its own error check is not allowed to stand as a plausible-looking guess: "voids were reintroduced to the ASTER GDEM2-filled SRTM DEM where the delta surface was equal to or outside +/-80 m" ([lpdaac-srtm-userguide-v3]) — an interpolated value that fails validation reverts to an explicit void rather than persisting as an unmarked estimate. [usgs-eros-srtm-void] independently confirms this at the product level: USGS ships "Non-Void Filled" and "Void Filled" as two distinctly named products from the same survey, so a user must opt into the filled version rather than receive it by default.

**Unsurveyed-area symbology gives a genuine third-plus state, distinct from a low-confidence value.** [uschartno1] (U.S. Chart No. 1) distinguishes, in one legend section: a measured negative ("no bottom found at depth shown" — a sounding was run and struck nothing, a confirmed absence rather than an unmeasured one); a measured-but-untrustworthy value (flagged by numeral slant — sloping versus upright digits — carrying the same number at two different trust levels through typography alone); and a true void ("unsurveyed or inadequately surveyed area," a named, bounded region with no depth information at all). Note the first of these — a confirmed, definite negative — is a shape of knowledge none of arcviz's three states currently has a slot for.

**A documented failure: the graded signal is switched off exactly when the stakes are highest.** [iho-s67-mariners-guide] admits this plainly: "This kind of symbology tends to clutter the screen, therefore during execution of a voyage mariners will most likely de-activate this setting." The confidence layer survives mainly at route-planning time and is routinely disabled during real-time transit — the moment a wrong assumption about "how well is this actually known" costs the most.

**A negative result, worth recording as a caution.** [unctad-map-disclaimer]'s disputed/undefined-boundary convention (solid = agreed, dashed = undefined, with a further dotted sub-case for a practically-followed line of unsettled legal status, e.g. the Kashmir Line of Control) was assessed and judged the weakest of the four cartographic analogues researched. **Inference (ours):** a disputed boundary is a case where multiple parties each claim *full* knowledge and disagree — a knowledge *conflict* — not a knowledge *gap*. Arcviz's three states are entirely about gaps. Casual descriptions of "the disputed-border convention" as an uncertainty analogue conflate two different things cartography itself keeps separate: contested location versus unsettled label for an agreed location. Neither maps cleanly onto withheld contents, a missing referent, or a blinded edge.

## 4. Legal and FOIA redaction practice

**Three legally distinct tiers, not a graded scale.** U.S. FOIA jurisprudence has produced what is structurally the richest analogue found for arcviz's own three-way split, because the law treats "produced with reasons," "no record exists," and "cannot confirm or deny" as different *kinds* of answer, not different points on one dial.

- **Produced, itemized, contents withheld.** [foia-552b-cornell] requires that wherever technically feasible, a redaction be marked *in situ* — "at the place in the record where such deletion is made" — with both its extent and the specific statutory exemption justifying it, not asserted once for the whole document. [vaughn-v-rosen-1973] goes further: the *Vaughn* index is a court-mandated, itemized attestation correlating each withheld portion with its claimed exemption, created specifically because "the Government need only carry its burden of proof against a party that is effectively helpless" otherwise. The remedy: "a system of itemizing and indexing that would correlate statements made in the Government's refusal justification with the actual portions of the document," so a court can rule without personally inspecting every withheld item and a requester has enough structure to contest the withholding. This is a close structural match to arcviz's state 1 — a node known to exist, contents withheld, but attested by a typed, external commitment about the *shape* of the withholding.
- **A missing referent is answered as a plain negative** — "no responsive record" — which the FOIA scheme treats as the ordinary, unremarkable case requiring no special apparatus.
- **The Glomar response is a distinct third tier, not a stronger redaction.** [phillippi-v-cia-1976] formulates it precisely: "the situation is as if appellant had requested and been refused permission to see a document which says either 'Yes, we have records...' or 'No, we do not have any such records.'" The agency will not resolve that binary because resolving it either way leaks the protected fact. The same opinion explicitly separates this from the *Vaughn* tier: "we stress that we are not requiring, at this stage, the Vaughn index... If the District Court should decide... that the Agency's refusal to confirm or deny... is unjustified, the standard Vaughn procedures... would then apply." A court, in other words, treats "produced-with-an-index" and "neither-confirm-nor-deny" as different doctrinal orders, one of which only becomes available once the other has been argued away.

**Why the law insists on typed, in-situ attestation rather than silence.** [foia-552b-cornell]'s own marking requirement carries a safety-valve — the marker can be omitted if the marker itself "would harm an interest protected by the exemption" — which is the statute's own acknowledgment that even signaling "something is withheld, and why" can sometimes be unsafe. That safety-valve is the direct statutory ancestor of the Glomar problem: the same law that requires attestation also anticipates the case where attestation itself is the leak.

**A documented failure mode the brief specifically asked for.** [nsa-redacting-with-confidence] names the mechanism precisely: "information hidden or covered in a computer document can almost always be recovered. The way to avoid exposure is to ensure that sensitive information is not just visually hidden or made illegible, but is actually removed from the original document." A black box over text is not a redaction; it is an unredacted document with a picture on top of it. [lopresti-spitz-2005], independently, documents a second and subtler failure: the *geometry* of a redaction leaks information about the withheld content even when the content itself is genuinely gone — "the width of the gulf between the cleartext immediately adjacent to the redacted region reveals the length, in characters, of the missing word(s)." Their own proposed countermeasure (fixed-width boxes) only hides length, not the fact that something was removed: "few will obscure the fact that it is [a word at all]." Two independent, technically grounded sources, decades apart, converge on one principle: **the marker of "something is missing here" must be a genuine structural absence, and must not itself encode anything about what is missing** — not even its size.

**Known limits.** [yale-jreg-glomar-opaque] reports that the U.S. government's own FOIA Advisory Committee (2020–2022) found the term "Glomar" itself "opaque to ordinary citizens" and recommended the plain-language "neither confirm nor deny" instead. This is an official body conceding that even the *name* of a well-established, litigated, forty-year-old third state needs a plain gloss to be understood without legal training — direct evidence against assuming a structurally sound three-way distinction is automatically legible to an untrained reader.

## 5. Medical and scientific imaging reporting

**The honest finding here is a limit, not a win — read it before reaching for BI-RADS as a model.** [acr-birads-faq]'s own category table is split into two headers, "Assessment is Incomplete" (Category 0, alone) and "Assessment is Complete – Final Categories" (1 through 6). That is **one** incomplete/unknown grade, not several: 1–2 are known-and-fine, 3 is known-probably-fine, 4–5 are known-probably-not-fine, and 6 is not an uncertainty grade at all — it exists purely so already-biopsy-confirmed cases can be excluded from audit statistics ("examinations meriting this assessment should be excluded from auditing... auditing that includes such examinations would inappropriately indicate inflated cancer detection rates," per [acr-birads-faq]). More tellingly: [acr-birads-faq] documents, in its own words, that BI-RADS 0 does **not** distinguish "ambiguous finding, need another view" from "technical failure — motion, artifact, insufficient tissue" — both collapse into the same single code, by the standard's own admission. A decades-old, professionally enforced, malpractice-tested standard did not fully solve even a two-way sub-distinction within its one "unknown" category.

**What does transfer: the reporting *structure*, not the category count.** [acr-communication-practice-parameter-2025] mandates "Findings," "Potential limitations," and "Clinical issues" as three separately labeled, mandatory components of every report, with an explicit, standing clause: "If there are factors that prevent answering the clinical question, these should be stated." That sentence is the professional-liability analogue of HP1 — a structural obligation to say, in words that cannot be mistaken for a finding, that the study could not answer the question at all, distinct from answering it in the negative.

**Why this obligation exists, stated by the field's own malpractice literature.** [berlin2000-vague-radiology-report] frames the hazard directly: "Did the language used in the radiology reports lull the referring physicians into inaction and lethargy... rather than alert them to the fact that prompt action must be taken?" — and credits BI-RADS specifically as the field's proof that a controlled vocabulary closes this gap where free text does not: "By providing clear and succinct language for reports, BI-RADS has achieved its goal of making communication of mammographic findings more accurate and decisive. Ambiguity and vagueness have, for the most part, been eliminated in mammography." The mechanism worth taking, separate from BI-RADS's category count, is the *closed vocabulary*: every FDA-approved wording variant for Category 0 keeps "Incomplete" (or lets it be uniquely inferred), so a radiologist cannot free-text an incomplete study into sounding like a clean one.

**A second honest limit.** RadLex, radiology's own decades-long terminology-standardization project, could not be independently verified this pass (both its ontology browser and its own site refused automated fetch), but the secondary literature surfaced in `refs/captures/precedent/radiology-birads-patient-understanding-and-radlex.md` consistently reports coverage gaps for exactly this class of qualifier language ("not visualized," "technically limited," "obscured by artifact") as opposed to anatomic nouns, which it covers well. **Inference (ours):** if even a mature, well-funded medical terminology standard has not fully closed the vocabulary for *why* something wasn't seen, arcviz's three-way distinction may be asking for a finer grain than medicine's own controlled vocabulary has achieved, even though the *obligation to surface an unknown as distinct from a negative* is very well established there.

## 6. Conservation practice and music-notation restoration

**Tratteggio, and distinguishability as doctrine rather than style.** Developed in the 1940s by Cesare Brandi at Rome's Istituto Centrale del Restauro, tratteggio fills a paint loss with fine, discrete hatching that optically blends at viewing distance but remains visibly composed of non-continuous strokes on close inspection — "no tools are required to detect and identify the conservator's intervention" (`refs/captures/precedent/conservation-tratteggio.md`, a secondary composite; the doctrinal grounding is verified primary-adjacent, via [brandi-occelli-tradition]). Brandi's own words state the requirement, not merely the technique: "to keep any integration within the strictest limitations, with practical implementations that are recognizable to everyone, so as **not to use the hypothetical reintegration to usurp the authenticity** which belongs only to the ways in which it was created." [brandi-barassi-2009] gives the cleanest restatement of the doctrine's first principle: "the unacceptability of creative conservation... a conservator must never attempt to substitute the artist... especially when it comes to filling in lacunas," paired with "the complete reversibility of any conservation work." [aic-code-ethics-art23], the American professional code (independently arrived at within a year of Brandi's own 1963 formulation), states the same three obligations as binding ethics rather than technique: any loss-compensation "should be detectable by common examination methods," "should be reversible," and "should not falsely modify the known aesthetic, conceptual, and physical characteristics of the cultural property."

**A genuine third option, not a fill/no-fill binary.** [brandi-occelli-tradition] documents that Brandi's own theory explicitly sanctions declining to fill a lacuna at all when the evidence is too fragmentary or historical value outweighs aesthetic unity: "the path chosen is not to complete anything," giving up "the development of the residual figurativeness of the image" (Brandi 1963a:23, as quoted there). So conservation doctrine, read carefully, has (a) bounded, recognizable reintegration; (b) a deliberate, doctrinally legitimate choice to leave the loss visibly unfilled; and (c) full, undocumented replication — which Brandi treats as forgery-adjacent and forbidden. That third category (leave it visibly unfilled) is a real design move this sweep did not expect to find explicitly sanctioned, and it is not merely "we didn't get to it" — it is a considered alternative to filling.

**Music notation converges on the same principle for editorial additions, with a thinner and more ad hoc practice for physically missing material.** [henle-what-is-urtext] states the scholarly-editing principle plainly: editorial decisions are marked "as such in the musical text," not folded silently into the transcription; square brackets are the near-universal marker across text-critical and musical editing alike for "not in the source, supplied by the editor" (`refs/captures/precedent/notation-urtext-brackets.md`). [grier-editing-grove] states the musicological mirror of Brandi's first principle almost word for word: "the temptation to improve on the composer, holds equal danger. An editor should not be open to the charge of printing the piece the composer would have written had he or she known as much as the editor." Text-critical practice for a *lacuna* itself (as opposed to an editorial addition) goes a step further, distinguishing a physically damaged gap (`***`) from a merely conjectural one (`<***>`) — the same bracket-versus-bare-mark logic Leiden uses for restoration versus reading confidence, applied to the fact of the gap rather than its filled content (`refs/captures/precedent/notation-lacuna-missing-measures.md`, sourced to a University of Toronto text-editions guide, marked secondary). But this research found **no codified convention for an entire missing measure** in music engraving specifically — that case is handled ad hoc, in prose, in each edition's critical commentary, not via a standard graphical symbol. And the famous public reconstructions (Mozart's Requiem, Mahler's Tenth) turned out to rely on prose disclaimers and separate critical apparatus rather than any in-score typographic device (a different notehead, an ink color) distinguishing composer's hand from completer's hand within the printed music itself — a negative finding worth stating plainly, since it is the opposite of what the urtext-bracket convention would predict for a large-scale reconstruction. Elgar's Third Symphony is the sharpest naming-level precedent here: the published work is titled "elaborated by Anthony Payne," never "completed by," a deliberate framing choice, with Payne publishing the source sketches in facsimile alongside his own additions so a reader can compare directly — and an earlier, abandoned BBC attempt at the same symphony, judged by its own producer to be "tinkering," is a direct musical instance of Brandi's "the path chosen is not to complete anything."

## Synthesis: what is worth stealing, and what is not

**The conventions most worth carrying into the design phase, each with the principle underneath it:**

1. **CATZOC** ([iho-s68-catzoc]) — confidence as a *mandatory, partitioning data attribute* rather than a rendering choice. The principle: make "we haven't assessed this" a value the data model requires you to fill in, not a default state achieved by leaving something blank. This is the strongest available answer to "how do you stop a hole from reading as fine" — the graph never has an ungraded region to begin with, because ungraded is itself one of the grades, drawn as a distinct shape (letter U, not a missing star).
2. **The SRTM NUM-file pattern** ([lpdaac-srtm-userguide-v3]) — keep the *displayed value* and its *provenance/confidence tag* as genuinely separate channels, the tag always queryable, never merged into or inferred from what's shown. The principle: a viewer should never have to infer confidence from the rendering of the value itself, and a low-confidence fill that fails validation should revert to an explicit void rather than persist as an unmarked guess.
3. **The Leiden vacat/lacuna pair and its EpiDoc `<gap>` encoding** ([dow1969leiden], [epidoc-guidelines-intro]) — "nothing was ever here" and "something was here and is now lost" are opposite epistemic claims that share a naive rendering (blank space); the convention exists specifically to keep them from collapsing into each other, and the digital descendant encodes *why* something is missing (`reason`) separately from *how much* is missing (`quantity`/`extent`) as independent, structured fields.
4. **The Vaughn index / Glomar split** ([vaughn-v-rosen-1973], [phillippi-v-cia-1976]) — "known to exist, itemized, withheld" and "cannot resolve whether-and-where without leaking the topology" are different *kinds* of answer, not degrees of the same one, and the law only reaches for the second when the first has been argued away. The principle: a withholding should be typed and attested (what shape is missing, and under what category) wherever attesting is itself safe, and only fall back to a structurally different non-answer when attestation itself would leak.
5. **Brandi's "leave it visibly unfilled" clause and the AIC's detectability/reversibility/non-falsification triad** ([brandi-occelli-tradition], [aic-code-ethics-art23]) — a synthetic fill is not the only legitimate response to an absence; declining to fill, visibly, is a considered doctrinal alternative, not a failure to design one. Whatever *is* filled must remain detectable as filled by ordinary inspection, forever, and must never be indistinguishable from what is actually known.

**Conventions distinguishing three or more grades of absence** — the brief's specific ask, since binary present/absent is easy to find and useless here: CATZOC (six grades: A1/A2/B/C/D/U); the Leiden apparatus (sixteen distinct epistemic states, per `refs/captures/precedent/leiden-symbol-table.md`); the SRTM NUM provenance taxonomy (roughly thirteen named source codes); U.S. Chart No. 1's depth section (measured-negative / measured-low-confidence / true-void, three grades in one legend, [uschartno1]); and Historic England's timber legend (original-in-situ / deduced-from-trace / modern-inserted, three grades for one feature class, [historic-england-2016-recording]). BI-RADS, despite its 0–6 numbering, is **not** one of these — it is a one-grade "incomplete" bucket plus five graded-and-known outcomes plus one bookkeeping code, and its own documentation admits the one incomplete grade conflates two distinct causes ([acr-birads-faq]).

**What does not transfer, and why — stated plainly rather than left implicit.**

- **Every discipline surveyed here grades uncertainty about an *attribute* of a located thing (thickness, depth, elevation, letter identity, exemption category), never about the *destination of a relationship*.** [historic-england-2016-recording] and [norvic-romfa-drawing] were checked directly against arcviz's states 2 and 3 and came back empty; **Inference (ours):** this is the likely reason HP1 has no direct hit anywhere — none of these fields has a first-class directed edge whose far end can be independently unresolved. The Glomar/Vaughn split ([phillippi-v-cia-1976]) is the closest anything comes to a *relationship* being the withheld fact rather than an object's content, and even there the "relationship" is metaphorical (the fact of a document's existence), not a graph edge with its own topology.
- **Several of the strongest analogues assume a trained reader and a legend the viewer has already studied.** Leiden requires a per-publication key; CATZOC's confidence layer is routinely switched off by mariners during the highest-stakes phase of use, per the standard's own guide ([iho-s67-mariners-guide]); a Vaughn index or a Glomar response requires legal literacy to parse even when correctly labeled, and the U.S. government's own FOIA Advisory Committee concluded the *name* "Glomar" itself needs a plain-language gloss ([yale-jreg-glomar-opaque]). arcviz's verifier is explicitly not a trained specialist, and there is no preface. Every borrowed convention in this document needs to be re-examined for whether its distinguishability survives *without* the training its home discipline assumes — this sweep did not test that; it only establishes that the source disciplines themselves openly rely on trained readers.
- **BI-RADS's category count overstates what medical imaging actually solved.** It is tempting to cite "BI-RADS has seven grades" as precedent for a rich taxonomy of unknowns; the primary source itself shows this is one incomplete grade plus five complete grades plus a bookkeeping grade, and that the one incomplete grade conflates causes the standard's own FAQ admits it does not distinguish ([acr-birads-faq]). What transfers from radiology is the *mandatory-disclosure structure* (a report component that must state when a question cannot be answered, [acr-communication-practice-parameter-2025]), not a taxonomy of unknowns to copy wholesale.
- **The redaction-geometry lesson cuts against decorative treatments of absence generally.** [lopresti-spitz-2005]'s finding — that the *size* of a redaction mark leaks information about what's missing — is a caution against any arcviz treatment whose visual weight, size, or shape scales with the amount of withheld content; the source discipline's own conclusion is that even a deliberately engineered countermeasure only hides one dimension (length) of the leak, never the fact of the redaction itself.
- **Disputed cartographic boundaries and Category 6 of BI-RADS are both near-misses that turned out not to be uncertainty states at all** — a boundary dispute is a conflict between parties who each claim full knowledge, not a gap ([unctad-map-disclaimer]); BI-RADS 6 is a bookkeeping code for a already-known outcome, kept separate purely to protect audit statistics ([acr-birads-faq]). Both are worth naming as things that looked on first pass like they might distinguish a fourth or fifth grade of absence and, on reading the primary source, did not.
