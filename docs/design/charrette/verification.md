# Charrette verification — adversarial pass

Independent verification of the four charrette proposals against [shape-catalog.md](../shape-catalog.md)'s "Fails if" lists for SC6, SC2 and SC3, the [disclosure-matrix.md](../../research/disclosure-matrix.md) §d rules, and [principles.md](../../research/principles.md) P9/P10/P13 and the T2 prominence budget. The self-assessments were read *after* the mocks; every verdict below was formed from the markup or geometry, then compared against what the designer admitted. Method: all twelve mocks read in full at the markup level (SVG geometry read directly; HTML+CSS reasoned about as rendered boxes); every SAID, AID, `dt`, `rd`, edge-block digest and operator claim cross-checked against the fixture files in `corpus/` and the two load recipes in `corpus/loads/`; the precedent SVGs additionally rasterized at 50% scale with `rsvg-convert` to test small-size claims; all files grepped for `:hover`, script, animation, and non-grey color (none found anywhere — the greyscale and no-hover claims are true for all four entries).

Verdict vocabulary: **pass** / **fail-admitted** (the designer's own self-assessment names it) / **fail-unadmitted** (the point of this exercise). A "pass·" carries a note in the findings.

## The tests

SC6 (shape-catalog.md:151): T1 pairwise confusability of H7/H8/H9 · T2 H7 names its blocking SAID, blocked checks unperformable · T3 H8 edge dropped or manufactured far node · T4 hole resembles loading/spinner/error · T5 cause word with empty cause channel · T6 two H8 edges drawn as definitely converging or definitely separate (including any leak of the fixtures' ground truth through labels, captions or file names) · T7 the `o`-less H7-far edge shows "I2I (default)" · T8 network activity on open.

SC2 (shape-catalog.md:63): T1 fewer than four tiers / delegation without reach annotation / root delegator omitted · T2 positive check outcome without a host vector · T3 default-vs-explicit `I2I` visible outside a raw view · T4 identical AIDs render non-identically · T5 exceptional/degraded framing.

SC3 (shape-catalog.md:87): T1 A appears twice · T2 any of the five edges absent · T3 the occurrences of A's SAID render non-identically · T4 focus interaction detaches the raised card or occludes a sibling's marker.

## Unadmitted failures, itemized

### Precedent — 5

**F-P1 (SC2 T4, mock defect).** The catalog's own occupancy note names "the shared `a.i` correlators of §7," and `ELiFwqI6…TVD` is that correlator: it is `vlei_ecr_auth`'s `a.AID` and `vlei_ecr`'s `a.i`. In the mock it renders as a bare inline monospace string in the ECR-AUTH card (`precedent/sc2-production-chain.svg:132`) and as a bordered pill in the ECR card (`precedent/sc2-production-chain.svg:160-161`). That is the literal SC2 failure observable — "identical AIDs across cards render non-identically (P13.1, Rule 14)" — and the README (`precedent/README.md:37`) claims this test passes, citing only `EOvbsyiK…48S`. The same pill-vs-plain-text split is systematic: every edge chip's `n` value is plain `tspan` text while the same SAID is a pill on the card it names (e.g. `EM7gTDXq…` at sc2 line 146 vs the pill at lines 123-124).

**F-P2 (SC3 T3, mock defect, plus a false self-report).** The footer asserts "The three occurrences of A's SAID … wear one identical pill face (Rule 14, P13.1)" (`precedent/sc3-diamond-depth.svg:140`). The markup contradicts it: A's `d` is a rounded-rect pill (lines 119-120); the `n` values in B's and F's edge chips are plain monospace text (lines 104, 111). The two `n` occurrences do match *each other*, so the catalog's narrowest reading survives, but Rule 14's "identical SAIDs render identically" does not, and the self-assessment affirmatively claims a treatment the file does not contain. A finding of this class in a shipped renderer would be a P13.1 violation; here it is an afternoon's fix — but it was reported as a pass.

**F-P3 (SC6 T6, annotation leak).** Panel D draws the converge and diverge fixtures side by side with the fixture file names as bold labels directly above each render — `two_blinded_edges_converge` at `precedent/sc6-hole-triptych.svg:217`, `two_blinded_edges_diverge` at line 238 — in the same typographic register as the panel's other in-mock labels. Rasterized, the words "converge" and "diverge" are legible in the visual field over two otherwise-identical drawings: the ground truth, which exists only in the fixtures' `.expanded.json` and file names, is resolvable from the mock. The README claims "pass by construction" (`precedent/README.md:35`) and never mentions the captions. Print's designer admitted exactly this failure class (`print/README.md:34`) for a provenance line *further* from the drawing than these captions are; the even-handed conclusion is that precedent has the same failure, closer to the render, unadmitted.

**F-P4 (SC6 T1, texture reuse, minor).** The whole-`e`-section-compact strip on the `blinded_edge_group` card is filled with `url(#trat)` (`precedent/sc6-hole-triptych.svg:142`) — the *tratteggio* hatch defined as H7's lacuna signature. A section-level hidden state and the H7 hole thus share their fill texture; the pairwise separation at that point rests on position and brackets alone. The legend's claim of distinctness "in shape, position, and prose" (line 271) survives, but the vocabulary's own discipline (one texture, one meaning) is broken without comment.

**F-P5 (Rule 2(e), minor).** `diamond_depth_origin` has two edges, hence two (check-6, subject) cells; the origin card draws a single nine-cell V row and the caption "0/9 performed" (`precedent/sc3-diamond-depth.svg:33-34`). One cell summarizes two subjects — the exact arity failure Rule 2 amendment (e) and the two-edge test in Rule 2 name. Both cells would read "unperformed," so nothing false is asserted, but the vector's shape is wrong and the proposal's CATZOC premise ("every card graded") makes the grading unit load-bearing.

Also verified for precedent: the delegator hole AID, all registry `rd` values, edge-block digests and `dt` values transcribe correctly; the three SC2 edge chips are worded identically ("derived") per Rule 9(a) with the raw-bytes difference confined to a caption marked "mock annotation" — the strongest Rule 9 handling of the four entries alongside graph's. At 50% raster scale the unperformed-cell dot (r≈0.55 px effective) disappears and the V wall reads as rows of *empty checkboxes* — the "awaiting ticks" reading SC9 warns against; the README's small-size admission covers the H7 brackets but not this.

### Structure — 4

**F-S1 (SC6 T6, in-render divergence).** The README claims the two fixtures "render structurally identically, differing only in the digest strings" (`structure/README.md:17`) and the page repeats "identical by construction" (`structure/sc6-hole-triptych.html:289`). The two renders are *not* identical: the explanatory paragraph inside each render's band differs — the left band reads "Two committed edges. Whether their destinations are one node or two is exactly what was withheld…" (line 254), the right band reads "Structurally identical to the render on the left. Only the digests differ…" (line 285). The band paragraph is inside the `.band` element, i.e. part of the drawn region, not an external caption — and the right-hand text is meta-commentary about the other render, which no real render could contain. Add the fixture-name headers over each column (lines 227, 258) and the ground truth is legible here exactly as in precedent. The admitted failure (1) covers the two-end-stops risk only.

**F-S2 (SC6, missing verification channel).** No card in the structure SC6 mock carries any verification state: attribute values, SAIDs and edges render with no V channel anywhere in the file (compare `structure/sc2-production-chain.html:92-104`, where every card carries the ten-row ledger the premise requires). Admission (6) (`structure/README.md:40`) concedes the omission for SC3's four subordinate cards but not for SC6, where *every* card lacks it. H1's naive lie in the matrix (§B1) is precisely "a card that renders values with no verification channel implies the display itself vouches for them." Mock-scope defect, but it is the one place a structure card asserts content with nothing saying "unverified."

**F-S3 (SC2, reach misattribution).** All four cards' delegation rows read "unperformable — root delegator `EDYl3N_T…bK8-` KEL absent" (`structure/sc2-production-chain.html:98,142,183,224`) while the KEL-anchoring row directly above each names the issuer's *own* KEL as absent. With zero KELs in hand, delegation is blocked at the first tier — the issuer's KEL — not at the root; naming the root as the blocker on every card implies the walk reached the root, which nothing in hand supports. Rule 10's demand is the blocking identifier; the identifier given is the wrong (deepest) one. Print states the same situation correctly: "reach is **zero tiers**" with two per-tier rows (`print/sc2-production-chain.html:207,235-236`).

**F-S4 (P12, shared with precedent and graph — see cross-cutting).**

Also verified for structure: the three SC2 operator plaques are byte-identical in wording including for the explicitly-written `o` ("I2I — derived"), which is Rule 9(a) applied exactly; `EOvbsyiK…48S` and `ELiFwqI6…3TVD` render as pills uniformly (SC2 T4 pass — the only entry precedent failed); the origin ledger carries separate rows for `shortPath` and `longPath` (Rule 2(e) done right); the origin's revocation row correctly derives "no registry committed (rd absent as issued)" from the fixture.

### Print — 3 substantive, 2 minor

**F-PR1 (SC3, wrong derived operator — the charrette's one factual-content error).** All five edge labels on `print/sc3-diamond-depth.html:92-96` read "I2I †," and the footnote (line 148) asserts the operators were "computed … per the specification's default-injection rule." The computation is wrong. No `diamond_depth_*` fixture carries an `o` field, and none of the far nodes has an `a.i` (no Issuee anywhere in the family — verifiable in `corpus/diamond_depth_*.json`), so every far node is untargeted and the injected default is **NI2I**, not I2I. The other three entries derived NI2I correctly and said why ("far node in hand, untargeted" — `precedent/sc3-diamond-depth.svg:42`; `structure/sc3-diamond-depth.html:94`; `graph/sc3-diamond-depth.html:32,56`). I2I asserts an issuer-to-issuee identity relation these artifacts do not state; presenting it as a computed effective operator is a manufactured claim of exactly the class Rule 9(c)/P16 exist to prevent, on a sheet whose footer says "no credential data invented" (line 150). The self-assessment (`print/README.md:32-42`), the most candid of the four, does not notice it — and it directly falsifies the README's claimed pass "the `le`/`auth` operator difference is invisible outside raw bytes … identical operator annotations": identical, yes; identically *wrong* in SC3.

**F-PR2 (SC6, manifest inconsistency).** The premise's central device is the section manifest "with no unmarked state, so absence is always a filled-in slot" (`print/README.md:7`), and the CSS comment promises "Every card prints all five slots" (`print/sc6-hole-triptych.html:63-64`). No card in either SC6 sheet prints five; sheet-1 cards print four (`s a e r`), and both panel-D cards print **three** — the `s` slot is silently missing (`print/sc6-hole-triptych.html:267-271, 290-294`) even though both fixtures commit a schema SAID. On the design's own logic an unprinted slot is exactly the "missing ink" the manifest exists to abolish; the device's first outing breaks its own invariant, unadmitted.

**F-PR3 (SC6/SC3, subject aggregation in the wall).** The README claims "the wall prints one row per (check, subject)" (`print/README.md:22`), and SC2 delivers it (fifteen rows, per-edge, per-registry, per-tier). The SC6 wall does not: "SAID self-consistency — each of the 7 credentials in hand — ☐" is one glyph for seven (check, subject) cells (`print/sc6-hole-triptych.html:320`), and five more rows aggregate the same way; SC3's wall aggregates delegation and duplicity across issuers (`print/sc3-diamond-depth.html:164-172`) while — inconsistently — splitting the five edge-constraint subjects out with a caption explaining why subjects must not be merged. Rule 2(e) ("one indicator per (check, subject)") is honored in one mock, waived in the next two, and `what-the-screen-adds.md`'s proof table re-asserts the per-subject claim for the whole entry.

**Minor:** the SC3 identifier index calls `ENkj_sx2…egT` "the SAID of the baseline edge block" (`print/sc3-diamond-depth.html:183`); it is the SAID of the `e` *section* in B and F — the baseline edge block's own SAID is `EL94FmuI…PWbF` (which graph and precedent cite correctly), and print's index omits it. **Minor/borderline:** the holder-facing SC3 strip carries "Possession of this paper proves nothing" (`print/sc3-diamond-depth.html:67`) — defensible as Rule 15 flash-pass chrome about the *paper*, but a strict Rule 18 reading ("no presenter indicator, positive or negative, in a holder-facing view") could count it as the foreclosed question rendered askable.

Also verified for print: the seven-credential subject count is right; the ECR card is the only one showing top-level `u` present, matching the fixtures exactly (the sharpest fixture-fidelity detail in the charrette, Rule 12-adjacent); the bona-fides row names ALL-EDGES as arcviz's own overridable rule (the cleanest Rule 11 compliance of the four); the SC2 reach statement ("zero tiers") is the only fully accurate one.

### Graph — 3 substantive, 1 minor

**F-G1 (SC6 T6, the strongest form of the leak).** The fixture names are rendered as the cards' *role lines* — `<div class="role">two_blinded_edges_converge</div>` (`graph/sc6-hole-triptych.html:210`) and `…diverge` (line 227) — the same in-card element that reads "ECR — presented leaf (vlei_ecr)" on every other card in the entry. This is not a caption near the render; it is ground truth printed inside the render's own chrome, in the slot a viewer is trained by the rest of the mock to read as the artifact's role. The README claims the two fixtures "produce identical renders because there is nothing left to differ" (`graph/README.md:33`) and its self-assessment names three risks for this panel, none of which is the role line. Of the four entries, this is the leak closest to the drawn object; only print admitted its (further-removed) version.

**F-G2 (SC2, manufactured holding).** The QVI ledger's schema row reads "unperformed (schema in hand, SAID-verified route required first)" (`graph/sc2-production-chain.html:90`). No schema document exists anywhere in the corpus — the fixtures commit schema SAIDs with nothing to resolve them against (the catalog's gaps G6–G8 exist precisely because no schema fixtures do). "Schema in hand" asserts a holding the material does not contain, and marking schema conformance *unperformed* (viewer's choice) rather than *unperformable-with-material-in-hand* misstates the H-gated reachability of the check on every card. Print read the identical situation as "▨ cannot be performed — no schema document in hand for any committed schema SAID" (`print/sc2-production-chain.html:232`). One of the two is wrong about the same bytes, and it is graph.

**F-G3 (Rule 2 vocabulary instability across the entry's own mocks).** Graph's SC2 renders no-KEL signature validity and no-TEL revocation as ⊘ unperformable on every card (`graph/sc2-production-chain.html:91,96` et seq.); graph's SC3 focused card renders the same substrate states as ○ unperformed — "signatures ○ (no KEL material in corpus) · … · revocation ○ (no TEL)" (`graph/sc3-diamond-depth.html:135`) — and elides checks 4–8 behind a literal "…", making five unperformed cells invisible in the one card the mock presents at full reading scale. Unperformed vs unperformable is the distinction Rule 2 says must never blur, and the entry blurs it *between its own files*, in the direction its own SC2 self-assessment (a) shows the designer knew was live. The "…" additionally fails Rule 2's "unperformed is invisible" test within the focused card.

**Minor:** "(no KEL material in corpus)" — authoring vocabulary ("corpus") inside render text; a render knows about "this material," not the project's fixture corpus.

Also verified for graph: the Rule 9(b) undetermined state is actually instantiated (panel 1a, via a declared load-variant on the diamond — the one liberty, admitted in `graph/README.md:25`), which no other entry did; the H8 "operator undetermined — whether the block even carries an `o` is itself withheld" is the most precise statement of that cell in the charrette; the NI2I derivations are correct; the ×N correlator chips with the open dock are the only always-on P13.1 enumeration; and SC3 panel 3's annotated pile is the only place any entry demonstrates rather than defers the SC3 T4 observable.

## Cross-cutting sweep

**Greyscale, hover, corner badges.** All twelve mocks are hue-free (grep-verified; the single "#160" hit is an `&#160;` entity), carry no `:hover` rules, no animation, no external loads, and no state living in a corner badge. P9/P10's mechanical tests pass everywhere. The honest caveat: precedent's unperformed-dot dies at 50% raster scale (verified image), print admits its identifier layer blurs below ~70%, graph admits the frontier's small-size survival is unproven, and structure's self-test strips are the only in-file demonstration of smallness — its claims are the only ones a reviewer can check without tooling.

**Fixture fidelity.** Every identifier spot-checked across all four entries transcribes correctly against `corpus/` (dozens of values, including registry `rd`s, edge-block `d`s, `dt` timestamps, the withheld-AID and withheld-SAID in both load recipes, and the presence/absence of top-level and `a`-level `u`). The two exceptions are semantic rather than transcriptional: print's I2I derivation (F-PR1) and graph's "schema in hand" (F-G2).

**Card titles are unprovenanced names (P12), three entries out of four.** "Legal Entity credential," "ECR Authorization credential," "QVI — root" appear as card titles in precedent, structure and graph with no marker of where the name comes from. No schema document exists in the corpus, so these titles cannot be verified-schema content; they are designer knowledge imported from the vLEI family the fixtures model. P12 requires every human-legible name to carry visible provenance. Print alone solves it, labeling the name a "host label ('vlei_ecr' — file name, not credential content)" (`print/sc2-production-chain.html:97`). Mock-level for all three, but it is the kind of habit that ships.

**The converge/diverge trap, summarized.** All four entries solved the *geometry* identically and correctly: a region/band with no coordinates, no far geometry ever minted, cardinality stated as 1-to-k. All four then leaked the ground truth through the label layer — print through a provenance line (admitted, flagged on the sheet, kept away from the drawings), precedent and structure through captions over the renders (unadmitted), graph through the in-card role line (unadmitted, inside the render). The uniformity is the finding: the honest-geometry discipline every premise arrived at independently does not extend to annotation discipline in any of them, and a real host will face the same problem the moment it labels a load with its source. The clean fix (opaque load labels, host-side) is the one print's README already names.

## Mock defects versus approach defects

**Mock defects (fixable in an afternoon, should not decide the charrette):** F-P1, F-P2, F-P4, F-P5 (pill/text and arity consistency); F-S1, F-S2, F-S3 (caption discipline, ledger coverage, blocker attribution); F-PR1, F-PR2, F-PR3 and print's two minors (one wrong derivation, slot and row consistency); F-G1, F-G2, F-G3 and graph's minor (label discipline, one wrong holdings claim, glyph stability). Every unadmitted failure in this verification is in this class. Notably, each entry's worst unadmitted defect is a *consistency* failure — the design vocabulary stated in its README is applied unevenly across its own three files — which is evidence about hand-built mocks, not about premises.

**Approach defects (premise-level, and who admitted them):** precedent — the apparatus assumes a trained reader its own sources document as not existing (CATZOC's confidence layer switched off by mariners; epigraphic markings omitted for beginners), and the scholarly aesthetic is itself an unearned trust signal; both admitted (`precedent/README.md:49`), neither solvable inside the premise, because borrowed familiarity is the premise's entire payload and arcviz's audience has not borrowed it. structure — the coined grammar has zero installed convention and pays a training cost per viewer, and geometry-as-currency is exactly what mobile lacks; both admitted (`structure/README.md:44`), both empirical, both adjudicable only by an SC13 mock nobody built. print — honesty bought with bulk trains dismissal (HP2), and the empty checkbox invites the hand-tick that manufactures the false record Rule 15 exists to prevent; both admitted (`print/README.md:50`), the second being a genuine physical-affordance problem the design cannot police. Additionally, F-PR1 dents the premise's own slogan: print enforces *channel* honesty (no hover, no color, no fetch) mechanically, but it did nothing to stop a wrong computed value — content honesty is not a property of the medium. graph — the permanent graph chrome taxes the common case, and the legibility floor that the entire pile refutation leans on is asserted at n=5 desktop and undemonstrated at SC13 scale; both admitted (`graph/README.md:54,64`).

## Closing judgment

**No unadmitted failure found in this pass is fatal to a premise.** The unadmitted column is populated entirely by execution defects — label leaks, one wrong derived operator, one invented holding, consistency slips — and every one of them is repairable without touching the premise it sits in. The designers' candour was real but systematically misaimed: all four self-assessments are honest about *approach-level* risk and blind at the *markup* level, which is exactly the direction of error a charrette's judges cannot see from the READMEs.

**The failures that threaten premises were all admitted, and they rank differently than the defect counts do.** Precedent's is the worst positioned: its central asset (installed convention) is contradicted by its own citations, its compensating prose is what dies first at small sizes (confirmed at 50% raster), and unlike structure's and graph's open risks there is no experiment that rescues it — more training is the thing the project cannot assume. Print's premise survives for the case it was built for (SC14/Rule 15 has no other credible demonstration in the charrette, and print's reach, arity and Rule 11/12 handling are the most spec-accurate of the four), but F-PR1 shows the medium's forcing function does not extend to content, and the bulk/habituation and hand-tick problems cap it as a component of an answer, not an answer. Structure's and graph's premises are the two still standing with only *testable* risks attached — both hinge on the same undemonstrated object, a mobile-width SC13 mock, and both entries say so. The single most consequential piece of missing work this verification surfaces is therefore not a fix to any mock: it is that **the charrette's binding case (SC13) was mocked by nobody**, and the two surviving premises differ exactly there — structure pays for holes in geometry that mobile lacks, graph pays in a legibility floor nobody has measured. Judging between them on SC6/SC2/SC3 alone would reward whichever premise's untested claim is asserted more confidently, which is the failure mode this document exists to prevent.

**Wrong-derivation postscript, for the synthesis to carry:** three of four entries independently derived NI2I for the diamond edges with correct targeted/untargeted reasoning; the one that got it wrong (print) is also the entry whose wording most strongly implies the value was computed. Whatever design wins, Rule 9's "derived, never read" must be implemented as an actual computation with the far node's targetedness as input — the charrette just demonstrated, on real fixtures, that a human annotator writing "effective" operators by hand produces exactly the manufactured-fact failure the rule names.
