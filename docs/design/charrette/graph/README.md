# Charrette entry: the graph-led premise

One of four independent charrette entries. This one starts from the graph and treats the card as one way of rendering a node. Throwaway mocks, hand-authored, no build step: open the three `.html` files in a browser.

## Premise

The object arcviz renders is a directed acyclic graph with diamonds, unequal depths, committed holes, and — because a blinded edge hides its destination — regions whose topology is itself unknown. Topology honesty is the primary obligation; card legibility is subordinate to it. The justification is not aesthetic preference: every graph-structural state in the matrix (H7, H8, H9-at-section, H6-in-position) is a *topology* fact before it is a card fact (shape-catalog I2), and the naive lies the matrix catalogs for them — trimmed edges, manufactured far nodes, absence read as fine — are all lies a card-first layout tells *by default*, because a card-first layout draws cards and lets the connective tissue be chrome. Here the connective tissue is the subject and the card is a node's expanded costume.

## The vocabulary (the design, in five terms)

- **Place** — a node whose content is in hand. Two renderings of the same object: a compact chip (SAID pill, role line, port strip, state marks) and a full card. Node identity is keyed on the SAID and nothing else (SC5's lesson, inherited).
- **Hole** (H7) — a *named* place whose content is not in hand. It has an address, so it gets node geometry: a double-bordered rectangle whose interior wears the unmapped hatch, carrying the blocking SAID/AID as a pill, "content: unknown", and — the graph-led addition — **"out-edges: unknown"**, drawn as a fringe of open-arrow stubs into the frontier. An H7 node's egress is unknowable for exactly the reason its content is; no other entry I know of renders that consequence, and SC6 panel 1 shows why it matters: withholding one interior node splits the known graph into components whose connection, if it exists, is *inside the hole*.
- **Frontier** (H8, and all unknowable topology) — a hatched *region, not a point*. A blinded edge terminates on the frontier boundary with an open arrowhead; no far geometry is ever minted for it. This is the load-bearing invention: **the far end of a blinded edge is a region**, so convergence questions have no coordinate at which they could be answered, and the converge/diverge fixture pair renders identically by construction rather than by discipline. The region carries a cardinality sentence ("committed destinations of these k edges: between 1 and k; coincidence with each other or with any drawn node unknowable").
- **Seal** (H9) — committed absence is not an absence in the render; it is a *presence*: a closed flat bar on the node's port strip, plus the committed-fact sentence phrased about the artifact ("this credential, as issued, has no edge section"), never the world (Rule 13). No hatch, no hole, no frontier anywhere near it.
- **Ports** — every node's edges depart from a labeled port strip, so "has edges", "sealed at issuance", and "edge into the frontier" are three states of one structural location rather than three ad-hoc decorations.

Focus is **in-place expansion**: click a node and it grows to card scale where it stands; the graph reflows but never reorders; everything else stays visible at compact scale with pills, ports, and budgeted markers held above a fixed legibility floor. Scale substitutes for z-order; compression substitutes for occlusion; compression, unlike occlusion, has a floor.

All SAIDs/AIDs are the real fixture values, truncated to ten characters for display; no credential data is invented. ## Greyscale

There is no separate greyscale variant because there is no colour variant: **all three mocks are authored in greyscale only**. Every distinction rides on geometry (bordered node vs unbordered region vs closed bar), line terminals (closed vs open arrowheads), texture (the unmapped hatch), discrete glyphs (○ ⊘ — per ledger cell), and text labels. Colour, if a later pass adds it, is a redundant channel by construction (P10 satisfied in the strong direction). Small-size survival is claimed for the compact chip's pill + port + glyph set and admitted as unproven for the frontier band (see self-assessment).

## Treatment per case

**SC6.** The three absences get three different *kinds* of geometry, not three styles of one kind: H7 is a node (it has an identity), H8 is an edge with no far geometry (it has no identity to draw), H9 is a mark on the near node (it is a fact about the near node alone). That kind-difference is what makes them pairwise unconfusable and is the direct application of the premise: each matrix state is rendered as the topology fact it is. The convergence composition is treated as a layout impossibility to be *represented* rather than resolved: since no honest renderer can place the far ends, the design gives the far ends no places — the frontier — and the two structurally-identical fixtures produce identical renders because there is nothing left to differ. The o-less-edge-into-a-hole state renders "operator UNDETERMINED", never "I2I (default)" (Rule 9(b)).

**SC3.** Layer by longest committed path from the presented node, so the diamond's depth inequality is drawn as an edge visibly spanning two layers. A appears once; all five edges are present; the byte-identical edge blocks in B and F render identically and are annotated as such (Rule 14). Focus is the in-place expansion, demonstrated with A focused: both parents remain visibly attached — the exact condition the pile cannot meet, demonstrated in the same file.

**SC2.** The chain renders as the normal case: neutral typography, no warning chrome, and an explicit normalcy statement. Incompleteness is carried structurally where it actually lives — a KEL hole above the chain (root delegator drawn, blocking AID named, reach annotation on every delegation cell) and ledgers whose cells are ○/⊘ throughout. The P banner is the render's one loud element, by Rule 18's requirement. The identity stratum (one AID node, one KEL hole, thin rounded geometry) exists because Rule 10 demands the root delegator be drawn, and stops there deliberately — KEL event structure is the catalog's named exclusion. The identity-reference link from the QVI card's issuer pill to the AID node is drawn in a third stroke class (thin, dashed) because it is inferred-by-equality, not a disclosed edge (Rule 14's three classes). Correlators are surfaced in the open dock and as ×N chips on pills, scoped to the material in hand.

## The verdict on the pile: refuted

Refuted, not constrained — and the argument is structural, so it should be attacked structurally.

1. A pile is *defined* by mutual occlusion. A stack of cards none of which occludes another is a spread, and the sketch's whole economy (many cards in the footprint of one) comes from the occlusion.
2. AF1's own recorded constraints require that a raised card stay visibly attached to its diamond parents and that no occluded card's budgeted marker vanish without trace. Markers and edge attachment points are *located on the cards*; a card that is 90% occluded has, with probability proportional to that fraction, lost the specific pixels where its marker sits and where its edges attach.
3. For SC3 specifically: B and F each carry an incident edge to A and an identifying pill that must render identically (Rule 14). Raise A and the pile must either keep B and F legible at their attachment points and pills — which means un-occluding most of both cards, i.e. not a pile — or attach A's two edges to hidden geometry, at which point the reader cannot distinguish the depth-2 path from the depth-3 path, which is the single fact SC3 exists to convey. The failure is forced by the diamond, not by an unlucky viewport.
4. The failure mode is not hypothetical carelessness but the documented indicator-removal effect: an occluded stale or P marker is a removed indicator, and removed indicators go unnoticed (Schechter, via AF1's failure column). A layout whose *normal interaction* removes indicators has automated the attack.

What survives of the commissioning sketch is everything that made it attractive except the z-axis: click-to-attend, one card at reading scale, context retained. Focus-in-place delivers those on the flat: the clicked node expands where it stands, everything else compresses but nothing disappears. The synthesis I am claiming is that **the pile's gesture never needed the pile's geometry** — occlusion was an implementation of attention, and compression-with-a-floor implements the same attention without discarding topology. `sc3-diamond-depth.html` panel 2 is the proposal; panel 3 is the pile rendered in good faith with each failed acceptance test annotated on the drawing.

## Self-assessment against the "fails if" lists

**SC6.** Pairwise distinctness of H7/H8/H9: met by kind-difference (node vs edge-terminal vs port mark). Blocking SAID named, checks report unperformable: met. H8 neither dropped nor given a far node: met. Cause words: "unknown" everywhere: met. Two H8 edges neither definitely converging nor definitely separate: met geometrically. No network activity: met (static files). **Where I fail or am at risk, plainly:** (a) the *unknowability* of convergence is only partly structural — the geometry declines to answer, but the statement "between 1 and 2, coincidence unknowable" rides on a text label, and P9's own literature says small passive text does not reliably carry meaning; a hasty reader may parse the frontier band as "a node named Unknown" and read the two edges as converging into it. I judge this the design's weakest point and have no structural fix that does not itself manufacture separation. (b) The unmapped hatch risks a skeleton-screen (loading) reading for users habituated to grey placeholder blocks; the mitigation is declarative static text and the absence of spinner geometry, which is again text doing safety work. (c) The hole's interior hatch is shared with the frontier deliberately (both mean unmapped); at very small scale, border-plus-pill may not survive, and a shrunken hole could read as a frontier fragment — the distinction degrades at exactly the small sizes the constraints say distinctions must survive.

**SC3.** A once, five edges, identical n-pills identical: met. Focus keeping parents attached and nothing occluded: met by construction in panel 2. **Where I am at risk:** the mock demonstrates focus at n=5 on a desktop-width board. The claim that compact chips hold their pills, ports, and budgeted markers above a legibility floor at SC13's scale (8–9 cards, mobile width, four budgeted states co-present) is asserted, not demonstrated — my pile refutation leans on that floor existing, and if the floor cannot be held at mobile width, focus-in-place inherits a milder version of the pile's own disease (markers legible in name only). I did not build the SC13 case and I do not claim it.

**SC2.** Four tiers: met. Root delegator drawn, reach annotations present: met. No positive outcome without a host vector: met (no pass glyph exists anywhere in the mock). Default-vs-explicit `I2I` suppressed: met — the three operator chips are deliberately indistinguishable, with the raw-bytes difference noted only in a caption outside the render. Identical AIDs identical: met, with ×N chips. **Where I fail or diverge, plainly:** (a) the catalog's stated occupancy is "nearly all cells *unperformed*"; my ledgers render checks 3/4/5/8 as *unperformable*, because these fixtures genuinely contain no KEL or TEL material — for the corpus artifacts that is the honest cell, but it means the mock does not actually exhibit the all-unperformed wall as a store-fed host would produce it, and a reviewer testing the stated occupancy verbatim will find I rendered a neighboring one. (b) "No exceptional/degraded framing" is a judgment call I may lose: the render's sum — black P banner, double-bordered KEL hole, two ⊘-heavy ledgers per viewport — could read as an alarm state despite the neutral typography and the normalcy caption. I claim the P banner's loudness is Rule 18's requirement, not my choice, but the *aggregate* impression is untested. (c) HP2 stands unanswered here as everywhere: I render the wall honestly and have no mechanism that prevents its habituation; the normalcy caption arguably *accelerates* habituation, and I chose it anyway because the alternative (alarm framing of the default state) is the worse lie.

## Prominence accounting

Budgeted states (T2): stale, P-unknown verifier-facing, conditioned metadata marker, H2-guessable. These mocks spend the budget once: the **P banner** (loud in SC2 where the V surface is large; matched-weight slim frames on SC6/SC3 where the V surface is compact, keeping P ≥ V per Rule 18). Stale never fires (no policy supplied is rendered as its own quiet state, Rule 4); the metadata and H2-guessable states do not occur in these three cases. Holes, the frontier, and seals are *not* treated as budget spends: they are terrain, not indicators — they occupy layout space the way nodes do, read as structure, and do not compete with the four alarm-grade states for the viewer's interrupt attention. A reviewer may reject that classification; if the frontier band is ruled a prominence spend, SC6 panel 4 is spending it twice per board and the accounting fails. The ×N correlator chips, ports, operator chips, and ledger glyphs are all quiet-tier by design.

## The strongest objection to this approach

The graph-led premise taxes the common case to be honest about the rare one. SC2 — the normal production artifact — is a straight line plus one hole, and SC1 is a single card; for those, this design's permanent graph chrome (ports, strata, a frontier that is usually empty, ledgers sized for per-subject arity) is overhead a card-led design would not pay, and the viewer who meets arcviz a hundred times meets a nontrivial topology perhaps twice. The design's answer — that the "trivial" case already contains a hole, a wall, and a P banner, so there is no chrome-free honest render of anything — is true but does not fully dispose of the objection, because the *degree* of subordination is a dial and this entry has it turned to the far end: identity pills outrank human-legible names everywhere, cards read as instrument panels, and a first-time holder looking for "what does my credential say" must read past scaffolding erected for an adversary they have never met. If the judges reject this entry, I expect that to be why — and the honest concession is that nothing in the research fixes where the dial belongs; the premise fixes it, which is what a premise is for.
