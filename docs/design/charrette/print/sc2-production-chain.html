Terms of use — and the space the truth would take

Each card on sheet 1 prints r: none as issued — committed in its manifest: these four credentials carry no rules section, and their SAIDs commit to that. That absence is a fact about these fixtures, not about the credential family they model. Production vLEI credentials each carry two or three legal-language clauses of 341–480 characters — against data fields of 12–44 characters — and terms encumber every reader of the render, so a card that hides them to save space is where the confidentiality chain silently breaks. The band below is a sizing specimen only — it is not credential content and says so, twice, because a specimen that could be mistaken for terms would be its own lie:

Sizing specimen — not credential content

This paragraph is a sizing specimen, not terms of use. It is set at data-field size and sized to the production band of three hundred forty-one to four hundred eighty characters, because a printed card must plan for prose of this length as the normal case, not the exception. On paper there is no toggle to collapse it behind, and that is the honest arrangement: a reader bound by terms must be able to read the terms on the same surface as the data they encumber. Specimen ends.

Layout consequence, stated plainly: the rules band wins its space and the data fields keep theirs — the band sits full-width below the fields it encumbers, so 44-character facts are never squeezed by 480-character boilerplate, and the boilerplate is never truncated. A persistent “terms attach” line with the r section's SAID would ride the manifest strip; the full prose prints here, once per credential, in full.
