# Charrette synthesis: the composite design direction

Synthesist's report on the four blind entries — [precedent](precedent/README.md), [structure](structure/README.md), [print](print/README.md), [graph](graph/README.md) — each of which mocked SC6, SC2 and SC3 from [the shape catalog](../shape-catalog.md). This is not a winner announcement. It is the composite a wireframe-and-interaction-spec phase should build from: each element taken from wherever it was strongest, with the reason it beat its alternatives, the conflicts the evidence does not settle framed for Daniel, and the failures the composite inherits stated plainly. Method note: every claim below that is load-bearing was checked against the mock markup, not just the READMEs — all four SC6 mocks in full, `graph/sc3-diamond-depth.html` (the pile exhibit), `structure/sc2-production-chain.html`, `print/sc6-hole-triptych.html`, and targeted checks of the rest. Where I could only verify a claim from prose, I say so. Statements marked **inference** are mine, not any seat's.

## 1. The convergences, audited

Four designers who could not see each other produced treatments that agree on the deep structure. But all four read the same research corpus, so before crediting convergence, each agreement has to be sorted into *rule-following* (the corpus dictated it — weak evidence), *independent derivation* (the premises produced it separately — strong evidence), or *shared blind spot*.

**Convergence 1 — a blinded edge gets no destination geometry.** Verified in the markup, not just the prose. Precedent: the edge runs solid, transitions to spaced dashes, and terminates at a fixed-size double-bar gate on a hatched "unsurveyed" region inside which nothing is ever drawn (`precedent/sc6-hole-triptych.svg`, panel B). Structure: the edge crosses a double boundary into a hatched "non-place" band and ends at a flat end-stop bar labeled with the edge block's own digest (`structure/sc6-hole-triptych.html`, panel 2). Print: the edge ends at a perpendicular terminus bar with no box of any kind — "a box here, empty or otherwise, would be an invention" (`print/sc6-hole-triptych.html`, panel B). Graph: the edge dies on the boundary of a hatched "frontier" region with an open arrowhead, no far geometry ever minted (`graph/sc6-hole-triptych.html`, panel 2). These are four renderings of one idea — the far end of the relationship exists as a commitment but receives no drawn identity — and in all four the converge/diverge fixture pair renders byte-identically at the canvas level, checked against the actual markup of each panel D/4.

Sorting it: the *no far node, no dropped edge* half is rule-following — the disclosure matrix names both naive lies and forbids them, so all four were complying, not converging. The half that is genuine independent derivation is **region, not point**: three of four seats (precedent from the hydrographic unsurveyed-area convention, structure from its space-is-commitment grammar, graph from layout necessity) separately concluded that unknowable topology needs a drawn *region whose declared property is that it has no coordinates*, so that convergence questions have no place at which they could be answered and identical rendering falls out by construction rather than by discipline. Nothing in the research says "draw a region"; the survey's own negative finding is that no discipline grades the destination of a relationship ([precedent-for-absence.md](../precedent-for-absence.md)), and precedent flags its band as adaptation past the edge of the evidence. Print declines the region (paper economy) but reaches the same identical-by-construction property with a grouping bracket and a cardinality sentence. Three independent derivations plus one compatible refusal is the strongest result this charrette produced.

**Convergence 2 — geometry follows ontology.** All four render the three absences as different *kinds of object* before any styling: H7 gets node geometry because a missing referent has an address (bracketed lacuna / dark socket / dashed named box / double-bordered hole — all at a real graph position, all carrying the blocking SAID); H8 gets edge-without-far-end geometry because there is no identity to draw; H9 gets no graph ink at all, rendering as a property of the *near node* (precedent's in-card vacat strip; structure's seam at the exact position where a sibling card has its edge-departure band; print's manifest slot reading "e: none as issued — committed"; graph's closed seal bar on the port strip). Verified in all four SC6 mocks. Sorting it: partially seeded — the catalog's I2 already says the occupancy states are topology facts, and the matrix defines the three states at different levels. But the specific placement consequence — that H9 lives *on the card* while H7 and H8 live *in graph space* — was dictated nowhere and appeared in all four. That placement is what makes the three unconfusable at any size where shape survives, and the composite treats it as settled.

**Convergence 3 — the hole's own egress is unknown, and severed components stay severed.** The graph entry claims its "out-edges: unknown" fringe is unique ("no other entry I know of renders that consequence," [graph/README.md](graph/README.md)). The claim is false: precedent draws the same fringe — dashed stubs above the lacuna captioned "its own edges: unknown — the artifact that would declare them is the thing missing" (`precedent/sc6-hole-triptych.svg`, panel A). Because the seats were blind, the false uniqueness claim is itself the best evidence of independent derivation in the whole exercise: two premises that could not see each other drew the same non-obvious consequence. All four also refuse to bridge the two served fragments that the H7 load recipe severs, each with an explicit no-manufactured-edge caption. The composite adopts both as settled.

**Convergence 4 — the shared blind spot.** All four seats end up with prose doing the last, hardest yard of the work, at exactly the sizes where their own premises say prose dies first. Precedent admits its glosses are "load-bearing at exactly the small sizes where prose dies first"; structure admits its band's positionlessness "is carried by a caption — i.e., at the weakest point of my own premise"; graph admits the cardinality statement "rides on a text label, and P9's own literature says small passive text does not reliably carry meaning"; print's identifiers "blur before the marks do" below ~70% scale. Four seats independently reaching for the same compensating device is not validation of the device — it is the corpus's real gap (no notation exists for "the destination of this relationship is ungraded") showing through every premise. The composite inherits this and section 5 says so.

## 2. The composite

Working vocabulary for the wireframe phase, chosen from the graph seat because its terms are plain English and carry no borrowed scholarly authority (precedent's own strongest objection is that *lacuna*/*vacat* radiate unearned trust): **gate** (render-level presenter enclosure), **hole** (H7), **frontier** (H8's region), **seal** (H9), **port** (per-node edge-departure strip), **ledger** (per-(check, subject) verification record), **manifest** (per-card section-occupancy row), **strip** (static-record header). The Leiden/CATZOC/Glomar names stay in the design notes as the arguments behind the forms, not as UI vocabulary.

### 2.1 H7 — missing referent: a hole, which is a node

From graph and structure jointly: full node geometry at the exact position the artifact would occupy, displacing the layout at full size (structure's rule: the layout *pays* for the absence), with a heavy doubled border, an interior wearing the same unmapped hatch as the frontier, the referent's real SAID as a pill on its face, and four fixed statements — what is missing, expected schema where an edge states one, "cause: unknown — no attestation supplied" (Rule 5), "no retrieval attempted" (Rule 17) — plus every blocked check reporting unperformable with the blocking SAID named (Rule 10). When the hole is interior, the egress fringe (Convergence 3): open-headed stubs into the frontier, and no bridge ever drawn between components the hole severs.

Why this over the alternatives: print's dashed border loses because the seat itself flags the collision with drop-target and skeleton idioms, and dashes are already spoken for in the third stroke class (below). Structure's solid dark slab is the best small-size survivor but loses on two grounds: it breaks the one-texture-for-unmapped vocabulary (the hatch shared between hole interior and frontier is what lets a viewer read "unknown territory" as one concept, with border-plus-pill meaning "a named window onto it" — graph's argument), and a black mass is the loudest object on any board while holes are terrain, not alarms (graph's prominence accounting, and structure's own "arguable (4)" concedes the gestalt cost). **Contingency, recorded rather than hidden:** graph admits the hatch-sharing degrades at very small scale — a shrunken hole could read as a frontier fragment. The SC13 build in section 6 tests exactly this; the slab is the named fallback if border-plus-pill cannot hold the distinction at the compact-chip floor.

### 2.2 H8 — hidden destination: an edge into the frontier

From graph, with precedent's constraints bolted on: the committed edge is always drawn, leaves the near node's port, and dies on the boundary of a hatched frontier **region** — a first-class drawn object whose declared property is that positions inside it do not exist. The terminal is an **open arrowhead** at the boundary. The edge label carries only what is committed: the slot name and the edge block's own SAID, explicitly glossed as the identity of the edge, never of any destination. Cause words are forbidden — "not in this presentation," never "withheld/private/redacted" (all four seats agree; Rule 5). From precedent, two constraints that survive as spec requirements: the terminal and entry marker are **fixed-size** — no property of the marker's geometry may scale with what hides (Lopresti–Spitz, `precedent/README.md`) — and a wholly compact edge *section* gets one stub with `{count: ?}` regardless of how many edges hide. From structure: frontier spend is sublinear by construction — any number of blinded edges terminate in the one shared band.

Terminal grammar, and why graph's wins: the composite needs three line-and-port terminals to be pairwise unconfusable — **closed arrowhead** = committed edge, destination in hand; **open arrowhead into the frontier** = committed edge, destination unmapped; **closed flat bar on the port strip** = seal, no edge as issued. Structure used a flat bar for both its H8 end-stops and (as a full-width band) its H9 seam — same glyph family for two states Rule 1 says must never share a face. Precedent's double-bar gate is distinct but adds a fourth glyph where three suffice. Print argues for no arrowhead at all ("there is nothing to point at"), but — **inference** — the open head answers that objection rather than violating it: a closed head asserts an object pointed at; an open head asserts only direction, and the edge genuinely has direction and genuinely continues, unmapped. Print's terminus bar remains the correct *print flattening* where the frontier band is cropped for space, with the bar defined as the edge's terminus.

**The convergence composition** (two blinded edges): k edges enter the frontier at k separated points; a grouping bracket spans the entry points (print's device); the bracket carries the cardinality sentence (graph's device): "committed destinations of these k edges: between 1 and k; coincidence with each other or with any drawn node unknowable from this material." The converge and diverge fixtures render identically by construction. The residual — a naive reader counting k terminals and concluding k destinations — has no structural fix in any seat; structure judged it "the sharpest residual risk in the entry and I do not know a geometry that removes it without lying in the other direction," and no other seat found one. The bracket-plus-sentence is mitigation, not solution; it goes on the section-5 list.

### 2.3 H9 — committed no-edge: a seal on the port strip, and a manifest slot

From graph and structure (who agree in substance): a closed flat bar at the exact structural location where edges would otherwise depart — structure's formulation "same place, opposite structure" is the composite's rule, and its H9 panel demonstrates it with the control twin whose only visible difference is departure-band versus seam. Worded always as the issuance-time fact ("this credential, as issued, has no edge section — committed by ⟨SAID⟩"), never as a fact about the world (Rule 13). Zero graph ink, no hatch, no hole, nothing dangling.

From print, the always-on **section manifest**: every card carries a filled slot per section (s/a/e/r), so absence is a filled-in slot rather than missing ink — the CATZOC no-ungraded-region move applied to card anatomy, and the thing that keeps H9 legible even when the graph context is cropped or the card travels alone (SC1). On compact chips the port strip *is* the manifest's e-slot. Precedent's full-width double-ruled vacat strip loses: it spends card-interior structure on the one absence that is actually resolved knowledge, and precedent itself flags the "budget inversion" objection in its README. The quiet seal plus manifest slot buys the same distinctness for near-zero prominence.

### 2.4 The normal case (SC2): gate, ledger, foundation

All four converged here so completely that most of this section is selection of wording rather than of structure — though the convergence is largely rule-following (Rule 18 dictates P-before-V and its loudness; P3 dictates the visible unperformed wall), so it counts as compliance, not independent evidence.

- **The gate.** Structure's geometry: a full-width double-bordered enclosure the chain visibly hangs from — render-level structure, one per render by construction, encountered before any verification material. Print's wording discipline inside it: "No evidence binds these bytes to any party controlling the holder AID they name. A replayed copy would satisfy every check below identically." Chosen over precedent's banner and graph's black pframe because the gate is *enclosure* rather than *label* — the chain hangs from it, so an issuer-controlled canvas cannot counterfeit its absence without counterfeiting the render's whole geometry (structure's P8-derived argument). In a holder-facing render the gate does not exist and nothing marks its absence (Rule 18's foreclosure; demonstrated by print's SC3 declared holder-facing).
- **The ledger.** Per-(check, subject), outcome space declared in the header ("no host vector supplied · outcome space {pass, unperformed, unperformable} · fail not offered — no host declaration"), unperformable rows naming their blocking identifier, revocation horizon-bounded, "no freshness policy supplied" rendered as a visible state (Rules 2–4). Cell vocabulary: word-states or closed glyphs (structure's small-caps words; graph's ○/⊘). **No empty-box glyph exists anywhere in the composite, on screen or paper.** This is print's own finding turned against print's own wall: an empty checkbox is an affordance for hand-ticking, and a reader who ticks it has manufactured precisely the false "checked" record the static-output rule exists to prevent — "the caption says 'never from this page,' but the affordance argues otherwise, and against a habituated reader the affordance usually wins" ([print/README.md](print/README.md), strongest objection). A word offers no blank to fill.
- **The foundation.** Below the chain root's seal, the render pays for what it does not have: a deliberately quiet identity stratum (graph's framing, structure's content) carrying the root delegator as a hole at KEL granularity, blocking SAID named, feeding the unperformable rows above, connected by the third stroke class. Verified in `structure/sc2-production-chain.html` and graph's SC2.
- **Three stroke classes** (Rule 14, precedent and graph independently): solid with terminal grammar = disclosed ACDC edge; thin dashed, no arrowhead = identity-reference/context link (inferred by equality or reported by host manifest, visibly not an ACDC edge); the frontier boundary. No fourth class without a rule change.
- **Operators** (Rule 9, all four consistent once the corner cases are laid side by side): far node in hand → one uniform wording regardless of syntactic `o`-presence ("I2I — derived (far node in hand, targeted)"), byte-identical across explicit and defaulted edges, checked by diff in structure's and precedent's SC2; `o` explicit but far node absent → operator shown as declared in the artifact, relation recorded uncheckable; no `o` and far node absent → "operator undetermined," never "(default)." Provenance of the operator ("derived") appears in chip text only, never as a badge.
- **No warning chrome anywhere.** The gate, the wall, and the foundation are the standing architecture of every verifier-facing render — they never toggle on "problems," which is the only available answer to "render as normal" that does not lie (structure's and graph's shared position, adopted). Whether honest normality nonetheless *reads* as degraded is empirical and unresolved; all four flag it; section 6 tests it.

### 2.5 The diamond (SC3), focus, and the pile

Statics — all four agree, all verified where read: one node per SAID (A drawn once, at its longest-path rank), all five edges drawn, the short path paying with an edge that visibly spans ranks, annotated at the point a reader would wonder (print's point-of-use caption habit, adopted composite-wide in place of any decoder-ring legend); the three occurrences of A's SAID wear one identical pill face with no connecting glow (surfacing equality without implying a comparison ceremony ran — Rule 14, AF10); the byte-identical edge blocks in B and F render identically and are annotated as equality, never similarity; path length is read by counting cards, and no derived quantity gets a badge.

Interaction — from graph alone, because only graph resolved it: **the pile is refuted, and the refutation is adopted.** The argument is structural and was verified against the good-faith pile exhibit in `graph/sc3-diamond-depth.html` panel 3: a pile is defined by mutual occlusion; AF1's own recorded constraints ([affordances.md](../../research/affordances.md) AF1 fails-if column) require occluded cards to stay legible exactly where edges attach and markers sit; for any DAG where an occluded card carries two incident edges — the diamond, necessarily — the pile must either un-occlude it (then it is a spread) or attach edges to hidden geometry (then depth-2 and depth-3 are indistinguishable, which is the one fact SC3 exists to convey). The failure is forced by the diamond, not by viewport luck, and an occluded budgeted marker is a removed indicator, which the literature says goes unnoticed. What survives of the commissioning sketch is everything but the z-axis: click-to-attend, one card at reading scale, context retained — delivered by **in-place expansion**: the focused node grows where it stands, the graph reflows but never reorders, everything else compresses toward a fixed legibility floor (SAID pill, port strip, budgeted markers held above it), and nothing disappears. Scale substitutes for z-order; compression substitutes for occlusion; compression, unlike occlusion, has a floor. The floor itself is a claim, not a demonstration — graph says so plainly — and it is the composite's single most falsifiable assumption (section 6).

### 2.6 The prominence rationing rule

Adopted from structure, because the audit shows it is not an artifact of structure's premise: **prominence is allocated by the ontological level of the state, never by its severity, one non-overlapping vocabulary per level.** Render-level (P): the gate — at most one per render by construction, O(1). Graph-level (holes, frontier, foundation): geometry — space allocated or refused; multiplies only as the graph's facts multiply, and a render with thirty holes *should* be dominated by holes; frontier spend sublinear. Card-level (T2's budgeted card states): deformations of the card's own form, disjoint per state, at most two per card. Unit-level (V cells, T rows, operator vocabulary, correlator marks): discrete symbolic form only, zero structural spend — distinct per Rule 1 through shape and text, deliberately quiet, because spending structure there is the inversion T2 forbids ([principles.md:149](../../research/principles.md)).

The generalization test the orchestrator asked for: descriptively, **all four seats already obey this rule** — each spends exactly one loud element (P), each renders the absences as space rather than as alarms, each keeps unit states in quiet symbols, and each says so in its own accounting section (precedent's "spent and withheld," print's "budget accounting," graph's "terrain, not indicators"). Structure did not invent a rule its premise happened to permit; it named the grammar the whole charrette's output was independently written in. That is why it is the composite's spine, and it is the charrette's answer to HP3 ([shape-catalog.md](../shape-catalog.md) §(c)): the four budgeted states deform four different levels, so co-presence never competes for one channel, and the only unbounded multiplier — unit states — is the one that costs nothing structural. Two honest boundaries: the card-level tier (stale breaking the frame, H6 unframed, H2-guessable spending transparency) is sketched in structure's README and mocked by no one, so it is direction, not design; and the whole rule's behavior under multiplication is argued, not demonstrated, until SC13 is built.

### 2.7 The static register

Adopted from print, whole: the static artifact is a **worksheet, not a certificate** — ragged utility typography, no seals, no borders that flatter; a static-record strip on every sheet ("STATIC RECORD — NOT A CREDENTIAL, NOT A VERIFICATION"), with a hand-fill "printed on / by" line because the artifact cannot know its own print moment; point-of-use captions instead of a legend; the identifier index always-on (P13.1 with no "on demand" to defer to); and the conformance-floor rule from [print/what-the-screen-adds.md](print/what-the-screen-adds.md): the print artifact is the floor — if the screen build cannot produce these sheets from the same render model by flattening alone, the screen build is carrying state where it should not, and no distinction may live on hover, collapse, or animation without a persistent printed-equivalent signifier.

This bears on the export dilemma exactly as far as it bears and no further. [threat-model.md:39](../../research/threat-model.md) leaves the sharpest residual in the model unresolved: an export carrying transferable proof removes the holder's control over resharing; one carrying none is a forgeable claim of verification; Rule 15 governs what the export *says*, and what it *carries* stays open. The charrette settles the *says* half — the register is the answer, and the no-empty-boxes rule (§2.4) closes the one channel print found by which a truthful worksheet could be promoted into a false certificate by hand. The *carries* half it does not touch (Daniel's decision Q3 below). One register risk print names against itself stands: forms feel official, so the register built to shed authority may quietly re-acquire it.

## 3. What each seat contributed to the composite

No seat's output is discarded; the composite's skeleton is graph's and structure's, its conscience is print's, and its defense-in-depth is precedent's.

**Precedent** supplies the constraints that will keep the wireframe phase honest under adversarial review, even though none of its skins won: the no-ungraded-region discipline (every render region graded, "unassessed" itself a state — the manifest and ledger both descend from this); the fixed-size anti-leak terminal (nothing about an absence marker's geometry may scale with what hides); the Glomar kind-argument (H8 is a different *class* of answer from H7, never a darker version of the same node — the reason the composite's frontier is a different genus from its hole, not a styling variant); the detectability obligation (every intervention distinguishable from content on close inspection); and the two warnings the composite must carry forward — trained readers switch the confidence layer off during the highest-stakes phase, and scholarly aesthetics is an unearned trust signal. Its README is also the citation map the interaction spec should inherit.

**Structure** supplies the composite's spine (the level-allocation rule, §2.6), the socket's full-displacement rule (absence costs the layout what presence would), the "same place, opposite structure" formulation that makes H9 legible, and a process convention worth making mandatory for every future mock and wireframe: the built-in small-size self-test strip — the whole board re-rendered at ~26–30% on the same page, so scale-survival claims are checked at authoring time instead of asserted.

**Print** supplies the register (§2.7), the section manifest, point-of-use captions, the conformance-floor rule, and two findings that changed the composite against other seats' choices: the empty-checkbox affordance (which removed empty boxes everywhere, including from print's own wall) and the provenance leak — fixture file names on the produced-from line resolving the very convergence fact the canvas refuses to distinguish. That second finding exits the renderer: it is a host-side requirement (opaque load labels in anything arcviz emits as provenance) and should be recorded as such in the interaction spec's host contract.

**Graph** supplies the frontier as a first-class object, the three-terminal grammar, the hole-egress fringe (shared honors with precedent, per Convergence 3), the ports that make "has edges / sealed / into the frontier" three states of one location, the working vocabulary, and the pile's refutation together with what replaces it. Its dial-position objection against itself is promoted to Daniel's decision Q1.

## 4. Decisions for Daniel

These are the points where proposals genuinely conflict, or where the charrette sharpened a question without earning an answer. I am not resolving them.

  [ Q-8Y3M ]  **Where does the scaffolding dial sit for holder-facing and first-contact renders?** The composite as specified is tuned to the verifier and the adversary: identity pills outrank human-legible names, cards read as instrument panels, and a first-time holder looking for "what does my credential say" reads past apparatus erected against an adversary they have never met. Graph names this as the likeliest reason to reject its own entry and states the honest boundary: nothing in the research fixes where the dial belongs — the premise fixes it. The options are a single register everywhere (maximal honesty, real first-contact cost, SC1 is where it bites) or a holder-facing register that promotes `a`-section content and demotes — never removes — the apparatus (better first contact; two registers to keep honest, and Rule 18 already forces one register split, so this would be a second axis). The evidence ends where this question begins; it shapes every wireframe, so it should be decided before that phase starts.

  [ Q-KP7T ]  **Does the verification ledger live on every card, or once per render inside the gate?** Three seats render per-card ledgers; print prints one aggregated wall inside the presenter frame (partly a sheet-economy choice, but the form exists and works). Per-card keeps state at the card it describes and is what the compact-chip floor assumes; it also multiplies linearly with subjects, which is exactly the SC13 stress. Centralized is sublinear and puts the whole wall behind the gate, at the cost of distance between a card and its own state — and a distance-channel for the habituated reader to stop crossing. The SC13 build (section 6) is designed to answer this empirically; the decision could be deferred to its results, but that deferral is itself the decision to make now.

  [ Q-4WDN ]  **What does a static export carry?** Unchanged from [threat-model.md:149](../../research/threat-model.md) but now the only open half of the export question: transferable re-verification material versus a record that proves nothing versus no export at all — "both are bad in different directions, and 'no export' is not obviously survivable in a real tool." The charrette's register work makes the labeling side as good as labeling can be, which sharpens rather than softens the residual: a beautifully honest worksheet is still only a worksheet. The threat model's named unblocking event stands — a demand test naming the actual re-verifier.

## 5. What the composite still fails

**Habituation (HP2) is not solved, and nobody should be credited with solving it.** All four seats declined to claim it; the composite inherits the decline. The steady state of every store-fed render is the all-unperformed wall, rendered honestly, calmly, and identically every time — which is precisely the stimulus the literature says people learn to stop seeing ([shape-catalog.md](../shape-catalog.md) HP2: "no research answer exists"). Graph's admission generalizes to the whole composite: the normalcy framing may *accelerate* habituation, and it was chosen anyway because alarm-framing the default state is the worse lie. The composite's honest claim is only that structure resists counterfeiting and survives channel loss — not that it produces comprehension.

The rest of the inherited-unfixed list: the **skeleton-loader misread** — a hatched or dark region at a node position lives in the visual neighborhood of loading placeholders, and every seat's mitigation is declarative text, which P9 says cannot be certified from the armchair. The **count-the-terminals residual** in the convergence composition (§2.2) — mitigated by bracket and sentence, structurally unfixed, possibly unfixable without lying in the other direction. **Prose is load-bearing at the sizes where it dies** (Convergence 4) — positionlessness, cardinality, and cause all ride text. **SC13 was never mocked** — the level-allocation rule's behavior under multiplication, the compact-chip legibility floor, and the frontier's sublinearity are all arguments awaiting a demonstration, and if the floor fails at mobile width, focus-in-place inherits a milder version of the pile's own disease. The **card-level deformation tier is unmocked** (no case in the charrette fires stale, H6, or H2-guessable). **Rule 16's real test cannot be run** — the corpus has no `r`-section fixture (gap G12), so the rules-band layout commitment rests on a sizing specimen. The **trained-reader problem** — the coined grammar has zero installed convention; point-of-use captions are the chosen compensation, and a print recipient still gets no tutorial. And the **AF1 interaction observables remain undemonstrated** in any interactive build — the pile refutation is drawn, but focus-in-place has never actually been operated.

## 6. The first thing to build, and what would falsify the composite early

**Build the SC13 stress board first**: 8–9 cards at mobile width (≈360 px), in the composite vocabulary, with the four budgeted states co-present, at least one hole, two blinded edges into the frontier, a seal, the gate, and per-card ledgers — plus the same board with the centralized ledger, for [Q-KP7T]. Static HTML with structure's self-test strip is enough; no build system, no interactivity beyond one focus-in-place state drawn at three moments (rest, mid-reflow, focused). This is the next artifact because it is where the composite's three most falsifiable claims meet: that the level-allocation rule survives multiplication (HP3), that the compact-chip floor can actually hold a pill, a port, and a budgeted marker at that width, and that hole-versus-frontier stays tellable when the hole is chip-sized (§2.1's recorded contingency).

Falsifiers, in order of how early and cheaply they can kill the composite: (1) **the floor fails** — if a compact chip cannot hold its identity and its budgeted marker above legibility at 360 px, focus-in-place loses its load-bearing property, the pile refutation's *alternative* collapses (the refutation itself stands), and the interaction model needs a different mechanism than compression — this is checkable in one afternoon of mock-building, and it should be checked before any wireframe assumes it; (2) **the levels compete** — if gate, holes, frontier, and a card deformation visibly fight for attention on one phone screen, the prominence spine is an artifact of three-case charrettes and HP3 is still open; (3) **hole reads as frontier at chip scale** — triggers the recorded slab fallback; (4) **the untrained read fails** — a small-n probe (five untrained readers, three questions: is this an error? is it loading? are these two arrows going to the same place?) against the SC6 triptych; the armchair cannot answer this (P9), the probe is cheap, and a majority "it's loading" or "two destinations" answer sends §2.1/§2.2 back to the drawing board rather than into the interaction spec. Alongside the board, one corpus chore unblocks a hole every seat worked around: a load recipe producing the o-less-edge-with-far-node-withheld state against the diamond family (print's admitted gap 2; graph manufactured it ad hoc), so Rule 9(b)'s "operator undetermined" is exhibited from a fixture instead of a liberty.

If the SC13 board survives its falsifiers, the wireframe-and-interaction-spec phase builds from sections 2.1–2.7 as written, with [Q-8Y3M], [Q-KP7T] and [Q-4WDN] decided by Daniel first.
