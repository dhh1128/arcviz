# The 320 px retest — results

> **Partly superseded, 2026-09-15.** Required changes 1, 2, 4 and 5 below have been carried out on a responsive rebuild of arm B — [../layout-comparison/RESPONSIVE.md](../layout-comparison/RESPONSIVE.md). That rebuild renders where this document reconstructs, and it corrects two figures here: the focus-register growth at 320 is +8.0%, not the +22–30% §3 predicts, and the 399–453 px range §3 gives for the rest register is not measurement uncertainty but an unmade decision about the packing rule (rendered: 379.8 px global, 495.8 px band). The §5 stale-banner overflow is confirmed as real but is stress-board geometry rather than a 320 px fact. Everything else here stands.

DD-2 fixes the binding minimum at 320 CSS pixels (WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.10 Reflow); every artifact in this repository was built and measured at 360. This directory holds the retest DD-2's "Consequence for work already done" paragraph called for: every claim re-measured at the width that actually binds. Three *Proposed* decisions — DD-4, DD-5, DD-6 — depend on the 360 numbers; the per-decision verdicts are below. Two of the 360 findings do not survive the retest, and one measured ordering reverses. That is the retest working, not a defect in it.

## Method, stated so the numbers can be audited

All measurements are from headless Chrome 153 on Linux. Chrome's new headless mode silently clamps window width to 500 px, so true 320/360 viewports were obtained by rendering each artifact inside a 320-wide (or 360-wide) iframe and measuring within it; element boxes via `getBoundingClientRect`, clipping via `scrollWidth − clientWidth`. Column reflow was measured by extracting every absolutely-positioned board element, re-rendering it in static flow at its authored width and at its 320-frame width (the 360→320 delta is 40 px: a 320 phone keeps the 5+5 px gate rails, so the board narrows 350→310 and a full-column element 288→248; fixed floor forms 76×44 and 150×72 keep their size by definition), then recomposing columns from measured natural heights plus the authored inter-row gaps, splitting any row whose members no longer fit the 310 board (first-fit, order preserved). **Instrument validation:** recomposing at the authored width reproduces the authored columns to within 0.1–1.3% (variant A 6,710 exactly; variant B 4,114 vs 4,110), and the whole-render and matrix figures published in [../stress-board/README.md](../stress-board/README.md) to within 2 px (6,885.9 vs 6,884; 4,859.7 vs 4,858; matrix 573.8 vs 573). The 360 document heights in [../shape-gallery/README.md](../shape-gallery/README.md) (5,304 / 4,214 / 2,347) were also reproduced exactly, offset by the 24 px body margin (5,328 / 4,238 / 2,371 measured on `documentElement`). Raw data: `/tmp` scratch was used for the harnesses; the numbers cited here are all in this file, and the captures in this directory are the visual record.

## 2 · Per-decision verdicts

### DD-4 — the pile is refuted, focus is in place: **the reversal condition is NOT met, but the standing caveat is triggered — the measurement that retired layering does not survive at 320**

DD-4's written reversal condition is "the compact floor proves illegible at 320 px." The floor form is fixed at 76×44 with 8 px pill type regardless of viewport, so nothing about legibility changes between 360 and 320 — and legibility was never measured at either width (it is the perceptual question P9 reserves for the small-n probe, per [../shape-gallery/README.md](../shape-gallery/README.md)). The retest cannot adjudicate that condition; mechanically, focus-in-place still works at 320: all thirteen objects stay co-present, the focused card keeps both parents attached, and the focus frame recomposes at 938 px (row-preserving) against 719 at 360 — about 1.65 phone screens at 320×568.

What the retest *does* change is DD-4's standing caveat, which records that Daniel's layering proposal "lost on measurement" — layering rendered "+34% taller at rest, +15% at focus" than compression at 360. **That ordering does not survive at 320.** Compression's rest register grows +24–41% (399–453 px, global-repack to rank-row-preserving bounds) because 76 px chips pack three across instead of four; layering's grows +0.5% (436 px) because full-width slivers are width-indifferent. The two arms land inside each other's error bands at both registers (rest: A 436 vs B 399–453; focus: A 895 vs B 875–938). The 360 height dominance is gone. What survives of the retirement case: layering holds its height only by *silently clipping content* (§4 — its `overflow:hidden` slivers discard 20 px of item 10's state words at the 248 run, where compression's chips never clip); the area-per-content dominance (one line per 4,464 px² sliver vs three per 3,344 px² chip) narrows but persists; and the partial-order/lane-apparatus objections are geometric and width-independent. The arm-A retirement therefore stands or falls on those arguments now, not on column height, and [../layout-comparison/README.md](../layout-comparison/README.md)'s recommendation needs re-arguing before DD-4's caveat can be considered discharged.

### DD-5 — compression to a floor, with a second floor: **both floors carry at 320 exactly what they carried at 360 (the forms are fixed), but the packing that priced the mechanism breaks, and the second floor's cost roughly doubles where budgeted cards cluster**

The 76×44 chip and the 150×72 mid form are fixed-size reflowed forms; at 320 they are pixel-identical to 360, so the chip carries identity plus one perimeter state and the mid form carries its policy kind and value ("STALE · PT72H exceeded") exactly as before. Whether either is *legible* remains unprobed — the coordinates are design choices, not measured floors, as [../shape-gallery/README.md](../shape-gallery/README.md) already records, and this retest adds no perceptual evidence.

What breaks is the geometry around them, measured ([packing-evidence-320.png](packing-evidence-320.png)): a 320 phone's board offers at most 288 px of run after the frontier gutter (274 after the arm-B right margin). Four chips need 328 px — **4-across dies; the packing is 3-across**, and the marginal column cost per floored object rises from ≈15.5 to ≈20.7 px (+34%). **Two mid forms need 308 px and can never share a row at 320** (a mid plus a chip fits: 234 px), so on the eleven-item load arm C's rest register grows 578→736 px (+27%) and its focus register 1,057→1,189 px (+13%); where budgeted carriers are the majority, the per-carrier cost roughly doubles against the 360 figure of ≈41 px. The stress board's frame-1 register (150-wide chips two-across) breaks the same way: 308 px needed, 280 available; 556→638 px restacked. DD-5's mechanism survives at 320; its price list does not — every derived area/cost figure in the arms' README is 360-specific and must be restated at 3-across/1-mid-per-row packing before DD-5 is ratified. The second floor's whole justification — parameters survive off-focus — is untouched, because it never depended on row-sharing.

### DD-6 — verification per card, not a central ledger: **the recommendation survives; the height economics are essentially unchanged (saving 29.4% → 30.2%), so the written reversal condition is not met**

Re-measured on the reconstructed pair at the 320 frame: variant A's board 6,710→7,264 px (+8.3%), variant B's 4,110→4,418 px (+7.5%), the matrix 574→597 px (+4%, footnote pills wrap), gate chrome unchanged (measured 141.9 + 34 px at both widths). Whole renders: **A 7,440 px, B 5,191 px — centralizing saves 30.2% at 320** against 29.4% at 360. The saving neither grows decisive nor collapses; both columns inflate together because the inflation is text reflow in the cards both variants share. The qualitative costs that decided the recommendation are width-independent and unchanged: blocking identifiers exiled to footnotes a–f, the three-hop read, and zero co-visibility once the matrix scrolls off. Two erosions worth recording: the matrix at 310 px width is 597 px tall, so "all 101 cells in under one phone screen" now fails on the shortest common 320-wide viewport (568 px), and its per-item columns narrow to ≈20.9 px (still sufficient for one glyph plus a superscript). And the scroll-occlusion wound (stress-board falsifier 2) deepens proportionally: variant A at 320 is ≈13 phone screens of 320×568, against "over ten" at 360 — at the WCAG-binding 400%-zoom desktop case (a 320×256 viewport) every register is many screens and every co-visibility argument weakens further.

Arm B ranges: the lower bound flows all thirteen objects 3-across under the design's own "row packing is viewport economy, not topology" rule; the upper bound splits only within authored rank rows. **The ordering the recommendation rests on changes:** at 360 it was compression 327 < layering 434 < DOI 578 at rest (decisive, −25% for compression); at 320 it is layering 436 ≈ compression 399–453 < DOI 736 — the compression-vs-layering ordering is no longer decisive in either direction, and layering's stability is purchased by clipping (§4). DOI remains clearly tallest at both widths, so arm C's position is unaffected. The full-document heights that [../shape-gallery/README.md](../shape-gallery/README.md) used to corroborate the 360 ordering (5,304 / 4,214 / 2,347) remain in the same order at a 320 viewport (5,473 / 4,528 / 2,533 measured, minus the 24 px margin convention), but at 320 that measurement is dominated by commentary reflow, not the renders, and should not be cited for the design again.

## 4 · The 288 px sliver at 320 (arm A; feeds the perimeter-glyph case)

At 320 the sliver run is 248 px, not 288. Measured on reconstructed slivers with arm A's own CSS ([sliver-288-vs-248.png](sliver-288-vs-248.png)):

- Perimeter-class states all survive, unchanged: the stale frame-break, the hole's double border, and item 10's corner ticks are width-independent geometry. The finding that perimeter states are what survives extreme vertical compression is *strengthened* at 320, because the word-carried states are exactly what dies next.
- The stale policy sentence survives verbatim: 186–190 px at 7 px type against a ≈245 px row. The sliver's one genuine advantage over the 76 px chip holds at 320 for this state.
- Item 09's abbreviated cardinality line ("committed dest.: 1–2 · coincidence unknowable") needs 242.7 px and clips by ≈1 px at 248 — at the exact limit as authored; any longer port set or tag pushes it under. It fit with 36 px to spare at 288.
- Item 10's H6 sliver needs 263.1 px and **clips 20 px at 248: "possibly a metadata ACDC" truncates mid-word**, silently, because `.slvh6` is `overflow:hidden`. The only carrier of item 10's budgeted state in arm A's rest register is partially discarded at the binding width.
- The full cardinality sentence still does not fit anywhere: the board's cap sentence measures 535.5 px at 6 px type (first clause alone 206.9 px). **Provenance flag:** the "352 px" figure in [../layout-comparison/README.md](../layout-comparison/README.md) could not be reproduced against any candidate string at 6 or 7 px; the substantive claim (does not fit even a 288 run) holds regardless, but the number should be re-derived or re-attributed before it is cited again.

Net: the sliver-run finding that fed the case for perimeter-class floor glyphs for H6/H2 survives at 320 in sharpened form — words die (by clipping here, by shedding on the chip), geometry survives, and the glyphs remain undesigned.

## 5 · A finding the 360 artifacts could not show: the stale banner overflows the 320 card

The full stale banner sentence ("STALE — as-of 2026-06-14 · age 92 d exceeds policy PT72H") measures 240.5 px at its 8.5 px card-scale type. A 248-wide expanded card offers ≈230 px of text row after padding, so the banner's flex lines collapse and the text clips ≈3 px past the border — measured on three cards in each stress-board variant and on arm A's focused card ([packing-evidence-320.png](packing-evidence-320.png), exhibit D). Every expanded-scale stale banner needs a defined shorter form (or a wrap rule) at 320; nothing in the current vocabulary specifies one.

## What must change before DD-4, DD-5 and DD-6 can be ratified

1. **Rebuild the gauge at the binding width.** Every phone frame, board, and the shape gallery's panels are 360-committed; a 320 frame (board 310, column 248) is the certification surface. Until at least one artifact exists at 320, DD-2's own consequence paragraph applies to everything here, this directory included — these are reconstructions, not renders.
2. **Restate the packing rules and every derived cost figure.** 3-across chips (marginal ≈20.7 px/object, not 15.5), mid forms one per row or paired with a chip (never two mids), and the arms' README tables re-derived at those packings. DD-5's price at 320 is materially higher than the number it currently circulates with.
3. **Re-argue the arm A retirement on its surviving grounds.** The height measurement that killed it reverses/ties at 320; the retirement must rest on the partial-order geometry, the lane apparatus, area-per-content, and the clipping behaviour §4 documents — or the question reopens. DD-4's standing caveat says exactly this would matter; it now does.
4. **Design the perimeter-class floor glyphs for H6 and H2.** At 320 the word-carriers for items 10 and 11 clip or shed at every compressed tier; the glyphs are now load-bearing for DD-5's floors, not an improvement.
5. **Define the stale banner's 248 px form** (§5), and give item 09's abbreviated cardinality line ≥2 px of slack or a shorter form.
6. **Fix the two files that fail even at 360** — focus-sequence's gauge row (scrollWidth 780) and arm A's gauge commentary (398) — when the artifacts are next touched; they are apparatus, but they make the files' own width claims false as documents.
7. **Correct the gallery's width-specific captions in its 320 variant** (panel 5's "four chips fit the width; a fifth wraps" is three-and-a-fourth at 320) and re-derive or re-attribute the 352 px figure (§4).
8. **Run the small-n legibility probe on 320-frame renders**, since it is the only instrument that can discharge DD-4's actual reversal condition and DD-5's floor coordinates; nothing in this retest substitutes for it.
