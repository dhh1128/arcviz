# What a credential's descriptor should say

**Read this first if you are picking up the descriptor question, or carrying it into a general arcviz design session.** It supersedes [description-evaluation-set.md](description-evaluation-set.md), which was written early on 2026-09-23 and describes a position that has since been corrected twice.

**Status.** §1 is Daniel Hardman's, settled 2026-09-23 and 2026-09-24, quoted rather than paraphrased. §2 is mechanism that exists and is tested. §3 is what remains inside this question. §4 is what belongs to the general charter and should not be answered here. Everything not marked as his is synthesized: recorded so the next session does not re-derive it, not so it can be cited as decided.

## 1 · What was settled, in his words

**The goal.** *Let the viewer say what part this credential plays in the argument the DAG is making.* Not what kind of thing it is in the abstract — the nine categories of [credential-categories.md](credential-categories.md) answer that, and are to be carried by colour and a watermark. Not which one it is — the SAID answers that, as a reference handle. What work it does **here**. The test is whether removing the node would let a viewer say what the argument lost.

**The scope.** *"I can't just look at one acdc and say, what should be its description? I have to look at that acdc as part of a larger corpus of evidence and say, what is it about this credential, in a larger corpus, that would help a person make sense of its intended role in the corpus at first glance?"* So the descriptor is a function of (credential, DAG), not of the credential — it cannot be precomputed, cached, or stored on the credential.

**The criterion.** *"The goal is to figure out which out of all possible useful data is the datum that is most helpful, in the context of this DAG."* Not "surface as much useful info as possible". A datum earns its place by **surprisal** — how much it tells a viewer that they could not have predicted from the type plus what is already shown. Two earlier objectives were wrong in opposite directions: minimal-distinguishing measured only discrimination and stopped at four bare type names on the vLEI chain; role-first measured only availability and emitted whatever was present regardless of whether the type already implied it.

**`ITEM NN` is deleted, not demoted.** *"I think the SAID of a credential was doing the same job."* It is: unique, present on every ACDC, and globally stable across renders, sessions and people, which a render-local number is not. A number also asserts an order the DAG does not have. One distinction rides along — displaying an identifier as a reference token is not the same act as wrapping it in a recognition-and-comparison ceremony. The SAID keeps the display and loses the ceremony; the AIDs get the ceremony.

**Identification is not evaluation.** *"Naming an issuer isn't supposed to be a reputation signal at all. Reputation should be signalled by something else entirely… Knowing that a witness testified to fact X in court, and knowing that witness X is trustworthy, are radically different questions."* So the name slot carries identity only, and every assessment rides in its own channel. A court transcript names perjurers and endorses none of them; withholding a name is strictly worse, because a viewer can no longer apply what they already know.

**Trust is made one identifier and one ACDC at a time.** Not corpus-wide, and not one value. At least three judgements are independent and must never be merged: the alias creator's COIA flags about an identifier; the host's confidence in the **identifier-to-alias binding**; and the host's **evidentiary stance** on a credential. His example is the one to remember — Provenant might be highly confident that an AID is controlled by a diploma mill, so the binding is strongly trusted, while having no intention of crediting the diploma.

**arcviz may compute and must not adjudicate.** *"The host application, never arcviz, knows something about that corpus. This is the same source that can look up AID values and return aliases. It can also decide whether those aliases are verified or trustworthy. Arcviz can't."* Recomputing a SAID is computation — a digest matches or it does not. Whether a set of values is single-origin, whether an alias names who it claims to, whether a directory is authoritative: all adjudication, all host-supplied. arcviz renders each claim with its source attached and never originates one.

**Abbreviation must be unambiguous in the domain.** `authorization` shortens to `authz`, never `auth`, because `authentication` is equally common here.

## 2 · What exists and is tested

In `tools/describe/`, which is **a prototype and an executable specification, not arcviz's implementation** — arcviz's renderer is React and there is no TypeScript to put this in. `vectors.json` is the durable, language-neutral artifact; the Python proves the vectors are satisfiable. If they disagree, the vectors win.

**The algorithm** is a minimal distinguishing description tightened by surprisal. A component is added when it says something the viewer could not predict: channels the type *entails* contribute nothing, channels constant across the DAG contribute nothing to discrimination, and a relative channel (a date band) is shown only where the node deviates from the majority — announce the deviation, not the default, which is the rule `credential-categories.md` already chose for its `alignment` axis. Every component carries its surprisal in bits, so a render with room for three lines drops the weakest rather than truncating whichever came last. When the channels are exhausted and nodes remain identical, the answer is *these are indistinguishable* — the petition case, where a label that looked like an answer would be the worst available failure.

**Entailment comes from the schema**, which is why schema resolution had to exist first. The real Legal Entity vLEI schema documents its `i` as "QVI Issuer AID" and its `a.i` as "LE Issuer AID", and carries `required: ['d','qvi']` with a `const` pinning that edge's target — so both the party relation and the edge label are fixed by the type, and naming either restates it in longer words. Note the party relation *varies* across the vLEI chain and is still worthless: it is determined in the world, not in the presentation, which no amount of looking at the DAG could reveal. The exception is the interesting half — a schema can entail that the issuee is a legal entity and cannot entail *which*, so an entailed party channel returns the moment the host can name either end.

**Schema resolution** (`schemas.py`, `refs/schema-registry.json`) has four states and no silent fallback. The registry is a hint, not a trust anchor: a schema is content-addressed, so a hostile source cannot substitute a different document — the worst it can do is decline to answer, or learn that somebody asked. Resolution requests nothing unless a caller passes a fetcher, because asking is itself a disclosure. `unverified` may lend a display name and may **not** be relied on for entailments, since acting on an entailment *suppresses* a component and a wrong one makes the label say less than it should. `mismatch` is a security event, never cached and never fallen back from.

**COIA consumption** (`coia_reader.py`) reads aliases and never mints one; §1 of that spec forbids parsing someone else's alias for meaning. All eleven normative parse vectors pass on their flag groups; §5 normalization is not implemented and not claimed. COIA §6.3 states this project's own thesis in someone else's specification — *"Absence is never a guarantee… An application MUST NOT render an absent flag as a positive assurance"* — so an unflagged alias is never reported as verified.

**The pill.** The host-settable slot is `label`, and its precedence is `label` → the gated mnemonic → the type text. Pass `None` and never `""`: an empty string is still a label, wins the precedence with nothing in it, and suppresses the type text. Flags never go in the label, which is entviz's *trusted* slot. Note the mnemonic — the value-derived `first…middle…last` fragment that reads as the identifier — is gated on the `corpus` trust posture, so what a deployment gets when no alias is known is a posture decision, and the posture is the host's.

**The channels, graded by provenance.** Issuer AID (universal, and blind at exactly the collision that started the question). Issuee AID (structural, present on 9 of 29 corpus ACDCs — not a coverage gap but a measurement of how often the subject is not a party). Issuance date, the strongest text channel: present on 5 of 5 ACDCs in the live VVP dossier, bound into the SAID, and it separates pre-existing background from material assembled *for* this claim. A thumbnail, which is not a description but evidence rendered directly — so the image carries the subject and the text is freed to carry the provenance. A suggested filename, uniquely valuable as the only field in the stack written by a human for a human, and weakest evidentially — render it as untrusted input, with the extension from sniffed content and bidi controls stripped. Attachment size, a last-resort tiebreaker as a human magnitude only. The referring edge label, which carries role and not instance. And, from a **field-role map** supplied per schema, a party named in a field, a locus, and a collection time distinct from issuance — the three the crime-scene case needs.

**The instrument.** `corpus/accident_*` is a nine-node bundle: three matched pairs colliding on schema, each separated by a different channel, plus all three image states and four subject kinds. Its photographs collide even on VIN and plate, so only what the image depicts separates them. The two vehicle photographs corroborate each other through their images alone — maroon paint transfer on the yellow pickup — a relationship no attribute states and no edge draws.

## 3 · What is left inside this question

Vector breadth. Twenty-six tests and 27 vectors is a skeleton; Daniel expected hundreds, and the seven unbuilt bundles of [description-evaluation-set.md](description-evaluation-set.md) §4 are where they come from. The petition alone should generate dozens, and it is the one case nothing in the design can currently express — at five hundred identical endorsements the honest answer describes a population rather than its members.

Metadata channels for non-image attachments. Measured: of six real office documents, **zero** carried an embedded thumbnail, while four carried a human-authored title or author plus slide, page and word counts one unzip away. Of nine PDFs, five carried a `/Title`. So the cheap universal win for non-image attachments is metadata, not pictures — and it is text, so it survives the floor form and a screen reader.

The unsolved dominance problem. Nothing in a JSON Schema says which field is the payload's *purpose*, which is why the field-role map is an input rather than a derivation. `credential-categories.md` measured the cost: per-label recall 0.94 against precision 0.77.

## 4 · What belongs to the general charter

Named here so a descriptor session does not spend itself on them. How a category is conveyed by colour and watermark, which is already a separate commission. The reputation channel that §1 requires and does not design — a border, a check against a cross. Thumbnail rendering and how the three image states look. Progressive disclosure and the tier budget that the surprisal ranking feeds. What `unknown` looks like against `ordinary`, which `credential-categories.md` calls the highest-stakes rendering question the scheme raises. And the goal **set**, which [posture.md](posture.md) §3 still records as unratified — this document settles the goal of one element, not the set.
