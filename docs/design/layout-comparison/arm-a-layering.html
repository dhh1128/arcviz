Arm A — layering: occluded cards keep a visible sliver

Mechanics under test: each connected component is a pile; an occluded card shows exactly its top edge — a full-width strip tall enough for one line — and the focused card renders whole where it stands in the pile. Every edge is drawn in the left lanes and attaches to visible sliver geometry, never to hidden geometry, which is what survives of the original model once the charrette's occlusion objection is answered. Pile adjacency itself carries no meaning; the render's own cap says so. Measurements and the seven-question table: README.md.

The sliver floor is 18 px: an 8 px pill (gauge A's legibility minimum, unchanged), a 6.5 px tag, 1.5 px of top border, and one line of run-room. Below ~16 px the pill dies and the sliver carries nothing. A perimeter state raises the floor to 25 px — the frame-break needs its own row — and a hole's double border to 21 px.

What fits: identity with its ×N mark; exactly one budgeted state, if it is perimeter-class (the stale break; the hole's double border; item 10's two top corner ticks — the only fragment of the withdrawn frame a top strip can show); an e-slot glyph relocated inline; and — the sliver's one genuine advantage — 288 px of run-room, so the stale policy sentence survives verbatim at 7 px where the 76-px-wide floor chip sheds it to the bare word. Item 09's cardinality bound also survives inline, abbreviated, at six-point ("committed dest.: 1–2 · coincidence unknowable") — the full sentence measured 352 px of run and did not fit even here.

What is shed first: everything below line one (ledger, manifest, a-content, T detail) — immediately and by construction; then the port strip's place-grammar: the seal survives only as an inline glyph, no longer at the place edges depart, so "same place, opposite structure" is lost; then the area-class deformations — item 11's transparent a-region has no area to spend and is words-only.

The cost accounting that kills the arm: a plain sliver is 288×18 = 5,184 px², a floor chip 76×44 = 3,344 px². The sliver spends 1.55× the area to carry one line against the chip's three. Layering does not buy density with occlusion; at equal legibility it pays more per object than reflow does, because a card's width is indivisible while a chip's is not.

COMMENTARY — OUTSIDE THE RENDER

Host stubs identical to the stress board and to arms B and C; load manifest in ../stress-board/README.md.

Linearization, the arm's second wound. A pile is a total order; the diamond is a partial one. Slivers 05, 06, 07 sit in some stacked sequence, and no stacking of them depicts "05 and 06 are siblings, 07 is below 06 only" — the two path lengths exist solely in the lane edges, and the render's cap must actively disclaim the adjacency the geometry can't help asserting. The five diamond edges needed four distinct lanes in a 30 px margin; lane count grows with rank-skipping edges, and the lane margin competes for the same left edge the frontier gutter owns. The charrette refuted the pile on occluded attachment; slivers answer that and then lose on this — a pile can show a DAG's cards, but its geometry keeps asserting a chain.

Determinism: pile membership is the component; stacking order is rank (longest committed path), ties by SAID lexicographically; lane assignment is by edge span, longest outermost. Pure function of (presentation, stubs, focus SAID); byte-identical on identical input.

Unknowable distance: a pile position is a rank, so the same domain restriction as arm B applies — the hole stacks at the rank its disclosed in-edge gives it; a blinded edge's destination never joins a pile (it has no rank to stack at) and dies on the frontier boundary as everywhere else; the frontier is not at any place and is never stacked, sized, or reordered by focus.

Print: the rest frame is the print form — an index of one-line entries. Every parameter that lives below line one is unreachable on paper, so a print artifact needs accompanying expanded sheets exactly as arm B does, except that the index page carries less than arm B's rest board does (no second and third chip lines). Nothing here depends on hover, animation, or focus to exist.
