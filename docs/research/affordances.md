## (a) Vocabulary

### Affordance and signifier, in Norman's own words

The two terms are widely used as synonyms, and the inventory is worthless if it does so. Norman's 2013 statement of the distinction, quoted rather than paraphrased ([norman_jnd_preface_doet2013], via [prior-art/literature.md](prior-art/literature.md) cluster 4): "Affordances define what actions are possible. Signifiers specify how people discover those possibilities: signifiers are signs, perceptible signals of what can be done. [...] Signifiers are of far more importance to designers than are affordances." And on his own earlier imprecision: "I should have used the term 'perceived affordance,' for in design, we care much more about what the user perceives than what is actually true" ([norman_affordances_and_design]). So in every row below, the *affordance* column names what is actually possible, and the *signifier* column names the perceivable cue — stated as a constraint on the cue, not a pixel spec, since design is Phase 6's job. The framework catches mismatches in both directions, and this domain produces both: a signifier with no affordance (a blinded H3 block that looks clickable-to-reveal — the exact example [prior-art/literature.md](prior-art/literature.md) cluster 4 constructs) and an affordance with no signifier (raw bytes reachable but no visible route). One more Norman point is load-bearing for this domain: absence can itself signify — "even for items that signify by their absence, as the lack of crowds on a train platform" ([norman2008signifiers]) — *provided* the absence is unambiguous. The inventory uses absence-as-signifier in exactly two places, and distinguishes them: a hole in the DAG (AF7), where the absence must not read as loading or error (H3's naive lie), and the holder-facing view's missing presenter indicator (AF17), where absence is safe because the question is foreclosed rather than unanswered (Rule 18).

### Intent boundaries and treatments

The definition, quoted from the essay (`~/code/me/papers/intent-boundaries.md:67`): "An intent boundary is a place where what is known about an intender's intent by an external party becomes inadequate. It is a boundary to the external party, because it prevents them from confidently characterizing the intent on the other side." The four principles are recognize boundaries; consider moving rather than crossing; consider peeking across; never sneak across (`intent-boundaries.md:75-127`; canonical citation `intent-monograph.md` per [prior-art/papers-intent.md](prior-art/papers-intent.md) §(a), with three essay-only clauses that bear here: the corollary against invented boundaries, the multi-stakeholder disclosure clause, and the audit-trail clause). The treatment column below uses five values:

- **none** — no boundary sits here, and per the corollary the inventory *refuses to invent one*: "Don't imagine boundaries where they don't actually exist" ([intent-boundaries-dont-imagine], `intent-boundaries.md:84`). Confirmation on a boundary-free act is friction with no safety.
- **move** — better signification collapses the boundary before it is reached (the red *Watch* button, `intent-boundaries.md:97-101`).
- **peek** — proceed on the default guess with stakes lowered: "Lowering the stakes for a decision until you organically reach confidence about the user's intent — by restricting its scope or making it easy to undo — is a way to peek across boundaries" (`intent-boundaries.md:112`).
- **confirm** — recognize the boundary and gate the crossing, with the gate worded per [hardman-sda-gate] as naming what the act does, never as suspicion of the actor.
- **explicit act** — the never-sneak cases: if the act is offered at all it is named, distinctly styled, and leaves a trace, because "preserving no audit trail when a boundary is crossed" is itself a form of sneaking ([hardman-intent-boundaries-logs]).

Boundaries are relative to an observer's ignorance, not fixed in the code ([hardman-intent-monograph-def]), and arcviz has at least three observers — the party operating the viewer, the holder who presented, and the subject the credential is about ([hardman-intent-boundaries-relative]; [prior-art/papers-intent.md](prior-art/papers-intent.md) §(f)2) — so several rows below carry different answers per viewer role. The roles are axis P's states plus the historical third case: **holder-facing** (P = self), **verifier-facing** (P = attested/unknown), and **historical** (a static export or replayed record, [presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(d)). The role is a host declaration under P16, with verifier-facing-with-P-unknown the fail-safe reading where undeclared (**ours**, extending §B6's weaker-reading pattern to this input).

### Typing the act: what this inventory adopts from the papers stream, and what it does not

[prior-art/papers-intent.md](prior-art/papers-intent.md) §(b) proposed, as its own inference, a composite: Syntelos types the *encompassing interaction* the viewer serves, and `sda.md`'s (effect × state-kind) coordinates type the *affordance itself*, because Syntelos's refusal test excludes deterministic tool acts by Hardman's own application of it ("The acts a delegation most needs to authorize or forbid [...] are exactly the tool-manipulation acts the refusal test excludes," [hardman-sda-syntelos-limit]). Adjudicated here rather than adopted whole (**ours**):

- **The (effect × state-kind) half is adopted.** It earned its place empirically in the stream's own draft table: the rows whose coordinate came out `create` rather than `observe` — fetch, cross-presentation join, export, persist — are precisely the rows that carry real boundaries, and the two rows serving no interaction the holder entered are the two most dangerous ([prior-art/papers-intent.md](prior-art/papers-intent.md) §(e), closing observations). A coordinate that predicts where the boundaries fall is doing work. Each row below carries it.
- **The Syntelos half is adopted as a scope claim, not as a column.** The claim that Syntelos types the interaction rather than the affordance is sound and is Hardman's own ([hardman-syntelos-scope], [hardman-sda-syntelos-limit]). But as a per-row assignment it was nearly constant across the stream's draft (`/govern/identify` or `/govern/audit` for all but two rows), the assignments are unfalsifiable at the affordance grain — the same click serves any encompassing telos — and no traced transport carries a typed interaction context a renderer could consume ([keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md) §(d) supplies nothing of the kind). The one discriminating output the column produced — *telos = none* marking arcviz's own acts — is carried directly instead, as the "whose act" annotation on AF14 and the retention question. The encompassing telos survives as a possible future P16 host input (a declared room change is a consent-relevant signal, per the stream's own corollary on [hardman-syntelos-consent-test]), with absent-semantics "undeclared" — but no row below depends on it, and it is not adopted into the interface until a row does.

### The prominence budget, restated as this inventory's ground rule

T2 ([principles.md](principles.md) Tensions) reserves P9's structural budget for the states whose naive lie is specifically "reads as fine": **stale** (§B4), **P = unknown in a verifier-facing view** (Rule 18), **the metadata-decoy state when Rule 12's conditioned trigger fires**, and **H2-guessable** (Rule 8). The consequence for an affordance inventory, stated once here and accounted row-by-row in §(e): *affordance signifiers are quiet by default*. Chevrons, hover highlights, copy buttons, toggles, and elevation are conventional and visually subordinate, precisely so that the four budgeted states — which are renderings of danger, not invitations to act — remain the only structurally loud things on the surface. An inventory that made every affordance prominent would have spent the budget on furniture (T2's own inversion test).

## (b) The commissioning sketch, adjudicated

Seven proposals from Daniel's original brief, each with a verdict — **sound**, **sound with constraints**, or **refuted** — and the evidence. Where a constraint binds only one viewer role or one substrate regime, that scope is stated, per P14's own instruction to ask which regime an affordance is in rather than assume.

**1. "Each credential renders as a card; the DAG renders as a tree of cards." Verdict: sound with constraints on the card; refuted on the tree.** The card unit is sound once corrected as the brief itself anticipated: the unit is the credential (the top-level ACDC), not the AID — H's addressable-unit definition (§a) makes the ACDC the natural card and its sections the card's internal units, while an AID is an identifier appearing *within* cards and renders as a pill (P8). Two constraints on the card. First, card chrome is itself a claim: a metadata ACDC wearing issued-credential chrome is Rule 12's named lie, so "credential-shaped" must be earnable, not automatic (P1). Second, a card design must leave room for the whole-card structural treatments the budget reserves (stale, P-unknown) — P9 forbids delegating those to a corner badge. The tree is refuted on topology: the real shape is a DAG with diamonds and depth — the corpus builds exactly this case (`diamond_depth_*`, five fixtures, a shared ancestor reached by paths of different length; [corpus/README.md](../../corpus/README.md)) and [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) §2C ordered it built because it breaks tree layouts. A tree render must either duplicate the shared ancestor (drawing one committed artifact as two nodes, misstating the graph the SAIDs actually commit to) or drop an edge (H8's naive lie — "a DAG with a committed-but-blinded edge has different topology than a DAG without it"). And the graph has *holes* that a tree walk trims: an H7 missing referent renders as an explicitly-unresolvable node, never as a shorter chain (Rule 10 — the production vLEI chain has exactly this hole, [corpus-vlei-chain.md](corpus-vlei-chain.md) §3). The renderable object is a DAG of cards with first-class holes. Node-link remains the right family for the central task: edge-following is a path task, and node-link beats matrices for it at the small, sparse scale real chains have ([ghoniem2005readability], with that paper's own scale caveat).

**2. "Clicking a card brings it to the top of the pile." Verdict: sound with constraints.** The act is local view manipulation of material in hand — free and leak-free on the traced substrate (P14; [keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md) §(e)) — and no intent boundary sits on it, so no confirmation may be invented (`intent-boundaries.md:84`). Three constraints (**ours**, each derived from a cited rule): elevation is a focus cue and must carry no trust or verdict semantics — z-order is not a ranking; a raised card must not occlude a sibling's budgeted state marker into invisibility, because an occluded stale marker is a removed indicator, and a removed indicator goes unnoticed by 92% of users even in a banking task ([schechter2007emperor]); and raising a card must not detach it from its edges — in a diamond, the raised card is still the child of two visible parents, or the pile has silently redrawn the topology proposal 1's verdict just forbade. How a pile and a topology-honest DAG view coexist is a genuine layout problem, logged for design in §(f).

**3. "Edges are colour-coded (or shape-coded)." Verdict: sound with constraints; colour-alone refuted, and one whole class of coding forbidden.** P9's capacity constraint puts security-relevant bits on discrete symbols, never on an analog channel an adversary can hide inside ([amp-diff-principle-5]); P10 makes the shipping rule explicit: colour plus shape plus label, or it does not ship — print may be monochrome, hover does not exist on paper or touch, colour-vision deficiency is common. Any palette is spaced by CIELAB lightness, never ΔE76 ([amp-diff-de76-trap]). What *must* be coded distinctly: the three edge classes of Rule 14 — disclosed-here, disclosed-elsewhere, inferred-by-equality — which must never share a stroke; the blinded edge (H8) and the operator-undetermined state (Rule 9(b)); and the derived effective operator where determined, marked as derived. What must *not* be coded at all: default-versus-explicit `I2I` when the far node is in hand and targeted — pair 2's resolution is opposite to every other pair, the renderer *suppresses* the syntactic difference, and "specified vs unspecified must never become a badge" (Rule 9(a)). T2 names this exact inversion: spending structure on the syntactic presence of an edge operator is the budget spent backwards.

**4. "Hovering an edge lights up the edge and the outline of the corresponding card in the pile." Verdict: sound with constraints.** As an enhancement it is sound and cheap: hover is free on this substrate because everything hover could reveal is already in hand (P14), and it serves a real need — disambiguating which cards an edge connects when edges cross. Two constraints. Hover must trigger no network act, ever — hover-to-fetch is Rule 17's named violation and A5's leak. And no distinction may exist *only* on hover (P10's explicit forbid): touch and print have no hover, so anything hover reveals must be reachable persistently (tap, selection, the printed legend). A third, smaller one (**ours**): the highlight styling must not read as a verdict — a glowing green outline on hover would counterfeit exactly the chrome P11's frame discipline protects.

**5. "Ugly fields render as entviz pills, labelled with a COIA alias where one resolves." Verdict: sound with constraints — and the constraints are the two principles the project already holds.** The pill is P8's own rule: every SAID and AID renders as a pill so an honest substitution disturbs a picture rather than hiding in a string nobody reads. The constraints: the pill rules out and never rules in — the one-glance read carries low-to-mid-teens of bits ([mglance2026]) and casual comparison against a ground-offline adversary "is broken" ([amp-diff], `amp-diff.md:324`) — so the pill must never imply a comparison ceremony happened, must keep the real ceremony one gesture away (AF10), and must never wear verified/current-badge connotations (§B3 check 1's naive lie; a SAID is content identity only, [acdc-mtc-said-vs-inclusion-summary]). An AGID never gets a SAID pill (Rule 7 — it is a different commitment object, `v1.1:672`). And the alias half is governed by P12: an alias is a private nickname, not evidence; its COIA flags render visibly and are never dropped; an unflagged alias is never treated as verified; and the display must reckon with the finding that a cryptographic-looking *form* is itself misread as a security mechanism ([korir2022wallet]) — the raw string's appearance already generates unearned trust before arcviz adds anything.

**6. "A raw view showing actual field values and a pretty view showing localised dates and times." Verdict: sound with constraints — and the raw view is upgraded from a feature to an obligation.** The corpus requires a route from any rendered element back to the bytes it came from: a rendering is a lossy derivation ("a JPEG of a JPEG," [hardman-proga-derivative]), Rule 9 makes the default-vs-explicit operator difference visible *only* in a raw view, and Rule 19's reserved-label states are parse-level facts about bytes. Constraints on the toggle: disclosure-state and verification markers are identical in both views — the toggle switches value formatting, never epistemic state, because deferring "this is blinded" or "this is unperformed" to a view the user must navigate to misuses progressive disclosure, which is for complexity, not for system status ([nngroup_progressive_disclosure]; [nielsen1994heuristics] heuristic 1). Pretty values remain issuer content rendered as text (P11; [hardman-acp-readtext]); a localised date is a viewer-side derivation of the committed `dt` and must not silently replace it without the raw route; and a pretty view must never repair what the raw view shows broken — a `-`-bearing label renders as a parse error in both (Rule 19).

**7. "Must work on mobile, desktop, and in print." Verdict: sound — and strengthened by the corpus rather than merely permitted.** P10 makes the three-medium requirement and adversarial robustness one move: the redundant discrete encoding that survives greyscale print and touch is the same property that denies a grinding adversary an analog margin ([amp-diff-accessibility]). But print is not just a layout target; it is a different *epistemic medium* with its own obligations: every V and P indicator in static output renders as a historical record, never live state (Rule 15 — the render-side answer to the mDL "flash pass" the AAMVA guidelines prohibit, [presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(e)); terms of use travel with the export (Rule 16); and the static render's overall authority must be visibly less than the live view's (P15). "Works in print" therefore means "degrades honestly in print," which is a stronger requirement than the sketch stated — and one no audited ecosystem meets, since none designed for the moment the verification channel and the rendering channel separate ([prior-art/wallets.md](prior-art/wallets.md) §5, via P15).

## (d) Notes on three contested rows

**AF14 (join) was retired by scope, which is the strongest form the answer could have taken.** The corpus had twice declined to decide whether arcviz should offer cross-presentation joining, and this document declined a third time, specifying only the admissible shape. Asked directly on 2026-09-11, Daniel Hardman answered that he never wanted it: arcviz renders one loaded presentation at a time, and correlation highlighting is *within* that presentation. That does not merely forbid the act — it removes the conditions under which the act could occur, so [hardman-if-owner]'s rule that linking facets is "never a default, never an inference a stranger is meant to draw" is satisfied by construction rather than by discipline. A determined verifier can still join by hand, outside arcviz, which is A8's accepted residual and not a thing the component condones.

**AF15's obligation is bounded by what the literature says any indicator can achieve.** The row obliges arcviz to make the cost of disclosure *visible*; it forbids claiming the cost is *understood*. A professional team applying warning-design best practice moved adherence and "ultimately failed at our goal of a well-understood warning" ([felt2015sslwarnings]); interruption changes behavior without guaranteeing comprehension ([akhawe2013warningland]). So the pre-presentation view is specified as evidence-rendering, and the parked question 7 — whether to add an interruptive treatment — is a question about adherence, to be answered inside the prominence budget, not a question about making the holder understand.

**AF16 is specified only on its honest half.** Rule 15 (what the export says) is testable now; the payload question (what it carries) is open question 10, parked pending a demand test, and A2's residual — "Both are bad in different directions, and 'no export' is not obviously survivable in a real tool" ([prior-art/papers-intent.md](prior-art/papers-intent.md) §(g) Q5) — is quoted here so no designer mistakes the parked half for a settled one.

## (e) Prominence-budget accounting

Per T2, structural prominence is finite and reserved for the four states whose naive lie is "fine": stale, P = unknown (verifier-facing), the conditioned metadata-decoy marker, and H2-guessable. Row by row:

- **Quiet — must not compete:** AF1 (elevation is focus, not signal), AF2, AF4, AF6 (hover is an enhancement by definition), AF8, AF9, AF10 (the ceremony route is reachable, not loud), AF11 (the vector is distinct-by-symbol per P1/P9's capacity clause, but its cells do not individually take structure — a nine-cell display that shouted every cell would be the inversion), AF13 (on-demand by design).
- **Touches the budget through the states it renders, not through its own cue:** AF3 — the H2-guessable treatment (Rule 8) is a budgeted state; the *expansion cue* stays quiet, the *state treatment* spends. AF7 — the Rule 12 marker, when its conditioned trigger fires, is budgeted; every other hole treatment is distinct (P1) but not loud.
- **Spends the budget directly:** AF17's verifier-facing P banner (Rule 18 mandates the prominence ordering). Stale spends the budget too, but attaches to card chrome as a state (Rule 4, appearing without user action), not to any affordance in this table — noted so the spend is accounted somewhere.
- **Candidate spend, deliberately undecided:** AF15's interruptive-warning option (§f question 7). If adopted it would be a fifth structural state; that it would dilute the other four is an argument the design phase must weigh explicitly — which is part of why the question is parked rather than answered here.
- **Distinct-but-not-loud, for the record:** AF16's historical restyling is a whole-medium degrade (Rule 15), orthogonal to the four-state budget.
