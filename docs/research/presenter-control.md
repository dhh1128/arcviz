# Presenter control — the gap the outside-model panel found

Phase 2D-c. Three independent non-Claude models, reviewing [disclosure-matrix.md](disclosure-matrix.md) blind to each other, converged unprompted on the same structural absence: every one of axis V's nine checks (§B3) is about the *artifact's issuance history* — SAID self-consistency, schema conformance, signature validity, KEL anchoring, delegation, edge constraints, issuer bona fides, revocation, duplicity. None establishes that the party who just handed a credential to a viewer controls the AID the credential names as holder ([reviews/2026-09-05-outside-model-refutation.md](../../reviews/2026-09-05-outside-model-refutation.md) §(b)1, adjudicated VALID). This document traces what evidence of presenter control actually exists in KERI/ACDC, whether it survives to a renderer, and what arcviz can honestly say.

## (a) What IPEX actually proves about the presenter

**The primitive that carries a presenter's signature is the `exn` (exchange) message, not the ACDC.** `exchange()` builds the SAD that `grant`/`admit`/`offer`/`agree`/`apply`/`spurn` each wrap (`~/code/wot/keripy/src/keri/core/eventing.py:1473-1538`). For the V2 protocol arcviz's corpus targets, the fields committed by the message's own SAID (`d`, computed by `SerderKERI(sad=sad, makify=True)` at `eventing.py:1538`) are (`eventing.py:1524-1536`):

```
i   sender AID (the discloser/presenter)
ri  receiver AID (the intended recipient)
x   exchange id — a session/thread nonce
p   prior exchange message SAID (thread continuity)
dt  RFC-3339 timestamp
r   route ("/ipex/grant", "/ipex/admit", ...)
a   attributes — for grant, {m: message, o: [disclosed-node SAID]}
```

`grant()` (`ipexing.py:1282-1353`) builds exactly this SAD, sets `a.o[0]` to the disclosed ACDC's own SAID (`ipexing.py:1330`, `_streamSerder(origin).said`), and calls `_sign()` (`ipexing.py:1351`). `_sign()` (`ipexing.py:286-321`) signs `serder.raw` — the entire SAD above, not just a subset — with the sender's current keys: `sigers = hab.sign(ser=serder.raw, indexed=True)` for a transferable AID, anchored to `hab.kever.lastEst` (the sender's last establishment event) via a `TransIdxSigGroup` (`ipexing.py:304-314`). **This is the same mechanism that signs any KERI-controlled message** — it proves whoever produced this signature currently controls the sending AID's keys as of its last establishment event. That is real, KEL-anchored proof of key control, not a bare unauthenticated assertion.

**What the signature binds, concretely:** sender AID, receiver AID, a session nonce, a prior-message link, a timestamp, the route, and the SAID of the specific disclosed credential (`a.o[0]`). It does **not** sign the disclosed ACDC's bytes directly — those travel as a separate nested CESR stream (`nests`, appended by `_sign(..., nests=nests, ...)` at `ipexing.py:1351`, stripped from the signed serder itself at `ipexing.py:1352`). The binding to content is indirect: the signed `a.o[0]` is the ACDC's SAID, and `IpexHandler.verify()`'s graph walk (`_validNodeNest`, `ipexing.py:463-492`) checks that the first disclosed nest's own computed SAID matches `origin` (`ipexing.py:484`, `nserder.compare(origin)`) — so a verifier that runs this check does confirm the attached bytes are the credential the signature's SAID commits to. This is sound: a SAID is a hash commitment, and committing to it is as good as committing to the content.

**So, answering the brief's question directly: yes, a `grant` is signed by the presenter**, over a structure that names the receiver, a session nonce, a timestamp, and the disclosed credential's own SAID. `admit()` (`ipexing.py:1356-1399`) is the mirror on the recipient's side — the recipient signs an acknowledgment naming the grant's SAID as `p` (`ipexing.py:1389`), which similarly proves the *recipient's* key control and receipt, but says nothing new about the discloser.

**What `IpexHandler.verify()` checks, and — the load-bearing negative finding — what it does not.** The verifier (`ipexing.py:345-461`) validates: route shape and required attribute fields (Stage 1, `ipexing.py:379-385`); that `apply`/`offer`/`grant` carry the disclosure-path grammar or the origin SAID correctly (Stage 2, `:387-421`); that a thread opener carries both `ri` and `x`, or a reply correctly chains to its prior via `_verifyReplyChain` (Stage 3, `:422-441`, `_verifyReplyChain` at `:494` ff.); that a `grant`'s disclosed graph is closed and semantically valid (Stage 4, `:443-460`, `_walkGraph` at `:538`); and that each disclosed node's issuer-auth (bona fides) proof checks out (Stage 5, `_verifyIssuerAuthGraph`, `:868-896`). **Nowhere in this method, or in `_validNodeNest`, `_walkGraph`, or `_verifyIssuerAuthGraph`, is the sender AID (`serder.ked["i"]`) ever compared against the disclosed credential's own holder/issuee field (`a.i` inside the disclosed node's SAD).** The checks are: is this message well-formed, does it chain correctly, is the disclosed DAG closed, and is each disclosed node's *issuer* authorized. Whether the AID *sending the grant* is the AID the disclosed credential *names as holder* is asked nowhere in this pipeline. **Inference from a direct read of the full method** — this is an absence, and an absence is not conclusively provable by reading one file; it is corroborated below by a case where the KERI test corpus itself had to build this exact check by hand because the reference verifier does not supply it.

## (a-cont) The worked example that proves the gap has already been hit, and patched by hand

`tests/acdc/test_guardianship_presentation.py` is keripy's own worked example of a presentation whose holder is not the credential's subject — Bob (a guardian) presenting a fact about Mia (his ward) to a relying party. Its module docstring states the finding in almost the exact words this document would otherwise have to construct from inference:

> "WHERE THE INVARIANT IS ACTUALLY ENFORCED, the panel review's dominant finding: not by NI2I, which is relationally inert and which verifyChain never uses to bind presenter against ward. The pins make only the impersonation SHAPE unrepresentable; the guarantee is the binding logic at `_verify_representation`, which an implementer must port rather than copy." (`test_guardianship_presentation.py:36-40`)

`_verify_representation` (`test_guardianship_presentation.py:1166-1213`) is application code the test file itself defines — it is not part of keripy's `Verifier`/`verifyChain`. It performs five checks, and check 3 is exactly presenter-vs-subject binding, done entirely off ACDC content fields rather than off any IPEX exchange signature:

```python
# (3) ward data NI2I, holder != subject.
assert e['wardId']['o'] == 'NI2I' and e['wardId']['n'] == wardSedi.said
assert e['wardAge']['o'] == 'NI2I' and e['wardAge']['n'] == wardAge.said
assert presentation.sad['i'] != wardSedi.iseaid            # presenter is not the ward
assert presentation.sad['i'] != wardAge.iseaid
```
(`test_guardianship_presentation.py:1204-1208`)

Two things this establishes, both load-bearing for arcviz:

1. **This binding is performed against the presentation-credential's own `i` (issuer) field — a static content field asserted at issuance of that one-time presentation ACDC — not against the IPEX grant's signer.** In this worked example the "presentation" is itself a freshly-issued, one-time ACDC (`'rd' not in presentation.sad` — "one-time presentation, not logged", `test_guardianship_presentation.py:1251`), so its own signature (checked by ordinary ACDC signature/KEL-anchoring verification, already one of the matrix's nine V checks) *does* prove Bob's key control at the moment this specific presentation credential was minted. That is a stronger, and different, mechanism than trusting an IPEX `grant`'s signature: it is presenter-control proven by the disclosed artifact's own issuance signature, because the act of presenting *is* an act of issuing. It only works because this presentation pattern mints a fresh credential per disclosure; it says nothing about the far more common case — arcviz's likely default — of disclosing a pre-existing, already-issued, unchanging credential via a bare IPEX `grant`.
2. **The module's own docstring states this binding "is not by NI2I" and must be "ported rather than copied"** — i.e., keripy's reference verifier does not supply it, does not enforce it, and every relying-party implementation must reimplement `_verify_representation`-equivalent logic itself. This directly corroborates the negative finding in (a): keripy ships the cryptographic primitives (I2I/NI2I operators, signature/KEL verification) that *could* support a presenter-binding check, but does not ship the check itself, even in its own most sophisticated worked example of exactly this problem.

## (b) What a replayer could concretely reuse

The gap in (a) is not abstract. Tracing the concrete ingestion paths [keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md) already mapped (§(c)) shows exactly what a party with no key control over a credential's holder AID can do with a copy of its bytes:

- **`POST /credentials/verify`** (current upstream KERIA, `credentialing.py:696-758` per [keria-signify-surface.md:68](keria-signify-surface.md)) "submits `acdc`+`iss` into `agent.parser.ims`" and funnels into the same `Verifier.processCredential` (`~/code/wot/keripy/src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:107-244`) every other ingestion path uses. That pipeline checks registry presence, a staleness gate on the *issuance event's* timestamp, schema conformance, and edge/chain constraints against locally-held far nodes ([keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md) §(c)). Its own enumerated "not checked here at all" list is explicit: "SAID self-consistency... signature validity and KEL-anchoring of the *credential's own* anchoring event... duplicity... issuer bona fides beyond the one-hop edge-chain check" are either checked once at original ingestion or never checked by this path at all — and **nothing on that list, or anywhere else in `processCredential`, asks who is submitting these bytes right now, or whether they control the AID named in the credential's own `a.i` (issuee/holder) field.**
- **Consequence:** a party who obtains a credential's raw SAD — by finding a leaked export, receiving a forwarded copy, or simply being handed it outside any IPEX exchange at all — can submit it to their own agent via `/credentials/verify` (or have it delivered as the `nests` payload of *any* grant, including one they construct themselves naming themselves as both sender and, functionally, the party benefiting from its acceptance) and have it pass every check in axis V, then render via `GET /credentials/{said}` (`credentialing.py:999-1049`) identically to a credential the viewing agent's own controller was legitimately disclosed. The gate is binary — accepted or escrowed — and, per [keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md) §(c), "There is no third state visible to the client and no way, from a *successful* `GET`, to learn which of the checks above ran or how they resolved."
- **The IPEX layer does not close this**, because (a) established that `IpexHandler.verify()` never compares the grant's sender against the disclosed credential's holder field. A grant addressed to me, naming *someone else's* AID as the disclosed credential's holder, structurally validates — the graph-closure and issuer-auth checks it does run are silent on that mismatch.
- **What is genuinely hard to replay:** a *fresh, valid IPEX grant purporting to be sent by AID X* cannot be forged by someone who does not hold X's current signing key, because `_sign()` requires `hab.sign()` over the sender's own keys (`ipexing.py:304-309`). The attack surface is not "forge a signature" — it is "the signature, once made, and the credential bytes it names, are freely copyable, and nothing downstream re-checks that the party now presenting them is the party the signature named as sender, nor that the sender is the credential's own holder." **This distinction matters for arcviz's rendering claims below**: a renderer can honestly say a *credential's own issuance chain* is intact without being able to say anything about whether the entity that just handed it over is entitled to.
- **Session-level replay of the `xid` itself is unconfirmed rather than ruled out.** `xid` is generated by the *sender* (`Diger(ser=Noncer().qb64b).qb64`, `ipexing.py:1308`), not issued by the verifier as a challenge — unlike, e.g., SD-JWT's Key Binding `nonce`, which the *verifier* is expected to supply so it can check the nonce it handed out is the one signed over (see §(e)). No code read in this pass performs verifier-side duplicate-SAID or duplicate-`xid` rejection at the `IpexHandler`/`Exchanger` layer (`handle()`, `ipexing.py:1036-1053`, only notifies); **this is an absence-of-evidence finding, not a confirmed absence** — KERIA's own escrow/notification layer (`~/code/wot/keria/src/keria/app/ipexing.py`) was not traced deeply enough in this pass to rule out deduplication happening there instead.

## (c) Does any of this evidence reach a renderer?

No, for the default JSON path arcviz will actually consume. [keria-signify-surface.md §(a)](keria-signify-surface.md) already established the full shape of `Credentials.get(said)`'s return value: `{sad, atc, iss, issatc, pre, schema, chains, status, anchor, anc, ancatc}` (`~/code/wot/signify-ts/src/types/keria-api-schema.ts:317-343`). **There is no field anywhere in that type for the exn message that delivered the credential, its SAID, its signature, or the presenting AID.** `cloneCreds` (`~/code/wot/keripy/src/keri/vdr/eventing.py:2427-2443`) builds the response from `creder.sad` — the ACDC's own SAD — plus registry/anchor metadata; the IPEX exchange that may have delivered it is a wholly separate object class in KERIA's data model, never joined into the credential response.

**The exn messages are not destroyed — they are just a different, separately-addressed resource that a host must know to ask for.** signify-ts exposes `notifications()`/`exchanges().get(said)` (`~/code/wot/signify-ts/src/keri/app/clienting.ts:470,494`, confirmed live at [keria-signify-surface.md §(d)](keria-signify-surface.md)) which can retrieve the raw apply/offer/agree/grant/admit `exn` messages, including (by construction, from (a) above) the grant's own signature and sender AID — *if the host knows the exn's SAID to ask for, and chooses to fetch it.* This is not automatic, not bundled with the credential object, and — per this document's own reading of `IpexHandler` — even if fetched, it hands the host a signed message the host must independently verify was addressed to *this* viewer, was signed by *this* AID, and names a credential whose holder field the host must then separately reconcile against the sender. None of that reconciliation logic exists anywhere in the traced client or server surface; it would be host-authored, exactly analogous to `_verify_representation` in (a-cont).

**Practical consequence for arcviz.** A credential retrieved the ordinary way (`Credentials.get()`/`.list()`) arrives **stripped of any presentation context by default** — this matches [keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md)'s existing gap #2 ("No attested-cause metadata") in kind, not degree: it is the same shape of gap (the transport carries the artifact but not the act that delivered it) applied to a different fact. A host wanting to say anything about presenter control would have to (1) separately fetch the grant exn by SAID, (2) verify its signature and KEL-anchoring itself (no library call in this surface does it automatically for an exn message the way `Verifier.processCredential` does for a credential), and (3) reconcile the grant's sender AID against the disclosed credential's holder field itself, because nothing in keripy, KERIA, or signify-ts does that reconciliation anywhere in the traced surface. **Marked as inference bounded by this pass's coverage**: `Escrows`, `Groups`, and `Challenges` methods on the signify-ts client were not opened in the KERIA/signify survey ([keria-signify-surface.md §(d)](keria-signify-surface.md), pair 7 row) or in this pass, so a residual, unexamined surface remains possible, though nothing in the architecture traced so far suggests it would supply this reconciliation.

## (d) Is presenter control even the right frame for every viewer?

**No, and the matrix's silence on *whose view* is being rendered is the more fundamental gap.** Two cases the current five-axis matrix does not distinguish:

- **Holder-facing (a wallet showing its own controller's credentials).** Here "does the presenter control the holder AID" is not merely satisfied, it is a category error to ask: there is no presentation event to evaluate, because the viewer *is* the holder inspecting their own store. A renderer in this mode should not surface a presenter-control indicator at all — doing so would imply a question is live that the viewing context has already foreclosed, which is exactly the kind of naive-lie-by-implication the matrix's whole apparatus exists to prevent (cf. Rule 13's "no negative claims" principle, applied here to a check that would be a non-sequitur rather than a negative).
- **Verifier-facing (a relying party who just received a presentation and is deciding whether to act on it).** Here presenter control is the whole question this document exists to answer, and (a)–(c) show it is answered by nothing in the default transport.
- **A third case the brief did not name but the evidence surfaces: a party inspecting a credential they neither hold nor were just presented — e.g. an auditor replaying a stored exn log, or a viewer of a static export/screenshot (Rule 15's case).** Here presenter control is a *historical* question ("did the original recipient's host verify this, at the time"), not a live one, and the honest rendering is closer to axis T's "as-of" framing than to a fresh pass/fail.

These three are not points on axis V (which checks are computable from the artifact and a substrate) nor on axis H (what's in hand) — they describe *why the viewer has this material in front of them at all*, a fact about the render's own provenance rather than the credential's. That is a different kind of thing from every existing axis, and it is also, on inspection, not really a *check* in the V sense: it cannot degrade to {pass, fail, unperformed, unperformable} on a per-addressable-unit basis the way V's nine do, because it applies once, to the whole render, not per section/edge/rule block.

## (e) The ecosystem's term of art

The KERI/ACDC spec corpus vendored for this project does not name this problem. `grep` across `~/code/me/kswg-acdc-specification`'s spec files for "holder binding," "device binding," "presenter," and "possession" turns up only incidental uses of the word "presenter" in prose about Private Edges (`spec/spec-body.md:1165`) and general architecture (`spec/spec-body.md:2934`) — never a named security property. arcviz's own prior-art corpus has the same gap: [prior-art/wallets.md](prior-art/wallets.md) covers Apple/Google/mDL/EUDI verification-state and trust-model claims in detail but never names device- or holder-binding as a distinct property, and the ISO 18013-5/18013-7 texts themselves are paywalled and marked `status: lead`/unverified there (wallets.md "Unverified" section) — this document inherits that same limitation and does not claim to have read ISO 18013-5's clause text directly.

Two adjacent ecosystems do name it, and arcviz should borrow their vocabulary rather than invent its own:

- **OpenID/IETF's SD-JWT ecosystem calls it "Key Binding."** RFC 9901 (published, freely accessible — retrieved via WebFetch 2026-09-11, not locally captured; see epistemic note below) defines it directly: "Ability of the Holder to prove possession of an SD-JWT by proving control over a private key during the presentation" (RFC 9901 §1.2). The mechanism is a **Key Binding JWT (KB-JWT)**, "tied to" a specific SD-JWT because its payload is signed with the key named in the SD-JWT and it carries `sd_hash`, a hash of the SD-JWT being presented (RFC 9901 §1.2). Its payload carries three fields doing exactly the replay-protection work IPEX's `grant` only partly does: `iat` (issuance time), `aud` ("a single string that identifies the intended receiver"), and `nonce`, which "ensures the freshness of the signature or its binding to the given transaction" (RFC 9901 §4.3). **The load-bearing difference from IPEX**: in the SD-JWT flow the verifier is expected to issue the `nonce` as a challenge and check the KB-JWT signs *that* nonce back; IPEX's `x` (xid) is generated by the *sender* (`Diger(ser=Noncer().qb64b).qb64`, `ipexing.py:1308`), so it functions as a self-chosen thread/session identifier, not a verifier-issued freshness challenge the recipient can independently confirm was never reused. This is a genuine, protocol-level asymmetry between the two ecosystems' notion of "binding," not a wording difference.
- **ISO 18013-5's mDL ecosystem calls the mechanism "mdoc authentication," built from a `DeviceKey` and a session-scoped `SessionTranscript`.** This document can confirm only the outline from the one locally-captured secondary source that mentions it: AAMVA's implementation guidelines state a "session transcript as defined in 18013-7/18013-5 is used as part of key derivation" for the encrypted device response (`aamva-mdl-implementation-guidelines-v1.6-2026-09-04.pdf`, locally captured at `refs/captures/wallets/iso-mdl/`, text at the page containing "The session transcript as defined in 18013-7/18013-5 is used as part of key derivation"). **The primary ISO 18013-5 clause text (which names `DeviceAuth`/`DeviceSignature`/`DeviceMac` explicitly) was not read in this pass** — it is paywalled, already flagged `status: lead` in [prior-art/wallets.md](prior-art/wallets.md)'s own "Unverified" section, and this document does not add a claim beyond what AAMVA's guide states in the passage above. What AAMVA's guide does independently and directly confirm is the concrete failure mode a presenter-control gap enables: it requires the presentation protocol be "fully specified... does not include options that... could lead to non-interoperability," and adds, as its own Note 1, that this "requirement prohibits the sharing of mDL data using the mDL as a 'flash pass' (i.e. by showing an image of a credential to a verifier); also see section 8" (AAMVA guide, same page as the session-transcript passage, Note 1). **That "flash pass" prohibition is the mDL ecosystem's own named instance of exactly the replay risk in (b)**: a static, previously-captured rendering of credential data, detached from any live proof of current key control, is explicitly called out and forbidden — arcviz's own static/screenshot rendering case (matrix Rule 15) sits squarely inside this named failure mode.
- **arcviz's recommended term of art: "presenter control" (this document's own coinage, per the commissioning brief) for the general property, with "holder binding" as the closest existing cross-ecosystem name for the mechanism** (used loosely across both SD-JWT's "Key Binding" and mDL's device-binding literature to mean the same underlying idea: a credential's presentation carries proof, independent of the credential's own issuance signature, that the presenter currently controls the key the credential names as its holder). This document is coining "presenter control" for arcviz's own rendering vocabulary rather than adopting "holder binding" wholesale, because "holder" in ACDC's own terminology already has a specific meaning (the party legally/operationally in possession, distinct from the *issuee*, which is the schema-defined subject) that risks collision — flagged here per house style, stopping short of building further vocabulary on it without confirmation.

**Epistemic note on the RFC 9901 citations above.** This document's file-ownership scope for this task is limited to `docs/research/presenter-control.md` and `refs/sources.presenter.yaml`; it does not extend to `refs/captures/`, so the RFC 9901 quotes above were not captured to a local file as EVIDENCE.md rule 3 would otherwise require, and are recorded in `refs/sources.presenter.yaml` with that limitation stated in the record itself. The quotes were retrieved via a single fetch-and-extract pass against `https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9901.html`, 2026-09-11; they read as direct extraction of a published, versioned IETF RFC's defined-terms and normative-requirements sections, not as a summary, but this document flags the missing local capture as a deviation from normal practice rather than silently treating it as equivalent to the fully-captured sources elsewhere in this corpus.

## (f) What arcviz can honestly render — rules in the style of §d

These are additions to, not replacements for, [disclosure-matrix.md §d](disclosure-matrix.md)'s existing fifteen rules. They assume the sixth axis proposed in the open questions below; each rule states what it means if that axis is adopted, and what it means if arcviz instead treats this as a rendering-mode precondition rather than a per-credential axis (both are defensible; see open question 1).

- **RULE P1 — a rendering that resulted from receiving a presentation, rather than from a viewer's own holdings, must say so before it says anything else about verification state.** *Test:* find a verifier-facing render (any UI reached by "a credential was just disclosed to me") that shows the nine-check V vector without a preceding, equally prominent statement of whether presenter control was established for this render. A V vector shown first, with presenter-control information available only on drill-down or absent, fails — it reproduces exactly the "every check is issuer-side, so the whole vector looks green" failure this document exists to correct.
- **RULE P2 — presenter control renders as unknown unless a host affirmatively supplies the exchange evidence AND its reconciliation.** Per (c), the default KERIA/signify-ts transport supplies neither the exn message nor the reconciliation of its sender against the credential's holder field. *Test:* find any verifier-facing render showing "presenter verified" or equivalent, backed only by `GET /credentials/{said}` having succeeded (i.e., by mere store presence). Store presence warrants nothing about presenter control — it warrants only what R0 in the matrix's revision history already says it warrants: "the substrate's ingestion gate did not reject this," which per (b) above is a check that a stolen credential's own bytes pass identically to a legitimately-disclosed one.
- **RULE P3 — holder-facing renders never show a presenter-control indicator.** *Test:* find a render, reached from "browse my own wallet" rather than "review what was just sent to me," that includes any presenter-control glyph at all, positive or negative. Its presence implies a live question that this viewing context has already foreclosed — the same category of naive lie Rule 13 forbids for negative claims, applied here to a check that is a non-sequitur rather than a negative.
- **RULE P4 — when presenter control *is* attested, the rendering names its scope exactly the way Rule 3 requires for verification claims: who attested it, over what signature, addressed to whom, as of when.** A bare "presenter verified ✓" fails the same test Rule 3 already applies to every other positive claim in this document; presenter control is not exempt from the as-of-scope discipline just because it arrives, when it arrives at all, from a different data path than the nine V checks.
- **RULE P5 — a static/exported/printed rendering (Rule 15's case) that carries presenter-control information renders it as historical, never live**, following Rule 15's own pattern exactly: "checked ⟨scope⟩ by ⟨tool⟩," naming the exn SAID and the host that verified it, not a fresh badge. This is the direct rendering-side answer to the mDL "flash pass" prohibition in (e): arcviz cannot prevent a viewer from screenshotting its own output, but it can prevent that screenshot from silently continuing to assert a presenter-control claim that was only ever true of one specific live session.

## (g) Open questions

1. **Sixth axis, or rendering-mode precondition?** This document leans toward a sixth axis (tentatively "P — presentation provenance," or folded into a broadened reading of axis C, since both are "out-of-band, mostly unknowable from the artifact alone" in the matrix's own existing language) over treating viewer-role as a mode switch outside the matrix entirely — an axis keeps the property inside the same testable-rule apparatus the rest of the matrix uses, where a mode switch would let it quietly fall outside review. Not decided here; needs the same kind of deliberate call [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) recorded for the staleness-threshold and E1E-operator questions.
2. **Does KERIA's own escrow/notification layer (`~/code/wot/keria/src/keria/app/ipexing.py`) perform xid- or SAID-level replay/duplicate rejection that this pass did not trace?** Flagged in (b) as an absence-of-evidence rather than a confirmed absence. If it does, the "what a replayer could reuse" picture in (b) needs narrowing to routes that bypass IPEX entirely (`/credentials/verify`, direct ingestion), which remain unaffected regardless of the answer.
3. **Does `_verify_representation`'s pattern — binding presenter to subject via the disclosed credential's own content fields rather than via the IPEX exchange signature — generalize to the common case of presenting a pre-existing, non-freshly-minted credential?** The guardianship worked example gets its binding "for free" because presenting *is* issuing in that pattern.
