## `m-glance.md` — Measuring the Glance (version 1.3, revision_date 2026-07-31)

This is the companion to `amp-diff.md` (already read by the orchestrator) and estimates, from an adversary's side, what a *habituated* — as opposed to *careful* — reader actually gets from glancing at an entviz figure.

**The numbers, with regimes.** The paper reports a curve, not a single figure, and is explicit that reporting one summed number was "the mistake the exercise exists to catch" [mglance2026, m-glance.md:56]. Four regimes matter for arcviz:

- **Involuntary one-glance gestalt: low-to-mid teens of bits.** entviz's global channels (background, cell-color field, color bar, ellipse silhouette, blank-location pattern) are pre-attentive and read together in one parallel glance, so the everyday, unforced read of a pill is this bundle — "in the low-to-mid teens of bits" [mglance2026, m-glance.md:16, m-glance.md:240-245].
- **Diligent (every channel, comparison JNDs) sum: low-to-mid twenties of bits** [m-glance.md:174-175] — this is what a *careful*, attentive-but-not-text-reading comparison yields, still well short of the full value.
- **Exact-field (bit-identical match on every channel) ceiling: ~30-48 bits** [m-glance.md:152] — a conservative upper bound, since exact-field distinctions are finer than any glance discriminates.
- **Lossless ceiling: the whole value, ≤512 bits**, reached only if the viewer reads the cell text, which is the input itself rendered losslessly [mglance2026-ceiling, m-glance.md:310-316].

**The comparison to SSH randomart (drunken bishop).** Measured the same way, randomart's habituated glance is comparable — about eleven to fourteen bits, depending on hash length [m-glance.md:293-294] — but its published whole-image figure of ~22 bits is a *hard ceiling*: there is no lossless text to fall back on, so a careful reader gains nothing further. entviz's advantage is not at the bare glance (a near-tie) but everywhere else on the curve: "One artifact can be read all the way down to certainty; the other cannot. That is the largest genuine difference between them." [mglance2026-ceiling, m-glance.md:316]

**The author's own caveats — load-bearing, not throat-clearing.** Every tolerance (τ for ellipse overlap, ±1-cell/slot matching, from-memory widening) is a *modeled* just-noticeable difference drawn from the JND and visual-search literatures, not measured on people [m-glance.md:382-384]. The paper states directly: "None of this is a human measurement, and we have been careful to say so; it is the most an adversary's arithmetic can establish before the study that would close the loop." [mglance2026-caveats, m-glance.md:406-408] The claim that entviz's structure *cultivates* a better-than-glance habit is "grounded in the artifact's affordances, not in observed behavior" [m-glance.md:388-390] — i.e., it is an argument about what the design *affords*, not a demonstrated behavioral effect.

**The design question, answered directly.** Given these numbers: an entviz pill's involuntary glance is worth roughly the entropy of a 4-5 digit PIN (2^13 ≈ 8,000 to 2^16 ≈ 65,000 candidates) against an attacker who need only match the gestalt, and that cost falls further for a *constrained* attacker impersonating one specific published identifier, who "picks the victim whose figure is cheapest to forge" [m-glance.md:268]. That is real, non-trivial resistance to a casual substitution attack — enough to make "swap in a look-alike SAID" a bad bet against a habituated viewer who has actually looked — but it is nowhere near cryptographic-strength verification, and its strength depends on a *guided, structured* comparison protocol being followed, not an unguided glance at a wall of pills.

## `if.md` — Identity Facets (version 0.11, revision_date 2026-07-11) and `3dim.md` (version 1.0, 2019-01-29)

**Facet-sameness is owner-exclusive by design.** "Sameness across facets is meant to be visible to Cecilia, and to no one else. A facet is, before it is anything else, a unit of isolation. ... Revealing that two facets are one person is Cecilia's call to make, one relationship at a time. ... this is never a default, never an inference a stranger is meant to draw, never a leak the software condones behind her back." [if2026-facet-isolation, if.md:61-63] `3dim.md` states the same principle in its earlier, less-developed form: collapsing every relationship's view into a single universal identifier "raises privacy concerns. If everyone identifies you the same way, then they can share information about you. Unchecked, correlation and surveillance become scary." [3dim2019-relationship-collapse, 3dim.md:29-31]

**Credentials have affinity, not ownership.** "A credential tends to have an affinity for one facet" [if.md:95], but this is a tendency, not a rule: attestations of an *act* (a notary witnessing a signature) can legitimately span facets, and pinning them to one facet "by fiat would discard information the credential never committed to" [if2026-credential-affinity, if.md:99-101]. arcviz should model credential-to-facet association as affinity, and should render credentials that span facets honestly rather than forcing a single-facet bucket.

**The two-tool division of labor is the direct rationale for the pill+alias pairing.** "The first question is 'which one is this?' ... The answer is a friendly, local alias ... The second question is 'is this the same one?' ... render the identifier as a picture a person can compare at a glance. ... Naming serves facets: it tells you which face you are dealing with. Visual comparison serves sameness: it lets a human confirm that this identifier is the same one they saw before." [if2026-two-tools, if.md:123-129] This is `if.md`'s own explicit statement of why entviz (sameness) and COIA (naming) are paired tools, not redundant ones — it should be treated as a stated design commitment arcviz inherits directly, not just an inference.

## `who-sign.md` (v1.0, 2026-02-07) and `sign-author.md` (v1.1, 2026-06-02)

**A signature's meaning is lifecycle-dependent.** "Issuers sign credentials. These signatures assert facts about the world ... The signer is an authoritative source, not the subject. Subjects sign collections of evidence. ... its signature is not claiming authorship of the underlying facts. It is saying: this is the set of evidence I stand behind, together, for this purpose." [whosign2026-lifecycle, who-sign.md:81-86] Applied to arcviz: rendering "issuer" and "issuee" labels must not imply the same relationship to the credential's content in both cases — an issuer's signature is an assertion, a holder's presentation signature is a stance, not authorship.

**Opaque control collapses inspectable distinctions.** "Most tech in common use today hides governance behind a single key. A sophisticated policy engine may exist internally ... but externally, the result is indistinguishable from a lone script with a leaked secret. ... Opaque control collapses meaningful distinctions, and collapsed distinctions force trust decisions to rely on reputation and hope rather than inspection." [whosign2026-opacity, who-sign.md:102-106] KERI's KEL/TEL data can make signing thresholds and role separation inspectable; a flat "signed ✓" badge that discards that inspectability reproduces exactly the flattening this paper warns against.

**A digital signature, alone, proves control-of-key plus integrity/origin — nothing about intent.** "A digital signature can show that someone controlling a private key processed some bytes. It does not, by itself, tell you whether they meant: 'I wrote this'. 'I witnessed this'. 'I approve this'. 'I authorise this'. 'I am acting as an agent'. Those meanings have to come from protocol, policy, ceremony, or surrounding evidence." [signauthor2026, sign-author.md:42-50] This directly forbids arcviz from rendering a valid signature as implying authorship or endorsement by default; the ACDC's own schema/edge semantics (per AGENTS.md rule 5, checked against the vendored spec) are what supply the actual meaning, not the signature icon.

## `authenticity-vs-veracity.md` (v1.1, 2026-06-02)

The sharpest single constraint in this corpus for arcviz's verification-state vocabulary. "Authenticity focuses on the truthfulness of an imputed origin. ... Veracity focuses on the truthfulness of claimed facts in content." [avv2025-definitions, authenticity-vs-veracity.md:22-26] "Judgments about authenticity can — if managed very carefully — be reduced to an objective mathematical computation, whereas judgments about veracity inherently require subjective assessments of reputation." [avv2025-orthogonality, authenticity-vs-veracity.md:35] The worked example matters for arcviz's edge cases: a court-stenographer misattribution breaks authenticity but not veracity; a notary witnessing an affidavit commits to its authenticity and nothing about its veracity [authenticity-vs-veracity.md:33]. arcviz's cryptographic verification machinery (SAID recomputation, signature check, KEL state) can only ever speak to authenticity. It has no mechanism that could ever speak to veracity, and no UI element should be worded or colored in a way that a viewer could read as "this claim is true."

## `zh.md` — Zooko and Houdini (v1.1, 2026-07-10)

"We exit the trilemma, and we achieve far better friendliness anyway, if we let humans look up identifiers using discoverable attributes, including friendly names they choose themselves, and then convert to an identifier that's secure and decentralized when they want to use actual protocols." [zh2024, zh.md:74] Human-friendliness is observer-local — the address-book analogy: "The person that I call 'Regina Q. Public', you might call 'Reggie P' or 'Mom' or 'the boss'." [zh.md:50] The paper also warns explicitly against mistaking a plausible-looking name for genuine recognition: "How many users of a blockchain are well acquainted with a person named 'Alice' that might NOT be associated with our imaginary payment address ...? Is the name 'Alice' friendly for them?" [zh2024-alice-warning, zh.md:62] This is the theoretical foundation `oia.md` and COIA build the alias convention on (both already covered by the orchestrator), and it means an alias rendered next to a pill is only ever a *local* convenience label — never evidence of a universally-recognized identity.

## What an entviz pill in arcviz may and may not imply

Rules for a designer, derived directly from `m-glance.md`'s numbers and the rest of this corpus:

1. **A pill's glance is a same/different discrimination aid, not a verification result.** It gives a habituated viewer roughly low-to-mid-teens-of-bits of resistance to a casually substituted look-alike identifier [mglance2026, m-glance.md:16]. That is useful — genuinely harder to fake than nothing — but it is not proof of authenticity, and it must never be labeled, colored, or positioned as if it were the credential's verification state. Verification state (SAID recomputed correctly, signature valid, KEL current) is a separate fact and needs its own separate signal.
2. **The pill's real strength is at the ceiling, not the glance — so the lossless text must stay reachable.** entviz's advantage over any other visualization is structural: it degrades gracefully down to the full value because the cell text is lossless [mglance2026-ceiling, m-glance.md:316]. A pill that cannot be expanded to the actual SAID/AID string on demand throws away the one thing that makes it more than a randomart-grade toy.
3. **Never let the pill imply cryptographic assurance it doesn't carry.** [korir2022wallet] (already in `literature.md`) found wallet users mistaking the mere presence of a random-looking string for "the secure encoding or encryption" itself. A pill is exactly the kind of confident-looking visual artifact that can produce the same false comfort if it is placed near a lock icon, a green checkmark, or any language suggesting "verified" — that word belongs to the authenticity check, not the pill.
4. **A pill's assurance depends on habituation and, per the paper, a guided comparison protocol — not passive proximity.** The design paper's "guided spot-check protocol" is what pulls a habituated viewer's attention up off the bare gestalt toward the ceiling [m-glance.md:322-325]. A UI that scatters many pills across a dense DAG with no structured "compare this one to that one" affordance should not be assumed to deliver even the low-to-mid-teens-of-bits figure — that number assumes the viewer is actually looking, at the right two pills, side by side.
5. **A pill may never be the sole gate on a high-stakes action.** Given the modeled numbers are unmeasured on people and the constrained attacker gets to pick the cheapest-to-forge target [m-glance.md:266-269], a pill match alone is not sufficient grounds for a consequential decision (approving a disclosure, trusting a new issuer) — it belongs alongside, never instead of, the authenticity check itself.
6. **A pill labeled with a COIA alias is a local convenience, not a claim of recognized identity.** Per `zh.md`'s Alice warning, the alias string next to a pill is whatever the viewer (or their local convention) chose — it is not evidence the identifier is who the label says, and should not read as though it were.

## What arcviz inherits

- The pairing of an entviz pill (answers "is this the same one") with a COIA alias (answers "which one is this") on every rendered SAID/AID is not just consistent with this corpus — it is `if.md`'s own stated division of labor [if2026-two-tools, if.md:123-129]. arcviz should treat this as inherited design intent, not merely a convenient convention.
- Verification-state UI must keep authenticity and veracity strictly separate, per `authenticity-vs-veracity.md` [avv2025-orthogonality]. Any single combined "trust" indicator that blends signature validity with content plausibility is a design error by this corpus's own terms.
- Issuer, issuee, and any delegated/witness signer must be labeled with role-appropriate language (assertion vs. stance vs. delegated operational act), per `who-sign.md`'s lifecycle view [whosign2026-lifecycle] — arcviz should not use one generic "signed by" phrasing for all three.
- arcviz must not build any affordance that helps a viewer infer that two different facet-identifiers (AIDs) in a credential graph belong to the same real-world party, unless the disclosed credential data itself establishes that link. This is `if.md`'s facet-isolation rule [if2026-facet-isolation] applied to graph rendering.
- COIA aliases displayed anywhere in arcviz should be understood and, where the UI allows, labeled as viewer-local, not globally authoritative — per `zh.md`'s resolution of Zooko's trilemma [zh2024].

## Contradictions and tensions

- **DAG rendering as a correlation engine, versus facet isolation.** The orchestrator's brief flagged this directly, and it is real but narrower than it first looks. An ACDC chain's edges are correlations the *issuer/holder already disclosed* by construction (a chained credential explicitly points at another SAID) — rendering those edges is not an inference arcviz is drawing, it is displaying data the credential already contains. The genuine risk is a *second*, un-disclosed layer of correlation: if arcviz ever groups, colors, or lays out nodes by a recurring AID it recognizes *across separate, otherwise-unlinked credentials or graphs* (e.g., "this same issuer AID also appears over here"), that crosses from displaying disclosed structure into surfacing a correlation the holder may not have intended to make visible together. This report cannot resolve where arcviz's actual feature set will land on that line — it is called out as a design decision to make deliberately, not a foregone conclusion either way. See unresolved questions below.
- **`m-glance.md`'s pill-glance assurance versus `literature.md`'s passive-indicator-failure findings.** These look like they conflict but answer different questions, and conflating them would be a mistake. [schechter2007emperor] and [dhamija2006phishing] (in `literature.md`) show that a passive, easily-missed *state* indicator (present-when-fine, absent-when-not) does not register with users — that is about signaling *verification/trust state*. `m-glance.md` is about a different task entirely: an entviz pill is a *content-comparison* aid, actively looked at side-by-side, not a background status light. A pill is not a counter-example to the security-indicator literature; it would become one only if arcviz ever tried to use "the pill looks normal" as a passive stand-in for "this is verified," which rule 1 above forbids for exactly this reason.
- **No direct contradiction found between this corpus and `literature.md`'s other findings** ([ghoniem2005readability]'s node-link-for-path-finding result, [maceachren2012semiotics]'s uncertainty-typology analogy, or the Norman affordance/signifier vocabulary) — this corpus operates one level up, on what identifiers and signatures are allowed to mean, and the earlier survey operates on how uncertainty and graph structure should be drawn. The two surveys are complementary, not overlapping enough to conflict.

## Unresolved questions for Daniel

1. Should arcviz's DAG layout ever visually group or color-link nodes by a *recurring* AID across otherwise-separate credential graphs (not just within one disclosed chain)? `if.md`'s facet-isolation rule argues against doing this by default, but the line between "displaying disclosed structure" and "surfacing an undisclosed correlation" needs a concrete decision, not just a principle.
2. Should the pill+alias pairing ever appear without the guided spot-check/compare affordance `m-glance.md` treats as load-bearing for reaching even the low-to-mid-teens-of-bits figure (e.g., a compact list view with many bare pills)? If so, what should the UI say about the weaker assurance that context provides?
3. Is there an appetite for surfacing KERI governance structure (multisig thresholds, witness sets) in the signature-state affordance, per `who-sign.md`'s "opaque control collapses distinctions" argument — or is that judged out of scope for the current phase?
4. COIA aliases are explicitly viewer-local per `zh.md`. Does arcviz have (or need) a mechanism for a viewer to attach and persist their own alias to an AID they encounter repeatedly, or is alias display limited to whatever a credential's own metadata supplies?
