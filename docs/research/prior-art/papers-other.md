## `cfa-paper.md` — Cross-File Associations (version 1.0, revision_date 2025-12-18)

This paper's whole subject is a formal, general model of how to express relationships between digital artifacts without a container or a central authority — precisely the abstraction arcviz needs for ACDC chain edges, even though the paper never mentions credentials.

**Relationships are first-class graph edges, and binding one is an assertion, not a proof.** "A CFA is a relationship, not a container. Conceptually, CFAs form edges in a graph whose nodes are files. Treating relationships as first-class in this sense means that they are explicitly representable and inspectable, rather than inferred indirectly from structure. Binding a CFA is a statement that a relationship is asserted to exist, independent of whether that assertion is accepted, validated, or contested. It is not a claim about ownership, authorship, correctness, or legitimacy." [cfa-edges-are-typed-assertions, cfa-paper.md:63-65; cfa-assertion-not-authority, cfa-paper.md:67] Applied directly: an ACDC chain edge (`e` block pointing to a source SAID) declares that a relationship exists. Whether that relationship is cryptographically sound (does the SAID actually resolve, does the schema permit the edge) is a separate question arcviz's verification layer must answer independently — drawing the edge is not itself a verification act, and the UI must not conflate "this edge is declared" with "this edge is confirmed."

**Directionality has a name and a reason.** "Some CFAs distinguish between files that provide a logical foundation for the relationship and files whose interpretation depends on that foundation. These are called directed CFAs. Files that provide the logical foundation for a directed CFA are called pre files. Files whose interpretation depends on a pre file within a directed CFA are called co files. ... Other CFAs treat all bound files as peers; these are called common CFAs." [cfa-directed-vs-common, cfa-paper.md:45-47] ACDC chains are asymmetric by construction — a credential's `e` section points to the credential(s) it depends on for interpretation — so they map cleanly onto CFA's "directed" case with the source credential as the pre file and the dependent credential as the co file. This gives arcviz vocabulary for a design decision it must make anyway: an edge in the DAG should read as a dependency arrow (this credential's meaning depends on that one), not as a symmetric "these two are related" line.

**Authority comes from the identifier scheme, not from the fact of declaration.** "The identifier does not, by itself, confer authority. Authority, where it exists, arises from the properties of the identifier scheme—such as cryptographic control or content addressing—not from CFAs as such." [cfa-assertion-not-authority, cfa-paper.md:159] For arcviz, this is a caution against rendering an edge's presence as sufficient evidence of legitimacy: an ACDC's SAID-based content addressing is what makes an edge checkable, and the checking is what earns the edge a "verified" affordance, not the edge's mere existence in the data.

**Surface disagreement rather than resolve it.** "CFAs do not attempt to resolve such disputes. They make no claims about correctness, ownership, or legitimacy. Instead, they expose assertions so that tools and users can evaluate them in context. The design deliberately favors visibility of contested assertions over implicit silence, on the assumption that inspectable disagreement is preferable to unexamined inference. ... Conflicts among CFAs ... are expected. ... CFAs provide a vocabulary for surfacing these disagreements without imposing a resolution model." [cfa-surface-dont-adjudicate, cfa-paper.md:191-197] This is a direct design precedent for a case arcviz will hit: a chain edge whose target SAID does not resolve, or whose schema forbids the claimed relationship. The paper's answer is to show the contested or broken assertion rather than silently drop it or silently "fix" the rendering — consistent with AGENTS.md's instruction never to let a broken or unverifiable state collapse into looking "fine."

## `x509-prob.md` — Why X509 Certs Should Be Secondary Evidence of Org Identity (version 1.3, revision_date 2026-06-08)

Mostly an argument against certificates as primary organizational-identity evidence, but it contains two terse, concrete, on-topic claims about ACDC itself that arcviz needs.

**ACDC chain links are weighted and semantically varied — not a uniform arrow.** "The chaining in ACDCs is like X509 chaining on steroids. It is safer, more efficient, and more cacheable. It doesn't depend on certificate authorities. It eliminates the need for trust registries. Links in a chain can be weighted, and they can have a variety of semantic meanings." [x509prob-acdc-links-weighted-semantic, x509-prob.md:168] This is a direct, if undeveloped, claim that arcviz's edges need at least two visual dimensions beyond mere connectivity: a weight (this credential's chain link carries a numeric significance, e.g. for multi-source aggregation) and a semantic type (the edge means something different depending on the relationship declared). The paper does not elaborate the mechanism — that has to come from the ACDC spec itself, per AGENTS.md rule 5 — but it corroborates that this is a real property to design for, not an invented one.

**Graduated disclosure is named as a real ACDC feature with privacy stakes.** "ACDCs can represent everything that fits in the VC data model, and many things that don't. And ACDCs come with a graduated disclosure feature that achieves important privacy objectives." [x509prob-graduated-disclosure-named, x509-prob.md:170] One sentence, no mechanism — but it is independent corroboration, from the author, that graduated (i.e., selective/partial) disclosure is a first-order ACDC capability that a display layer must handle, not a peripheral one.

**Certificates cannot answer historical questions; sequenced signatures can.** "The binding between a private key, its public complement, and an X509 certificate is inherently subject to retrograde attacks, because there is only a crude, unreliable way to guess at the timing of the event relative to the timing of a revocation or key rotation. This means that certificate-based identity solutions cannot provide a solid audit trail or answer historical questions; they can only be evaluated in real-time." [x509prob-certs-no-audit-trail, x509-prob.md:111-112] The contrast case matters for arcviz: whatever "verified" affordance the UI shows needs a notion of *when* it was true, because the underlying architecture (unlike X.509) actually supports that question — a verification badge that only ever answers "valid now" would be throwing away a real capability of the substrate it renders.

## `wtbo.md` — Where Trust Bottoms Out (version 1.8, revision_date 2026-08-20)

This paper is mostly about identifier-level trust roots (X.509/CT vs. KERI witnesses and watchers), one layer below where ACDC credentials sit, and most of it doesn't bear on arcviz's rendering problem. One distinction does, and it goes to the heart of what arcviz's "unverified" state — one of the four AGENTS.md names explicitly — is allowed to mean.

**"Duplicity-evident," not "duplicity-resistant," bounds what a verified badge can honestly claim.** "This is why KERI's security posture is best described as duplicity-evident rather than duplicity-resistant. It doesn't try to make duplicity impossible — that would require global consensus, with all the costs that entails. It makes duplicity provable and attributable, which is sufficient for a system built around reputation, accountability, and recovery rather than prevention." [wtbo-duplicity-evident-not-resistant, wtbo.md:113] An ACDC's issuer is an AID whose own state rests on this witness/watcher architecture. If arcviz ever shows an "issuer verified" affordance, this distinction says precisely what that affordance may and may not assert: it can mean "no fraud has been detected by the witnesses/watchers consulted," never "fraud is impossible" or "this issuer is guaranteed honest." That is a narrower, more defensible claim than a plain green checkmark usually implies, and it is exactly the kind of overclaim AGENTS.md's four-state distinction (absent/undisclosed/redacted/unverified) is designed to prevent for the "unverified" case specifically.

**Detection depends on which witnesses were actually asked.** "If a watcher asks two witnesses for the head of the same identifier and gets different hash values, it has detected duplicity... The worst a dishonest watcher can do is stay silent about duplicity it has seen — a risk addressed by running your own watcher or consulting multiple independent ones." [wtbo-watcher-detects-not-prevents, wtbo.md:109, wtbo.md:111] A corollary for arcviz: whatever verification pipeline feeds its UI, "verified" is a claim about the checks that were actually performed, not about the identifier in the abstract. If the tool wants to be honest about this, the affordance should be traceable to what was checked, not phrased as an unconditional verdict.

## `ev-life.md` — The Evidence Lifecycle in Telco (version 1.0, revision_date 2025-08-07)

A guest post describing a real, deployed evidence architecture (Verifiable Voice Protocol dossiers) built on exactly the credential stack arcviz visualizes — AIDs, ACDCs, vLEIs — and it happens to describe a worked example of the DAG arcviz must render.

**A dossier is a signed root linking to multiple independently sourced, independently revocable credentials.** "At this point, the business collects all the evidence that it has received or created, and connects the artifacts to each other, making them a definitive embodiment of its intentions ... by placing them in a dossier. This dossier is tiny. It snapshots a collection of tamper-evident hyperlinks to tamper-evident credentials." [ev-life-dossier-is-a-credential-dag, ev-life.md:71-73] This is close to a real-world sample of arcviz's target object: a root artifact (the dossier) whose edges point to a business identity credential, a phone-number-rights credential, a brand-rights credential, and any delegation credentials, each issued by a different authority (the legal registry, the rangeholder carrier, a brand licensor) with its own governance.

**Every linked credential carries its own revocation state, so a single chain-level verdict is not enough.** The paper spells out what a verifier ends up needing to check per credential, not once for the whole chain: "The business has not revoked its intention to make verifiable phone calls with this combination of characteristics. None of the other issuers of credentials have revoked their credentials due to changes in phone number or brand usage rights, etc." [ev-life-verifier-checklist, ev-life.md:85-86] For arcviz, this argues against any UI shortcut that rolls a whole chain's status into one badge — each node's revocation/freshness state is independent and needs its own affordance, since a compromised or revoked leaf credential does not necessarily show up if only the root dossier's own signature is checked.

## `telco-ev-reqs.md` — What Does Telco Need? (version 1.0, revision_date 2026-03-11)

A requirements document, not an architecture proposal, and two of its five properties translate directly into constraints on what arcviz's rendering needs to be able to express.

**Delegation is a weighted, constrained graph, not a tree.** "This is not a hierarchy. It's a graph with weighted edges, conditional logic, time windows, geographic constraints, and spend limits. The authorization semantics are rich because real-world authority is rich." [telco-delegation-is-a-weighted-graph, telco-ev-reqs.md:74] This corroborates the same point `x509-prob.md` makes about weighted, semantically varied chain links, from an independent angle (real-world delegation requirements rather than ACDC's technical feature set) — arcviz's DAG layout and edge affordances need to accommodate edges that carry constraints (time windows, geography, purpose), not just a source-target pointer.

**A verifier needs "what was valid then," not only "what is valid now."** "The system must support verifiable reconstruction of evidence and authority relationships that were valid at an arbitrary point in the past. It must be possible to distinguish what was valid then from what is valid now." [telco-historical-vs-present-validity, telco-ev-reqs.md:154, restating telco-ev-reqs.md:58] Combined with the explicit freshness requirement — "Freshness assumptions must be explicit so verifiers can determine whether the system meets their operational risk thresholds" [telco-revocation-freshness-explicit, telco-ev-reqs.md:164-166] — this argues that arcviz's verification-status affordance needs a visible time dimension: when was this checked, and against what point in time is "valid" being asserted. A static badge that silently means "valid as of right now" discards a capability (historical provability) that both this paper and `wtbo.md`/`x509-prob.md` independently name as a real, load-bearing property of the KERI/ACDC substrate.

## Confirmed not relevant

**`aold.md`** ("Actors, Objects, and Linked Data") argues that the W3C VC spec's mandatory `@context` grafts human-subject credential data into a correlatable global semantic graph, and recommends making `@context` optional for credentials about people [aold.md:128, aold.md:162]. The surface parallel to arcviz — a tool that renders a person's chained credentials as an explorable graph — is real enough to be worth naming, but the specific mechanism the paper objects to does not exist in ACDC. I checked the vendored specification at `~/code/me/kswg-acdc-specification/docs/` directly (per AGENTS.md rule 5, since this is a claim about ACDC semantics): no file contains `@context` or "JSON-LD." ACDCs bind meaning through schema SAIDs, not JSON-LD context resolution, so the correlation mechanism aold.md is arguing against is absent from arcviz's substrate by construction. What remains is a general ethical caution about rendering human-subject data as a graph, which is real but not a model of relationship kinds or directionality (the test this bucket was asked to apply), and treating it as such would have been the strained connection the brief warned against.

**`tsc.md`** ("A Theory of System Coherence") is about keeping AI-assisted codebases legible to their own maintainers — intent trees, governance by declared ends, floor-and-ratchet metrics for code drift. Nothing in it touches credential rendering, trust chains, or evidence display; its vocabulary (absent, undisclosed) is coincidental, not substantive overlap.

**`crna.md`** ("A Call for Reciprocal Negotiated Accountability") proposes watermarks, provisional anonymity, and escrowed "front-door" keys for lawful-access/communications-surveillance policy. Its verifiable-encryption mechanism superficially resembles ACDC redaction (prove something was sealed without revealing it), but the resemblance doesn't survive inspection: crna's watermark proves compliance with terms of service after data has already been shared, which is a different problem from depicting, at render time, what an ACDC's own redaction or non-disclosure state is. Forcing the analogy would misdescribe both mechanisms.

**`ctf.md`** ("The Coming Tsunami of Falsehood") is a 2024 warning about AI-enabled deepfakes and scams at societal scale, with generic advice to become careful truth-tellers and prove the authenticity of one's own communications. It never addresses how a rendered credential or a screenshot of one could deceive a viewer, which was the specific test for this category.

**`active-discovery.md`** ("Building Active Discovery") designs a privacy-preserving rendezvous/discovery protocol using KERI/ACDC attestations, Tor onion services, and provisional anonymity. ACDC appears only as an off-the-shelf attestation format for proving who is asking; the paper has nothing to say about drawing chains, disclosure states, or evidence display.

**`ai-coca.md`** ("AI User Code of Conduct") is a three-paragraph personal pledge about the author's own AI usage ethics (truthfulness, boundaries, responsibility, safeguards). No content bears on arcviz's design problem at all.
