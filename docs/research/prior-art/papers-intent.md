### Supersession: `intent-boundaries.md` vs `intent-monograph.md`

Neither supersedes the other, and the frontmatter says why. `intent-boundaries.md` is `category: Analyses`, `item_id: CC-ANA-250602`, dated 2025-06-20, `citations: hyperlinks`; `intent-monograph.md` is `category: Papers`, `item_id: CC-PAP-251207`, dated 2025-12-11, `citations: acm`, and carries a DOI (`10.2139/ssrn.5909382`). Both are version 1.1; the monograph's `revision_date` is later (2026-07-11 vs 2026-06-02). The monograph is the academic recasting of the essay for SSRN — same definition of intent, same definition of the boundary, same four principles, same streaming-service anecdote with the protagonist renamed from Alice to Arthur.

**Cite the monograph** as the canonical, citable statement: it is the one the rest of the corpus points at. `sda.md:172` reference [19] gives the URL `intent-monograph.html` explicitly; `syntelos.md:672` reference [1] gives the monograph's title and journal ("Intent and Boundaries: A Framework for Digital Agency. SSRN Electronic Journal") without a URL, which matches the monograph's `doi: 10.2139/ssrn.5909382` and not the essay, which has no DOI.

**But the essay is not obsolete, because the monograph drops three things.** First, the corollary that *inventing* a boundary is also an error — "Don't imagine boundaries where they don't actually exist" [hardman-intent-boundaries-noboundary] — which appears in the essay's Principle 1 and has no counterpart in the monograph. Second, the multi-stakeholder disclosure item under "never sneak": failing to disclose that software optimizes for some stakeholders at others' expense [hardman-intent-boundaries-stakeholders]. Third, the audit-trail clause: the monograph keeps "logging user actions inaccurately" but drops "or preserving no audit trail when a boundary is crossed" [hardman-intent-boundaries-logs]. All three bear directly on arcviz, and all three must be cited to the essay.

## (b) The intent taxonomy

Syntelos is a real taxonomy, not a gesture at one. It has a stated unit, discriminating tests for what may be a category and what may not, a notation with defined semantics, and a proposed two-level tree.

### The unit: *telos*

A category is named for the **telos** of an interaction: "the potential outcome that all rational stakeholders should agree embodies the interaction category's raison d'être, imagined as far toward ultimate intent as consensus allows, and framed with maximum specificity" [hardman-syntelos-telos]. The definition does four things at once, and section 3.8 works each through a camera-purchase example: the telos must be an *outcome* (so "mutual gain" qualifies but "failing to come to terms" does not, since that is not the reason to interact); it must command *consensus among all stakeholders* (so "finding a spouse" cannot be the telos of dating, because not all daters would agree); it must be *as ultimate as that consensus allows* (so "transferring assets" beats "mutual gain", which is truer but less specific); and it must describe a *category*, not an instance ("deciding whether a specific camera pleases Carlos" is disqualified for this reason alone).

### Five discriminating tests

1. **The refusal test.** A category is in scope only if it "involve[s] at least two actors with potentially unaligned motives" who can "**reject, delay, or negotiate**" [hardman-syntelos-scope]. Booking a room through an agent is in scope; calling an API that unlocks a door is out, because the tool is deterministically obligated. This is the test that constrains how arcviz may use the taxonomy — see the caveat below.
2. **Policy relevance.** The interaction must potentially cross an intent boundary where it is subject to governance, consent, or routing (`syntelos.md:141`). Heartbeats and keep-alives fail.
3. **Semantic state change.** The interaction must resolve an informational or social deficit (`syntelos.md:145`). A trust-ping, where only the fact of transmission matters, fails.
4. **The consent distinguishability test.** A category must carry enough information for someone to say "yes, I am interested in this kind of interaction" *without* thereby agreeing to specific terms: "Consent to a /gov/Identity.Verify interaction means 'I agree to start a verification process.' It does not mean 'I agree to share my passport number right now.' [...] The taxonomy defines the 'room' we are entering; the subsequent protocol negotiation defines what happens inside that room" [hardman-syntelos-consent-test].
5. **The perspective test.** Every stakeholder must recognize the category as applicable despite their differing roles [hardman-syntelos-perspective]. Buyer and seller meet at one node (`/trade/swap`) with complementary role parameters; there is no `/trade/buy` opposite a `/trade/sell`.

### Two structural rules

**Hierarchy is load-bearing.** A policy at an interior node cascades to everything beneath it unless overridden, which is what makes broad-then-specific consent expressible: "If we know that a policy exists for intent /x/y/\*, then we know it also applies to intent /x/y/z (unless overridden). This facilitates cascading models of consent" [hardman-syntelos-cascade]. Section 6.2 spells out the resulting evaluation order: root check, branch check, leaf check, parameter check.

**The cuts are placed at intent boundaries on purpose.** This is the explicit join between the taxonomy and the boundary framework: "Our taxonomy should therefore *draw lines at likely intent boundaries*, because they represent ideal points for consent and policy enforcement" [hardman-syntelos-boundaries]. **Ours:** the corollary for arcviz is that a category change in the encompassing telos is itself a signal that a boundary may have been crossed — a viewer used inside a `/govern/identify` and then reused inside a `/govern/audit` has moved rooms, in the sense of [hardman-syntelos-consent-test], and the holder consented to the first room.

**Parameterization keeps the tree small.** The who, what, where and how are parameters hanging off a leaf, not additional branches: "a massive hierarchy is an antipattern, especially if all the leaf nodes are identical except for a specific attribute" [hardman-syntelos-parameterization]. Resource is a UNSPSC code, role is `Proposer=Seller`, privacy constraints come from W3C DPV, and negotiated terms come from IEEE 7012 (`syntelos.md:113`, `syntelos.md:119`, `syntelos.md:237-241`).

### Notation

Category names are kebab-case ASCII matching `^[a-z0-9]+(-[a-z0-9])*$`, compared case-insensitively after trimming. Paths concatenate names with `/`, read general-to-specific, and behave like filesystem paths: `/a/b` and `/a/b/` are synonyms, canonical form omits the trailing slash, and containment defines parent-child relationships. Definitions attach to *paths*, not names, because a name may recur at more than one place in the tree. `*` globs within one level; `**` globs across levels. Ad-hoc extension is by an `x-` prefixed name. (`syntelos.md:267-274`, section 4.)

### The tree

Eight domains [hardman-syntelos-domains], each defined by the telos its children share, with the leaf telos given in the paper for each (`syntelos.md:282-509`, sections 5.1-5.8):

| Domain | Domain definition (telos of the family) | Leaves |
|---|---|---|
| `/relate` | establish, maintain, or deepen human connection for its own sake | `seek` (find new connections), `chat` (sustain existing), `play` (share an activity for enjoyment), `celebrate` (mark an occasion or ritual) |
| `/share` | transmit, generate, or verify meaning, knowledge, or culture | `ask` (retrieve specific data points), `inquire` (request info for public/formal record), `study` (generate new knowledge methodically), `teach` (impart skills), `guide` (issue authoritative technical direction), `assist` (resolve a technical or knowledge issue), `notify` (push a one-way alert), `create` (co-create IP), `perform` (broadcast an artistic experience) |
| `/care` | nurture the physical, mental, or developmental well-being of a living subject | `treat` (clinical care), `support` (social/mental welfare), `attend` (custodial oversight) |
| `/serve` | apply skilled labor to modify or maintain the physical world | `repair` (restore a broken system), `maintain` (routine upkeep), `groom` (personal appearance or fitness) |
| `/align` | synchronize independent variables — time, space, movement, logistics — between parties | `meet` (synchronize time), `move` (synchronize movement of goods), `book` (reserve a location or resource), `travel` (synchronize multi-modal transit) |
| `/trade` | exchange ownership, rights, or value between parties | `swap` (static terms), `deal` (malleable terms), `bid` (compete via price offers), `subscribe` (recurring access), `lend` (temporary transfer with expectation of return), `invest` (commit assets for future gain), `hold` (keep assets safe for another), `employ` (contract for human labor), `give` (transfer without expectation of return) |

| `/govern` | establish order, truth, permission, or consensus | `identify` (verify or assert identity and credentials), `permit` (grant or revoke access rights), `audit` (verify adherence to rules or standards), `vote` (collective decision), `chair` (manage procedure), `advocate` (persuade before a decision), `settle` (resolve conflict formally), `process` (execute compulsory legal steps), `enforce` (impose order or ensure safety) |

The paper is explicit that this is provisional — "this paper is not a definitive standard, but rather the conceptual framework for one" (`syntelos.md:280`) — and section 3.11 handles composite activities by decomposition in time rather than by hybrid categories: ask what the telos is *at point in time T*, and accept that a travel booking is `/align/*` at T1 and `/trade/*` at T2.

### The caveat that governs how arcviz may use it

**Syntelos does not type arcviz's affordances, and Hardman says so himself.** The refusal test excludes deterministic tool manipulation, and he applies that limitation to his own taxonomy in `sda.md`: "This is a sound boundary for an intent-negotiation grammar. It is the wrong boundary for a delegation. The acts a delegation most needs to authorize or forbid [...] are exactly the tool-manipulation acts the refusal test excludes" [hardman-sda-syntelos-limit]. A viewer is a tool. Clicking "expand this edge" is not an interaction with a volitional counterparty who might refuse.

**Ours:** the resolution is composite, and it is the main methodological output of this stream. Use **Syntelos to type the encompassing interaction the viewer serves** — the presentation is part of a `/govern/identify`, `/govern/audit`, `/govern/permit`, or `/trade/employ` — because that is what a policy would be written against and what the holder consented to enter. Use **`sda.md`'s act coordinates to type the affordance itself**, because those were built for exactly the tool-manipulation acts Syntelos excludes. Section (e) writes the inventory in both.

### The second and third axes, from `sda.md`

**Effect** is a closed set of five transformations: `observe`, `create`, `modify`, `preserve`, `destroy`, and it is a separate lock from telos — "One axis contains the purpose; the other contains the power" [hardman-sda-effect].

**State-kind** is a closed set of six, and it is *named* rather than inferred: `information` (not yet authoritative), `record` (an entry others may rely on), `commitment` (binds a principal), `authority` (delegations, grants, keys), `resource` (systems, code, money, secrets), `relationship` (a connection to another party) [hardman-sda-statekind]. One act may occupy several coordinates at once, and its gate is the join — the strictest any coordinate demands (`sda.md:73`).

**Gates are derived, and a gate is not an accusation.** "most gates in this model exist for other reasons entirely — because an act enters an authoritative record, creates a standing commitment, crosses into another domain, or must be coordinated through a shared resource" [hardman-sda-gate]. **Ours:** this is directly usable as arcviz UX rhetoric. A confirmation step in arcviz should never be worded as though the user were suspected of something; it should name what the act does.

**And the honest bound.** "Name the category, gate the boundary, audit the remainder — and do not pretend to check the heart" [hardman-sda-sincerity]. **Ours:** arcviz cannot verify why someone is looking at a credential. It can make what they are doing legible and gate the few acts that warrant it. Any design that claims more is claiming something Hardman says is not available.

## (c) Assurance in degrees

`prog-a.md` is about organizational identity assurance, not about display. But it is the corpus's only sustained model of assurance arriving in degrees, and four properties of that model transfer to arcviz's problem of rendering partial assurance honestly.

**Assurance is at least two independent axes, not one scalar.** *Reference assurance* asks how confidently the identifier is tied to a specific legal entity and its reference data; *control assurance* asks how strongly control of the identifier is bound to the entity, an accountable human, or a governed program [hardman-proga-axes]. The paper holds reference assurance fixed while developing four levels of control assurance, and is explicit that these "should be evaluated independently" (`prog-a.md:83`). **Ours:** a single "trust score", meter, or 0-100 badge in arcviz would collapse axes the source model deliberately separates. If arcviz shows degree at all, it shows degree *per axis*, named.

**The four levels of control assurance, each with its stated limitation:**

- **LoA 0 — unambiguous reference affidavit.** An assertion that an entity exists and can be referred to unambiguously. "LoA 0 provides zero proof of *control*. It is an affidavit [...] not a credential conferring entitlement. Anyone can use it to refer to the entity, including competitors, critics, or attackers" [hardman-proga-loa0].
- **LoA 1 — foundational control.** Binds entity, cryptographic identifier, and a controllable digital asset (a domain, a phone number). "LoA 1 proves *some* agent associated with the organization wants the identifier; it does not prove which agent, or whether they are authorized to act for the organization" [hardman-proga-loa1-limit].
- **LoA 2 — human accountability.** Adds non-repudiable proof of a specific human's legal identity plus verifiable delegation (an OOR/ECR vLEI role credential, or a GCD credential with explicit scope, jurisdiction, expiry and thresholds). Its limitation: it establishes accountability for the requester, not good internal governance, and "a properly authorized requester can still go rogue without tripping automated alarms" (`prog-a.md:133`).
- **LoA 3 — robust governance.** Adds adherence to a published, externally audited governance framework, plus demonstrated competence: weighted multi-signature thresholds, independent witnesses, pre-rotation. Its limitation is stated rather than hidden: "No system is perfectly secure. [...] What LoA 3 guarantees is that the system is as transparent, auditable, and resilient as current technology and governance allow" [hardman-proga-loa3-limit].

**The shape of that structure is the finding, more than the level names.** Every level in the paper states, in the same breath and the same register, what it establishes *and* what it does not. **Ours:** this is the pattern arcviz should reproduce. A display of partial assurance should be able to say what has been established and what has not with equal prominence — not establishment in the headline and the gap in a tooltip. This converges with what stream 1E found in the security-indicator literature about passive signals; it arrives here from Hardman's own model rather than from usability research.

**Assurance decays with time, and the decay has a name.** The LEI's LAPSED status "indicates that the data has not been reconfirmed within a specified period of time, with the implication that some of the data may or may not be the most current or accurate" [hardman-proga-lapsed]. **Ours:** *stale* is a fifth state alongside AGENTS.md's *absent*, *undisclosed*, *redacted*, and *unverified* — and it is the one most likely to be silently rendered as *fine*, because a credential that was valid when captured looks identical to one valid now. The source model gives it a name rather than leaving it implicit.

**A rendered checkmark with no verifiable proof behind it is not a credential.** "There is no underlying cryptographic proof a third party could verify if they wanted to. The verification is a UI element, not a credential" [hardman-proga-uielement]. **Ours:** this is the sharpest available statement of arcviz's failure condition, in Hardman's own words, aimed at someone else's product. Any glyph arcviz draws that is not derived from a check arcviz actually performed is the thing this sentence condemns.

**Derivation is lossy and the loss should be visible.** ACDCs are the archival source; SD-JWTs and other formats are exports for a specific use — "A RAW file is the archival source, lossless and reusable; a JPEG is exported for a specific use" [hardman-proga-derivative]. **Ours:** arcviz's rendering is itself a derivation, further down the chain than an SD-JWT. Rendering a graph is a JPEG of a JPEG, and it drops precisely the material — key state at a sequence number, the chain back to the root, the constraints — that the ACDC exists to carry. That argues for a visible, always-available path from any rendered element back to the bytes it came from.

**Do not phone home to verify.** Real-time status queries create a central point of failure and leak the verifier's behavior: "every OCSP query reveals which sites a user is visiting" [hardman-proga-phonehome]; `sda.md` states the same rule harder — "verification must succeed from the credential and key-state alone, and any registry or log is an issuance-time and audit-time convenience, never a verification-time dependency" (`sda.md:119`). **Ours:** an arcviz that resolves a schema `$id` over the network, fetches a remote OCA overlay, or loads an image referenced by an ACDC has (i) made verification depend on a network call the architecture forbids, and (ii) told a third party which credential is being viewed and when. Both are disqualifying, and (ii) is a correlation leak arcviz would be creating, not merely displaying — see (d).

## (d) Where a DAG visualization does harm

This is the uncomfortable section, and it should stay uncomfortable. arcviz's core function — drawing lines between identifiers so relationships are legible at a glance — is, in the vocabulary of this corpus, correlation. Hardman has written a paper against making correlation easy, and arcviz makes it easy. That is not a reason not to build arcviz. It is a reason to build it with the harms named.

### The argument as Hardman makes it

He grants the premise and then refuses the conclusion. "Whether correlation happens" is the wrong question; the useful ones are: "How expensive will the correlation be? / Exactly who is going to correlate you? / When are they going to correlate you? / How completely are they going to correlate you? / How much control will you retain?" [hardman-wbca-questions]. Each has a direct reading against a DAG viewer.

**Expense.** The harm is in shifting the economics, not in any single act of correlation: "what happens if we make correlation far easier, far more accurate, and far cheaper to maintain? [...] They shift the economics of correlation, trivializing the assembly of rich profiles" [hardman-wbca-expense]. **Ours:** a DAG view is, by design, a device for making correlation cheap. The work an analyst would do by hand — reading SAIDs, matching issuer AIDs across credentials, reconstructing a chain — is precisely the work arcviz automates. Every affordance that reduces the cost of joining two identifiers is on the wrong side of this argument by default, and has to earn its place.

**Who.** "the shifty guy who begs for Alice's verifiable business card at the bar has access to the same lookup power as the HR department [...] It doesn't matter that Alice only discloses her first name at the bar; the shifty guy gets a perfect correlator anyway" [hardman-wbca-who]. **Ours:** a good open-source viewer is a democratizing tool, and democratizing a correlation capability is exactly the harm named here. arcviz cannot restrict who runs it. What it can decide is what the *default* view does without being asked.

**Completeness, and the negative inference.** This is the passage that should govern arcviz's cross-credential features. "The scope of correlation and the scope of her credential use are co-equal by design" [hardman-wbca-scope] — and the consequence is not just more knowledge but a different *kind*: "it's now possible to search for Alice's correlators and know with perfect accuracy whether she used a credential in a given context, *even if the answer is no*. This lets parties with a wealth of data prove facts that are only probabilities today. Do we want a world where an impeached president can identify a whistleblower by quickly and perfectly disproving all other candidates?" [hardman-wbca-negative]. **Ours:** an arcviz feature that renders a *complete* graph — "here are all the credentials chained under this issuer", "this AID appears in none of your other loaded credentials" — supplies negative facts, and negative facts are the whistleblower-identification mechanism in that quote. A view that is honestly incomplete is safer than one that is completely honest.

**Control, and the knowledge/proof gap.** "This is true, but it conflates knowledge with proof. If Bob wants to dox or bully or blackmail Alice, there's a world of difference between making unsubstantiated assertions about her, and being able to prove them" [hardman-wbca-control]. **Ours:** an arcviz export — a PNG, an SVG, a copied JSON blob, a shared permalink — converts a viewing into a portable artifact. If the export carries the underlying signed material, it is transferred *proof* and the holder loses the control Hardman is defending. If it does not, it is a screenshot that asserts without proving, which is a different harm (a forgeable claim of verification). Export is not a neutral convenience feature; it sits on a real boundary, and this stream does not think it has a clean answer.

**And the definition of privacy the argument rests on.** "not that we have constant anonymity, but that we can create a few private spaces. 99% of the space in our lives might be public, but protecting the remaining 1% is really important" [hardman-wbca-privacy-def]. **Ours:** the failure mode is not "arcviz reveals something already public." It is "arcviz eliminates the last separation."

### The sharpest formulation, from `if.md`

`wbca.md` is from 2020 and argues in terms of correlating versus non-correlating credentials, a framing that predates ACDC. `if.md` restates the same commitment in current terms, and its sentence is the one this project should hang on a wall:

> "Revealing that two facets are one person is Cecilia's call to make, one relationship at a time. In a system that works as designed, this is never a default, never an inference a stranger is meant to draw, never a leak the software condones behind her back." [hardman-if-owner]

And the reason each facet gets its own opaque identifier: "A single identifier worn across facets would be exactly the correlation handle that isolation exists to deny: any two parties holding it could compare notes and collapse the faces back into one person" [hardman-if-anchors].

**Ours, stated without softening: an edge in an arcviz DAG is an inference a stranger is meant to draw, drawn by default, by software, on the holder's behalf and without asking.** That is a sentence-by-sentence match to what [hardman-if-owner] says a system that works as designed never does. It does not follow that arcviz should not draw edges — the edges are in the credential, the holder disclosed them, and refusing to render disclosed structure would be its own dishonesty. What follows is that the *set* of edges arcviz draws is a design decision with a moral weight the drawing code does not advertise, and that arcviz owes an answer to the question "which of these edges did the holder actually disclose to *this* verifier, and which am I inferring by joining sessions?"

### What arcviz must not make easy — this stream's recommendations

Marked "Ours" throughout; these are inferences from the quotes above, not positions Hardman has taken about arcviz.

1. **Cross-credential joining must not be a default view.** Loading two credentials into one arcviz session and having it silently draw the AID they share is the cheapest possible correlation, performed without being asked. If arcviz offers it, it should be an explicit, named act — and per [hardman-intent-boundaries-logs], the act should leave a trace, not just happen.
2. **Never render an inferred edge with the same weight as a disclosed one.** AGENTS.md's four confusable states are all about a *node*. This adds a fifth confusion at the *edge* level: disclosed-in-this-presentation, disclosed-in-a-different-presentation-I-happen-to-have, and inferred-by-identifier-equality are three different things and must not share a stroke.
3. **No negative claims.** "This identifier appears in no other credential", "no other children found", an empty-result state that reads as exhaustive — these supply the negative facts of [hardman-wbca-negative]. Absence of evidence in a partially-disclosed graph is never evidence of absence, and the UI must not let it look like it is.
4. **No persistence across sessions by default.** A cache that accumulates every credential arcviz has ever rendered is a correlation corpus, built by us, sitting on the verifier's machine. If there is caching, its scope and lifetime should be visible and clearable.
5. **No network fetch triggered by credential content.** Schema `$id` resolution, remote OCA overlays, remote images: each tells a third party which credential is being viewed, when, and by whom [hardman-proga-phonehome], and each violates the open-loop rule [hardman-sda-openloop]. This is arcviz *creating* a correlation signal that did not previously exist, which is worse than displaying one that did.
6. **Treat export as a boundary crossing, not a convenience.** Per the knowledge/proof gap [hardman-wbca-control].

### The one place where the corpus lets arcviz off, and it is narrower than it looks

Selective disclosure has a hard limit in an open-loop architecture. "Choosing open-loop *forces* full disclosure: a stranger deciding whether to honor an act must see *every* constraint on it, because it cannot ask the issuer what was meant — there is no graduated, ask-as-you-go disclosure when there is no one to ask" [hardman-sda-openloop]. **Ours:** this is worth stating carefully, because it is easy to over-read. It says a verifier evaluating an *authorization* must see all constraints on that authorization; it does not license a viewer to show everything it can reach. arcviz's job is to render what was disclosed, not to help a verifier reach further. The passage constrains what a verifier may *demand*; it does not expand what a viewer should *offer*.

### (d.5) Testing `svce.md`'s four metaphors against disclosure states — they do not map

The brief asked whether sentries, confessionals, vaults, and envelopes map onto disclosure states. Tested directly: they do not, and the paper itself warns against the stretch. Hardman's own caveat is that metaphors "may also be applied too literally or too broadly, and they can imply color that is unintended" [hardman-svce-caveat].

The four are a taxonomy of **protocol families distinguished by their relationship to time and session** — OIDC/sentries and CHAPI/confessionals are real-time and session-bound, DWN/vaults decouple the owner's and accessor's timing through a third party, DIDComm/envelopes are fully asynchronous (`svce.md:60`, `svce.md:78`, `svce.md:102`, `svce.md:124`). They classify *how a credential moves between parties*. Disclosure states classify *what a verifier can see about a field*. Those are orthogonal, and a forced mapping (vault ≈ undisclosed? envelope ≈ redacted?) would produce four metaphors that each mean something the paper did not say. Reported as a negative result.

What `svce.md` does contribute is the **xeno-interop** discipline: where two adjacent tools solve genuinely different problems, the work is to draw the boundary crisply — "How do we recognize and delineate boundaries between our problem domain and a neighbor's as crisply as possible, so we are differentiated and collaborative across the boundary" [hardman-svce-xeno] — and the accompanying warning that overlap is inevitable and the real failure is to "misunderstand, misdescribe, undervalue, or mistarget the sweet spot of each" [hardman-svce-mistarget]. **Ours:** this is the frame for arcviz's relationship to `entviz` (identifier visualization), to OCA (presentation overlays), and to wallets. The question to ask at each seam is not "should arcviz also do this" but "where is the boundary, and is it crisp."

### Power asymmetry: who the interface serves

`bdlp.md` names credential tooling directly. "we assume that digital credentials are held by people with the goal of making issuers and verifiers happy" [hardman-bdlp-assumption], and "Standardizing credential exchange as a client-server interaction marginalizes people as mere holders, and makes big desks bigger" [hardman-bdlp-holders]. The broader claim is that solving authenticity, confidentiality and privacy is not enough — "*ACP can (and will) be exploited, given enough distortion in power*" [hardman-bdlp-acp].

The paper's four diagnostic questions [hardman-bdlp-questions] can be asked of an interface as readily as of a protocol. **Ours**, asked of arcviz:

- *Which direction do terms flow?* If arcviz is embedded in a verifier's console, the holder never sees the interface that renders their credential and has no say in what it emphasizes.
- *Who gets to report errors?* If a credential fails to verify, arcviz will say so to the institution. The person the credential is about learns nothing, and cannot contest a rendering they never see. **This is the single most actionable asymmetry in arcviz's design**: a viewer that reports "unverified" to a verifier while the subject stands there unable to see what was checked reproduces exactly the pattern `bdlp.md` describes.
- *Whose view of interaction state is authoritative?* The verifier's screen becomes the record of what was disclosed.
- *Who defines the interface?* Us — and per `bdlp.md`, that is a responsibility, not a neutral fact.

**Ours:** arcviz should be usable by the subject of a credential, on their own device, to see what a verifier sees. Not as a feature request, but because the alternative is a tool that is only ever pointed at people. `sss.md` gives the standard to hold: self-sovereign security includes that "no other entity on the face of the planet can correlate the two of us by the identifiers we're using" [hardman-sss-uncorrelated].

### Handling hostile content

`acp.md` is consumer advice, but two lines set a threshold worth adopting. "It's safe to read text. Anything more should require very strong confidence about context" [hardman-acp-readtext], and the "don't call me, I'll call you" rule: a legitimate counterparty can always be reached by starting the interaction from your own side, "using something OTHER than a special link they give you" [hardman-acp-dontcallme]. **Ours:** an ACDC arriving from a counterparty is unsolicited inbound content with attacker-controlled strings in it. Rendering it as text is the safe floor. Following a URI it contains, executing anything in it, or letting it initiate an outbound request is the escalation the rule warns against — and it converges with the no-phone-home conclusion of (c) from an entirely different direction.

## (e) Candidate affordances and the boundaries that sit on them

A first draft of the Phase 4 inventory, written in the composite vocabulary of (b): the **encompassing telos** is a Syntelos path naming the interaction the viewer serves; the **act** is the `sda.md` (effect, state-kind) coordinate of the affordance itself; the **treatment** is one of Hardman's four principles — recognize, move, peek, never sneak.

**Everything in this table is ours.** Hardman has not written about arcviz. The vocabulary is his; the assignments are inferences by this stream, offered as a starting point for Phase 4 to argue with.

| # | Affordance | Encompassing telos | Act (effect · state-kind) | Boundary? | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Render a disclosed field's value | `/govern/identify` | observe · information | No | **Move.** No boundary — [hardman-intent-boundaries-noboundary] warns against inventing one. Confirming a disclosed field is friction, not safety |
| 2 | Render a field the holder did not disclose | `/govern/identify` | observe · information | **Yes** — this is arcviz's founding boundary | **Move.** The four states must be distinguishable *before* the viewer forms a belief, not corrected after. Per (c), the not-established side must be as prominent as the established side |
| 3 | Expand a compacted block (SAID → contents) where the material is in hand | `/govern/audit` | observe · information | No | **Move.** Signify that the block was compacted, not absent |
| 4 | Fetch material not in hand to expand a node | `/govern/audit` | observe · information + **create · record** (a network event at a third party) | **Yes** | **Confirm.** A fetch tells someone else what is being looked at. Per [hardman-proga-phonehome] this is a leak arcviz creates. Default off |
| 5 | Render an issuer/holder AID (entviz pill, alias) | `/govern/identify` | observe · information | No | **Move.** Per [hardman-if-anchors] the AID *is* a facet anchor; per stream 1E, its form must not read as proof |
| 6 | Draw an edge disclosed within one presentation | `/govern/identify` | observe · relationship | No | **Move.** The holder disclosed the chain. Render it |

If offered at all, it is explicit, named, distinctly styled, and logged |
| 8 | Show verification status of a signature | `/govern/audit` | observe · record | No, if honest | **Move.** [hardman-proga-uielement]: a glyph not backed by a check arcviz performed is the condemned pattern |
| 9 | Show a chain as complete / show an empty result | `/govern/audit` | observe · record | **Yes** | **Never sneak.** Supplies negative facts [hardman-wbca-negative]. State the bound of what was searched, never assert exhaustiveness |
| 10 | Copy an identifier or field to clipboard | `/govern/identify` | create · record | Low | **Peek.** Reversible, low-stakes; a brief non-blocking notice fits [hardman-intent-monograph-peek] |
| 11 | Export the graph (image, JSON, permalink) | varies | create · record, possibly create · commitment | **Yes** | **Confirm.** The knowledge/proof gap [hardman-wbca-control]. Whether the export carries transferable proof changes which harm applies, and it must be stated at the point of export |
| 12 | Persist / cache viewed credentials across sessions | none — arcviz's own act | create · record | **Yes** | **Confirm**, and default off. A cache is a correlation corpus we built |
| 13 | Search or filter across everything loaded | `/govern/audit` | observe · information | **Yes** | **Confirm.** Search across separately-obtained presentations is affordance 7 by another route |
| 14 | Resolve a schema `$id` or fetch an OCA overlay / remote image | `/govern/identify` | observe · information + **create · record** at a third party | **Yes** | **Never sneak.** Credential content initiating outbound requests is what [hardman-acp-dontcallme] and [hardman-proga-phonehome] both forbid. | 15 | Show the raw bytes behind any rendered element | `/govern/audit` | observe · information | No | **Move.** The antidote to the JPEG-of-a-JPEG problem in (c) |

| 17 | Render a credential whose data is stale but was valid when captured | `/govern/audit` | observe · record | **Yes** | **Recognize first.** The LAPSED case [hardman-proga-lapsed]; the fifth confusable state, and the one most likely to render as *fine* |

Three observations about the table rather than about any row. Almost every affordance is `observe`, which per [hardman-sda-gate] would normally be automatic and ungated — and that is exactly why arcviz is hard: the harms concentrate not in what arcviz changes but in what it *reveals*, and the gate function was not built for that axis (see (g), Q1). The rows where an act turns out to be `create` rather than `observe` — 4, 7, 11, 12, 14 — are the rows carrying real boundaries, which is a useful sign that the coordinate is doing work. And rows 7 and 12 are the only two whose encompassing telos is *none*: they are acts arcviz performs on its own initiative, serving no interaction the holder entered, which is the strongest structural argument this stream has for treating them as the dangerous ones.

## (f) What arcviz inherits

Positions arcviz takes as settled unless it states a reason to depart, each with the source that settles it.

1. **The four intent principles**, as stated in `intent-monograph.md` §4 and `intent-boundaries.md`: recognize boundaries, consider moving rather than crossing, consider peeking across, never sneak across. The characteristic violation they all guard against has one shape — the system "substitutes a guess [...] for knowledge" and then acts on it [hardman-intent-monograph-substitute].
2. **A boundary is relative to an observer's ignorance**, not a fixed line in the code [hardman-intent-monograph-def]. arcviz has at least three observers — the verifier operating it, the holder presenting to them, and the subject the credential is about — and they face boundaries in different places [hardman-intent-boundaries-relative]. An affordance inventory that assumes a single user is wrong by construction.
3. **Inventing a boundary is also an error** [hardman-intent-boundaries-noboundary]. Confirmation dialogs on disclosed data are friction with no safety, and this corpus is as hostile to them as to sneaking.
4. **Describing intent more strongly than the evidence supports is sneaking**, in a log, a label, or a record — and so is keeping no trace of a crossing [hardman-intent-boundaries-logs]. arcviz must never label a rendering "verified" when it performed a weaker check, and boundary crossings should leave a trace.
5. **Software has multiple stakeholders, and concealing whose intent it optimizes for is sneaking** [hardman-intent-boundaries-stakeholders]. arcviz will be commissioned by verifiers and used on subjects; that should be said in the documentation, not discovered.
6. **Intent is typed by telos, not by mechanism** [hardman-syntelos-telos] — the hierarchy, the parameterization discipline, the consent-distinguishability test, and the eight domains as extracted in (b).
7. **Acts are typed by effect over a named state-kind, and gates are derived from what an act does** [hardman-sda-effect, hardman-sda-statekind, hardman-sda-gate]. A gate is not an accusation, and arcviz's confirmations should be worded accordingly.
8. **Assurance is multi-axis and degraded honestly**: reference and control assurance separately [hardman-proga-axes]; every level states what it does not establish alongside what it does; and staleness is its own state [hardman-proga-lapsed].
9. **A checkmark not backed by a check the software performed is a lie** [hardman-proga-uielement].
10. **No verification-time phone-home** [hardman-proga-phonehome, hardman-sda-openloop], and no outbound request initiated by credential content [hardman-acp-dontcallme].
11. **Correlation is a variable to be managed, not a fate to be accepted**, along the five axes of [hardman-wbca-questions]; and negative facts are a distinct harm [hardman-wbca-negative].
12. **Only the owner links facets** [hardman-if-owner]. This is the constraint the DAG most directly threatens. A credential has an *affinity* for one facet but not ownership by it — "the pull is a tendency, not a law", and pinning a credential to one facet by fiat "would discard information the credential never committed to" [hardman-if-affinity] — so arcviz must not infer a facet grouping the credential did not assert, in either direction.
13. **A trust context is portable, and that portability is the point** [hardman-sss-portable]. **Ours:** arcviz is a viewer, not a home. It must not become the silo that owns a rendering of a relationship, which is what a persistent cross-session cache (affordance 12) would make it.
14. **Power asymmetry is a trust failure even when ACP is solved** [hardman-bdlp-acp], and credential tooling built only for institutions is named as an instance of it [hardman-bdlp-holders].
15. **Boundaries between adjacent tools should be crisp, not merged** [hardman-svce-xeno] — the frame for arcviz's seams with entviz, OCA, and wallets.
16. **Category is checkable; sincerity is not** [hardman-sda-sincerity]. arcviz cannot know why someone is looking.

## (g) Unresolved questions for Daniel

**Q1. The axis arcviz sits on is one you have named as open.** `sda.md` §6 ends by flagging an axis the model does not specify: "there is an *outbound* mirror: what a delegate may *reveal about its principal*, to whom, and unlinkably. [...] This attribute-disclosure axis [...] is the natural home for purpose-limitation, and this paper names it without claiming to have specified it" [hardman-sda-outbound]. That is arcviz's entire problem. The gate function of `sda.md` §4 governs inbound authority and treats `observe` as automatic; arcviz's harms are all outbound disclosure, where no derived gate exists. **Is there work in progress on this axis that arcviz should be tracking, or is arcviz expected to develop a first cut of it?** This stream thinks the affordance inventory is, in effect, a partial specification of that axis for one class of tool, and it would be better to know that deliberately than to discover it.

**Q2. Where does arcviz land on affordance 7 — joining separately-obtained presentations?** [hardman-if-owner] says linking facets is never an inference a stranger is meant to draw. A verifier holding two ACDCs with a shared issuer AID will see the join whether or not arcviz draws it. Options: refuse (arcviz never joins across presentations); offer explicitly with a distinct visual treatment and a trace; or offer it as an ordinary feature on the grounds that the correlator is already in the verifier's hands. This stream leans to the second, but this is a values call, not a technical one, and it is the highest-stakes single decision in the inventory.

**Q3. Is `wbca.md` (2020) still your position given ACDC, and how do you read ACDC against it?** The paper's framing is correlating versus non-correlating credentials, from an AnonCreds-era vantage. ACDC's SAID chaining is a perfect correlator in the paper's own sense — the same value on every use, one equality test — even though ACDC has graduated and partial disclosure that the 2020 framing does not contemplate. `wbca.md:101` promises "in a future post I'll explore why correlating credentials have benefits and should play a role in an overall strategy." **Was that post written?** If so it is directly on point for arcviz and this stream did not find it in the corpus; if not, the question of how a correlating-by-construction credential format squares with the 2020 argument is one arcviz would benefit from your answer to.

**Q4. Who is arcviz's primary user?** `bdlp.md` argues that credential tooling built to make issuers and verifiers happy marginalizes people as mere holders [hardman-bdlp-assumption, hardman-bdlp-holders]. If arcviz is a verifier-console component, it is an instance of the pattern that paper warns about. If it is also meant to be run by holders and subjects, that changes several rows of the inventory — most sharply the error-reporting asymmetry in (d). This stream assumed both, and would rather be corrected now than in Phase 4.

**Q5. Export.** This stream could not find a clean position. Carrying transferable proof in an export removes the holder's control over resharing [hardman-wbca-control]; carrying no proof produces a forgeable artifact that asserts verification without it [hardman-proga-uielement]. Both are bad in different directions, and "no export" is not obviously survivable in a real tool.

**Q6. Should the papers be vendored into `refs/captures/`?** See the capture note at the top of `sources.papers-intent.yaml`. They are your own open-access work so nothing forbids it, but locators here are line numbers into a live working tree that will drift. Vendoring pins them; not vendoring keeps the repo smaller and avoids a stale copy. This stream took no action either way.
