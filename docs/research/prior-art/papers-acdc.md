## (b) Deep-read sections

### What must a display assert, and what must it never assert? (Q1)

**A SAID proves content identity, nothing about freshness, membership, or timing.** `acdc-and-mtc.md` states this as the central contrast with Merkle Tree Certificates: an ACDC SAID's "core commitment object" is "SAID: a self-addressing identifier that commits to the content and structure of a claim," proving "that the disclosed view corresponds to a specific underlying claim and verifies equivalently" (`acdc-and-mtc.md:48-51`, comparative-map table). The paper's summary line is blunt: "A Merkle inclusion proof establishes placement in a history. A SAID establishes identity of content." (`acdc-and-mtc.md:86`). arcviz's entviz pill for a SAID must not visually imply "this was checked as current" or "this is a member of an audited set" — a SAID pill is a content-identity claim only; freshness/membership are separate axes that need their own indicators.

**A bare signature proves almost nothing about *when* it was made relative to key compromise.** `was.md` is the sharpest source here. Its central claim: "the secret upon which they depend (a private key) can be compromised or revoked, but *simple signatures are difficult to sequence relative to a compromise or revocation event*. An inability to reason about sequence enables retrograde attacks." (`was.md:18`). The paper walks through why even "obvious" mitigations fail, then states KERI's fix as a one-sentence formula: "Keep tamper-evident records that can prove how a given signing event relates in time to changes in the associated key state." (`was.md:70-72`). This is the definition of an *anchored signature* — and it means a naive "green checkmark = signature validates" UI (the X.509/OCSP-style current-moment-only check the paper calls "a pyrrhic victory," `was.md:51-52`) is actively misleading for ACDC evidence, because it collapses "valid now" and "valid then" into one signal when KERI is specifically designed to keep them separable.

**What an anchored signature adds, mechanically:** `keri-primer.md` gives the verifier's actual procedure — "extract the issuer's AID," "retrieve the issuer's KEL from a witness," "traverse the KEL to the sequence number specified in the signature," "verify that the *digest* of the ACDC is present in that event" (`keri-primer.md:196-200`). It contrasts this with "paired signatures" (JWT, X.509, W3C VC `proof` blocks) where "the two go together because the format says so" with no independent, KEL-anchored provable association (`keri-primer.md:190-194`). It also quotes NIST SP 800-102 that a signed message alone "provides no assurance that the private key was used to sign the message at that time" (`keri-primer.md:202`) — the same point `was.md` makes at length. **arcviz's display should therefore be able to distinguish, visually, "signature validates" (paired/bare) from "signature is anchored to key state as of event N" (KERI-anchored)** — these are not the same claim and conflating them reproduces exactly the ambiguity both papers argue against.

**What a witness adds — and does not add.** `keri-primer.md` is explicit that a witness is not a CA: "A witness makes no such assertion. A witness simply stores and serves events." (`keri-primer.md:153`). Its function is availability and duplicity detection, performed by watchers who compare what different witnesses report: "If Hash X and Hash Y differ, the watcher has detected duplicity." (`keri-primer.md:159`). `heartbeat-and-witnesses.md` reinforces the same point from a different angle: witness rejection of a conflicting event at the same sequence number is "cryptographic proof that the controller is compromised or dishonest," not a liveness-based "winner" selection (`heartbeat-and-witnesses.md:40-42`). **A witness badge in arcviz must not read as an endorsement or trust signal** (the CA-icon mental model); it should read as "this event is served/replicated here," with duplicity status as a wholly separate, and much more consequential, signal.

**Revocation/status is event reconciliation, not a live query in the OCSP sense.** `acdc-and-mtc.md`'s table states revocation is "Expressed as events in key state or registries, reconciled over time" and that "the verifier's task is to reconcile events, not to query a live service" (`acdc-and-mtc.md:54, 134`), and that "verification can be offline, provided the verifier has access to the relevant events and receipts" (`acdc-and-mtc.md:132`). This is in some tension with `keri-primer.md`'s claim, in its closing summary, that "ACDCs anchored in TELs allow for real-time status checks without privacy leaks" (`keri-primer.md:269`) — see Contradictions/tensions below.

### Edge and chain semantics (Q2)

**Edges are typed, directional, and can carry native attributes — not bare boolean links.** `acdc-vc-diff.md` is explicit that ACDCs "use a property graph model, where edges are first-class objects that hold attributes... The weight (`w`), operator (`o`), schema (`s`), and target node (`n`) are native fields" (`acdc-vc-diff.md:61`), contrasted with RDF triple-reification "bloat" needed to attach the same properties in a W3C VC context (`acdc-vc-diff.md:51-59`). `keri-primer.md`'s worked supply-chain example makes the direction and semantics concrete: each ACDC's edge "points back to" its predecessor by SAID (ore → refiner → manufacturer → retailer), and a consumer's verifier "follows all edges in the entire evidence graph, verifying signatures, anchors, and non-revocation status" of every ancestor (`keri-primer.md:206-215`). The chain is explicitly a DAG: "using ACDCs, you can build a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of arbitrarily rich semantics" (`keri-primer.md:206`). **This confirms arcviz's DAG assumption and additionally licenses per-edge weight/operator metadata as a real protocol-level concept**, not a UI invention — if arcviz wants to show edge strength or type, there is a spec-level field to bind that to.

**Chains prove transitive verifiability, not transitive endorsement of truth by the last issuer.** `keri-primer.md`'s consumer, scanning the QR code, "gains *transitive trust*; they know that the metal in the phone actually came from a conflict-free mine, not because the manufacturer said so, but because the cryptographic chain leads back to the mine's original issuance" (`keri-primer.md:215`) — and, importantly, "no party in the middle can swap out a bad component for a good one without breaking the chain" (`keri-primer.md:215`). What the chain proves is *tamper-evident provenance of the assertion graph*, not that any given upstream claim is factually true — each node's claim is only as good as its own issuer's credibility, which the graph makes traceable but does not itself certify.

**Verifiable credentials, including ACDCs, are "open-loop."** `sda.md` frames this precisely: "Verifiable credentials are **open-loop**. The entire point is that a third party who is *not* the issuer can verify authority *without* consulting the issuer — no callback, no shared state — from the credential and the actor's key-state alone." (`sda.md:115`). This matters for how arcviz should present a fully-traversed chain: verification success means "the artifact and its ancestors are internally self-consistent as checked against key state," not "the issuer was consulted and stands behind this right now."

**Foreign (non-ACDC) evidence can terminate a chain edge.** `keri-primer.md` notes "ACDCs even offer a path to include foreign evidence types such as signed PDFs, scientific measurements, W3C VCs, X509 certificates, biometric readings, and so forth" (`keri-primer.md:206`). These foreign nodes are not bound by ACDC's own strict rule that "issuers MUST be identified with an AID" (`sdjwt-acdc.md:39`) — an X.509 cert or plain PDF at the end of a chain has none of KERI's anchoring, witness, or pre-rotation guarantees. **arcviz needs a way to visually flag a chain node whose verifiability class is different from its ACDC neighbors** — this is a real open design question (see §e).

### SAID mechanics relevant to display (Q3)

**Standard SAIDs assume canonicalizable, rewritable structured data.** `bes.md`'s abstract states the CESR SAID mechanism "assume[s] data structures that can be canonically serialized and safely rewritten after hashing" (`bes.md:7`), and describes the base algorithm: reserve a placeholder in the "d" field, canonicalize, hash, and overwrite the placeholder with the computed SAID (`bes.md:22`).

**Two additional, named SAID variants exist for opaque (non-JSON/CBOR) byte streams, and they are not interchangeable with a standard SAID or with each other.** A **bytewise SAID (bSAID)** is embedded directly in file content at a delimiter-marked insertion point, found by a generic byte scan; it works for formats that permit post-save rewriting (`bes.md:53`, `bes.md:22-91`). An **externalized SAID (xSAID)** is instead carried in the filename, for formats (compressed, encrypted, or offset-table-dependent, e.g. `.docx`, PDF) where in-place rewriting after save would break internal structure or integrity checks (`bes.md:110-128`). Both are explicitly named as distinct from a plain SAID: "a SAID produced by it a *bytewise SAID* (or *bSAID* for short)" (`bes.md:53`); "a SAID produced by it an *externalized SAID* (or *xSAID* for short)" (`bes.md:121`).

**The tamper-evidence guarantee differs by kind.** The threat model section states plainly: "Any change to the byte stream after saidification necessarily produces a different SAID and is therefore treated as a new version of the artifact" (`bes.md:152`) — but for an xSAID, tamper-evidence depends on the filename being preserved and matching a regex constraint embedded in the file content; a naive rename silently breaks that binding unless the constraint is checked (`bes.md:119, 126`). **Relevance to arcviz:** if the entviz SAID pill is ever attached to a node representing foreign/opaque evidence (a PDF affidavit, a photo, per the Q2 foreign-evidence case above), the pill's implicit "this hash was computed the standard, spec-default way" assumption may not hold — a bSAID or xSAID has a different verification procedure and a different failure mode (an xSAID's binding can be broken by mere renaming, with no content change at all). This is worth flagging to Daniel as an open UI question rather than assuming today's entviz pill design already covers it.

### ACDC vs. W3C VC vs. SD-JWT (Q4)

`acdc-vc-diff.md` and `sdjwt-acdc.md` are Daniel's own account of terrain the completed W3C audit (`docs/research/prior-art/w3c.md`) also covers, from the W3C side. Key points, condensed:

- **Lossy vs. lossless is the organizing frame**, not a side observation: "W3C VCs derive their validity from a reference to their proximate issuer only... The VC itself contains no cryptographic link back through this chain" (`acdc-vc-diff.md:35`), vs. ACDC's SAID-chained graph that "preserve[s] this graph natively, and essentially for free" (`acdc-vc-diff.md:39`).
- **Terminology is a deliberate departure, not an oversight.** Daniel explicitly rejects "selective disclosure" for ACDC: "The decentralized identity community began using this term (unwisely, IMO)... SD-JWTs built on the term. I prefer the ACDC term ['graduated disclosure'], because it won't confuse and put off people in the regulatory community." (`sdjwt-acdc.md:31`). This is a stated authorial position arcviz should follow in its own copy when describing ACDC-specific behavior (see §c).
- **Identifier strictness differs.** "SD-JWTs allow an issuer and a holder to be identified with a DID, a URL, a URN, or some other identifier scheme. ACDCs are stricter: issuers MUST be identified with an AID" (`sdjwt-acdc.md:38-39`).
- **Chaining is the structural difference.** SD-JWT's "single, standalone envelope... is the unit of signing, data exchange, and verification" (`sdjwt-acdc.md:59`), which is why "SD-JWTs will need registries of trusted issuers" (`sdjwt-acdc.md:63`), whereas "ACDCs prove the bona fides of issuers by chaining to other ACDCs" (`sdjwt-acdc.md:65`) — the same SAID-linking mechanism used for holder credentials is reused for issuer qualification, with no separate registry infrastructure.
- **Compaction goes further than either SD-JWT or a plain W3C VC.** "ACDCs can elide data that's already known from another context, allowing compaction down to something as small as a single identifier (hash) of about 44 bytes for an entire data structure of arbitrary size (multiple KB), or an entire tree of data structures (possibly, MB)." (`sdjwt-acdc.md:72`).

### Graduated disclosure (Q5)

`keri-primer.md` describes the mechanism: a payload "structured into sections... the issuer creates a random salt and a digest for each section... the holder presents the credential to a verifier, they can use a Merkle-proof style strategy that reveal[s] only the [needed section], with other sections represented only by their hash" (`keri-primer.md:219-221`). `acdc-and-mtc.md` states the equivalence property that matters most for display: "A fully expanded ACDC and a partially disclosed one verify the same way, lead to the same status checks, and anchor to the same underlying claim." (`acdc-and-mtc.md:104`). This licenses arcviz's expand/collapse affordance as a faithful UI mirror of a real protocol property (same verification outcome regardless of disclosure level) rather than a cosmetic simplification.

**A genuinely hard case for arcviz's absent/undisclosed/redacted distinction:** `keri-primer.md`'s privacy section states that under GDPR-style erasure, "When a user requests data deletion, the payload is destroyed. The hash remains in the log to preserve the integrity of the chain, but without the payload, the hash is irreversible." (`keri-primer.md:223`). A verifier looking at that hash cannot, from the artifact alone, distinguish "the holder chose not to disclose this section in this presentation" from "this section's payload was permanently erased and no longer exists anywhere." Both present identically: a bare digest with no recoverable payload. This is a real, spec-level ambiguity, not an arcviz oversight — see §e.

## (c) What arcviz inherits

These are positions Daniel has already taken in his own papers; arcviz's design should be consistent with them absent a stated reason to depart.

1. **"Graduated disclosure," not "selective disclosure," is the correct term for ACDC's own mechanism**, per Daniel's explicit, reasoned preference (`sdjwt-acdc.md:29-31`). arcviz's UI copy and internal naming should use "graduated disclosure" for ACDC-native behavior; "selective disclosure" is fine only when describing SD-JWT/other formats by their own name.
2. **A SAID is a content-identity claim only.** It says nothing about freshness, currency, or membership in an audited set (`acdc-and-mtc.md:48-51, 86`). The entviz SAID pill must not visually borrow the connotations of a "verified" or "current" badge.
3. **Anchored vs. bare/paired signatures are different claims and must be visually distinguishable.** A bare signature check ("does this validate right now") is what `was.md` calls a "pyrrhic victory" (`was.md:51-52`); an anchored signature additionally proves *when relative to key-state* the signing occurred (`was.md:70-72`, `keri-primer.md:196-202`). If arcviz shows any "signed/verified" indicator, it needs at minimum a way to mark whether the anchoring check was performed and passed, not just the bare cryptographic validity of the signature bytes.
4. **A witness/watcher indicator is not a trust or endorsement badge.** Witnesses store and serve events; they assert nothing about the truth of what they store (`keri-primer.md:153`). Duplicity detection is the meaningful signal, and it is binary and severe (compromise/dishonesty), not a graded liveness score (`heartbeat-and-witnesses.md:40-42`).
5. **Edges are typed and can carry native weight/operator/schema/target metadata** (`acdc-vc-diff.md:61`), so an arcviz edge-rendering design that only supports "linked / not linked" is under-using what the format actually permits.
6. **Chain verification proves tamper-evident provenance, not upstream factual truth.** "No party in the middle can swap out a bad component" (`keri-primer.md:215`) is a claim about integrity of the assertion graph, not a claim that any node's content is correct.
7. **Not every SAID is computed the same way.** bSAIDs and xSAIDs exist for opaque, non-ACDC evidence at the edges of a chain and carry different tamper-evidence guarantees than a standard structural SAID (`bes.md:53, 121, 152`).

## (d) Contradictions and tensions

**Within this corpus: "real-time" vs. "reconciled over time" revocation.** `keri-primer.md`'s closing summary claims "ACDCs anchored in TELs allow for real-time status checks without privacy leaks" (`keri-primer.md:269`), while `acdc-and-mtc.md`'s comparative table, written five months later, describes KERI/ACDC revocation as "Expressed as events in key state or registries, reconciled over time" and states plainly that "the verifier's task is to reconcile events, not to query a live service" (`acdc-and-mtc.md:54, 134`). These are not flatly incompatible — "real-time" plausibly means "as soon as the revocation event is witnessed, any subsequent query reflects it," while "reconciled over time" describes the verifier's *process* of replaying/catching-up on events — but the two papers pick different emphases that could mislead a UI designer into either overpromising instant freshness or underselling how current a status check actually is. **arcviz should not adopt either paper's phrasing uncritically**; it needs its own precise statement of what "checked as of" a status indicator means, and should treat this as a place where two of Daniel's own papers are in tension rather than a settled fact to inherit wholesale.

**Between this corpus and the completed W3C audit: terminology divergence is deliberate, not an error to reconcile.** The W3C audit (`w3c.md`) necessarily uses W3C's own vocabulary ("selective disclosure," per `w3c.md:66`'s captured VCRM quote). This is not a disagreement between sources — it is two ecosystems using different words for related-but-distinct mechanisms, and Daniel's papers make the divergence intentional (`sdjwt-acdc.md:31`). Flagging this explicitly so arcviz doesn't accidentally normalize the terms into synonyms in shared UI copy, when the underlying mechanisms (JWT-envelope selective disclosure vs. graph-native graduated disclosure) genuinely differ in what they let a verifier know, per `sdjwt-acdc.md:58-67`.

**Between this corpus and the OCA audit: OCA has no notion of "erased," and neither, fully, does this corpus.** The OCA report (`oca.md:90-94`) already flagged that OCA's overlay taxonomy has no counterpart for signaling "a hole in the data" at all — it assumes every attribute in scope is present. This corpus goes one step further and surfaces a case OCA's report didn't reach: even within ACDC/KERI's own model, "undisclosed" (holder's choice, this presentation) and "erased" (payload permanently destroyed, GDPR-style) are structurally indistinguishable to a verifier — both are just a bare digest with no recoverable payload (`keri-primer.md:223`). **This sharpens, and slightly complicates, AGENTS.md's own framing** ("never letting absent, undisclosed, redacted and unverified be mistaken for one another") — for at least one real case, the underlying protocol itself may not carry enough information for arcviz to tell two of those states apart from the artifact alone. That is a genuine open question, not a display bug to fix (see below).

## (e) Unresolved questions worth putting to Daniel

1. **Undisclosed vs. erased.** Per `keri-primer.md:223`, does the ACDC/KERI ecosystem carry *any* out-of-band or convention-level signal that distinguishes "holder withheld this in this presentation" from "this payload was permanently erased for compliance," or is that genuinely unrecoverable from the artifact — meaning arcviz's absent/undisclosed/redacted/unverified taxonomy needs a caveat that "redacted-for-compliance" and "undisclosed-by-choice" may be visually identical states, both correctly labeled but not distinguishable further?
2. **"Real-time" revocation, precisely.** Given the tension noted in §d between `keri-primer.md:269` and `acdc-and-mtc.md:134`, what should arcviz's status-freshness indicator actually claim — "checked against the latest event this verifier has seen" (which could lag) vs. some stronger real-time guarantee? Is there a spec-level answer, or is this genuinely dependent on witness-polling cadence per deployment?
3. **Foreign evidence nodes.** When an ACDC chain edge terminates in non-ACDC evidence (a signed PDF, an X.509 cert, per `keri-primer.md:206`), possibly saidified via `bes.md`'s bytewise or externalized algorithm rather than a standard structural SAID — does arcviz need a distinct visual treatment for "this node's verifiability class differs from its ACDC neighbors," and if so, does Daniel already have a view on what that distinction should communicate to a viewer?
4. **bSAID/xSAID in the entviz pill.** Has entviz's SAID-pill design already considered bytewise/externalized SAIDs (`bes.md`), or is that a gap this audit is the first to surface? If a gap, is it in scope for arcviz's first milestone or a deferred concern (since the mandatory test corpora — keripy/keria/vLEI — may rarely if ever produce a bSAID/xSAID in practice)?
5. **Weight/operator edge metadata in the UI.** `acdc-vc-diff.md:61` documents `w` (weight), `o` (operator), and `s` (schema) as native ACDC edge fields. Does Daniel want arcviz to expose edge weight/operator visually (e.g., line thickness, badge) in an early milestone, or is that lower priority than getting the absent/undisclosed/redacted/unverified node states right first?
