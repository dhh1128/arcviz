## (a) The axes

### The flattening this corrects

The project has circulated two lists as if they were one taxonomy: the spec's seven *disclosure variants* (Compact, Metadata, Partial, Nested Partial, Full, Selective, Bulk-issued Instance — `v1.1:1763-1787`), and a research-derived list of viewer-side conditions (*stale*, *erased*, *absent*, *unverified*, *not-yet-fetched*, plus AGENTS.md's *undisclosed* and *redacted*). These are not points on one scale. "Compact" is a property of which bytes are in hand; "erased" is a claim about why bytes are not in hand; "unverified" is a claim about what checks this viewer has run; "stale" is a claim about time. Flattening them produces states that are simultaneously true ("compact AND unverified AND stale") and states that look distinct but are the same observable ("erased" and "withheld" present identically — see §c).

### The six axes (five original; P added 2026-09-11)

The decomposition below differs from the candidate decomposition in the commissioning brief in one structural way: **"Form" (which spec disclosure variant the artifact is in) is not an axis.** Three arguments, each grounded in the spec itself:

1. **Form is not single-valued.** "All the Graduated Disclosure mechanisms MAY be used in combination" (`v1.1:1789`). A real artifact can be a bulk-issued instance whose `A` section is selectively disclosed while its rules section is compact — three "variants" at once. An axis whose values co-occur freely is not an axis; it is a vocabulary.
2. **Form changes verification only through H.** (Restated 2026-09-11; the original "Form does not change verification" overstated the cited passage — KRT-F5.) "a signature on any variant MAY be used to verify the Issuer's commitment to any other variant... This cross-variant Issuer commitment verifiability is an essential property" (`v1.1:2023`, quoted in [acdc-inventory.md](acdc-inventory.md) §5) makes *Issuer-commitment* verification form-invariant: one Issuer commitment, every variant a view of it. Verification generally is not form-invariant — at compact form, schema conformance of the expanded section, edge constraints, and bona-fides traversal are all unperformable, as this document's own H axis says — but form's effect on which checks are performable factors entirely through which bytes are in hand, i.e. through H. So "which variant" carries no independent epistemic weight beyond what the per-unit holdings axis (H, below) already records, which is the stronger argument and the one the rest of the document actually uses.
3. **Two of the seven variants are not visibility degrees at all.** Metadata Disclosure is a *decoy commitment* — a differently-SAIDed artifact deliberately uncorrelatable with the real one (`v1.1:177-179`). Bulk-issued Instance Disclosure changes nothing about what is visible in this instance; it changes what disclosure *correlates to* across contexts (`v1.1:1787`). Putting these on a Compact→Full scale is precisely the category error being corrected.

The variants survive intact as *coordinates* on the axes below (mapping table at the end of this section), so nothing the spec names is dropped.

The six axes, with the observable each one carries:

| Axis | Name | Unit of application | What it records | Readable from the artifact alone? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H** | Holdings | per addressable unit (see below) | what this viewer has in hand for that unit: content, commitment, reference, or nothing | yes (with the schema; see H-states) |
| **C** | Cause | per unit not fully in hand | *why* it is not in hand | mostly **no** — this is the out-of-band axis |
| **V** | Verification | per check, per subject (per-artifact for most checks; per-edge for edge constraints; per-tier for delegation — amended 2026-09-11, see §B3) | which independent checks were run and what each returned | no — it is a record of this viewer's own acts |
| **T** | Time | a coordinate on every V claim, plus derived artifact states | as-of-when each claim holds; valid-then vs valid-now; staleness; reconciliation lag | no — requires clocks, event streams, and policy windows |
| **X** | Correlation | per artifact | instance identity (singular vs bulk-instance) and the perfect-correlator surface of what is in hand | partly — correlators are visible, instance-siblinghood is not |
| **P** | Presentation provenance | per render | why the viewer has this material in front of it at all: **self** (holder-facing — the viewer's own store), **attested** (host-verified presentation evidence), or **unknown** (the default) | no — it is a fact about the render's own provenance, not about the credential ([presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(d)) |

**Axis P (added 2026-09-11).** Three independent non-Claude reviewers converged unprompted on the same structural absence: every V check concerns the artifact's issuance history, and none establishes that the party presenting the credential controls the AID it names as holder ([reviews/2026-09-05-outside-model-refutation.md](../../reviews/2026-09-05-outside-model-refutation.md) §(b)1, adjudicated VALID; traced in [presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md)). P is deliberately not a tenth V check: it cannot degrade per addressable unit, because it applies once, to the whole render ([presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(d)). Its states: **self** — the viewer is the holder inspecting its own store, and a presenter-control question is a category error the render must not imply is live; **attested** — the host affirmatively supplied *and reconciled* presentation evidence (e.g. an IPEX `grant` exn's signature, verified, its sender compared against the credential's holder field — a reconciliation nothing in keripy, KERIA, or signify-ts performs anywhere in the traced surface, [presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(a),(c)); **unknown** — the default, and the truth of any credential retrieved via the ordinary `Credentials.get()`/`.list()` path, which strips presentation context entirely ([presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(c)). Rule 18 carries the rendering, R9 the combination, §c pair 12 the indistinguishability. P is per-render — a third index arity alongside H's per-unit and V's per-(check, subject) — which sharpens rather than resolves the index-domain finding folded into §B3 and §B6 (KRT-F2).

**Addressable unit** (for axis H): any block or field the disclosure machinery treats independently — the top-level ACDC; each section (`s`, `a`, `A`, `e`, `r`); each nested sub-block of `a`; each element of the `A` aggregate array; each Edge and Edge-group; each Rule and Rule-group; each *referenced* artifact (an edge's far-node ACDC, a delegator's KEL, a registry's TEL, a schema). The uniform `d`+`u` mechanism applies at every one of these levels (`v1.1:102`; [acdc-inventory.md](acdc-inventory.md) §6), which is what makes one per-unit axis sufficient where the brief's candidate had separate "Form" and "Field epistemics" axes.

**Where each flattened observable lands:**

| Flattened term | Decomposition |
|---|---|
| absent | H (unit state H9, or H7 for a missing referenced artifact) |
| undisclosed / withheld | H2–H5 or H8 (commitment in hand, content not) × C (cause = holder choice — usually unknowable, see §c) |
| redacted / erased | same H states × C (cause = compliance erasure — usually unknowable, see §c) |
| not-yet-fetched | H7 × C (cause = viewer-side, knowable from the viewer's own fetch log) |
| unverified | V (checks unperformed or unperformable) — **not** an H state at all |
| stale | T (assurance window exceeded) — orthogonal to everything else |
| compact / partial / nested-partial / full / selective | H coordinates of the affected units |
| metadata | H6 (whole-artifact decoy commitment) |
| bulk-issued instance | X (instance identity), H unchanged |

**Orthogonality caveat, stated honestly — restated 2026-09-11 after five of six outside-model seats caught the same overclaim in the same sentence.** C applies only when H ≠ content-in-hand, and T scopes V — so these are not a full Cartesian product. As first written this paragraph claimed "no value on one axis implies a value on another," which the document's own cells contradict: an H7 unit *forces* every dependent V check to unperformable (H7's row; Rule 10), the H2/H3 distinction is exactly what decides whether a bare SAID is a perfect correlator on X (§B5's flagged inference, now cross-referenced from here), and disclosing any `a` sub-block forces `a.i` and `a.dt` to H1 (§B1's forced-transition note). The defensible claim is narrower: the axes *measure different things*, and no axis is redundant with another's domain — a fully-held unit can be unverified, a verified artifact can be stale, a blinded unit can have any cause. H-states gate which V outcomes are *reachable*; they never supply V's values. That is the property the flattened lists lacked.

### The seven spec variants, decomposed (nothing dropped)

| Spec variant (`v1.1:1763-1787`) | Axis coordinates |
|---|---|
| Compact Disclosure | affected units at H2 or H3 (digest in hand); C typically "pending" — the spec frames it as commitment "that MAY be more fully disclosed later" (`:1775`), an *undisclosed-yet* state, not a redaction |
| Metadata Disclosure | whole artifact at H6; V: issuer-commitment checks *unperformable by design* (`:183`: "only cryptographic commitments from the Discloser are attached, not commitments from the Issuer") |
| Partial Disclosure | units at H3, movable to H1 by disclosing content + `u` (`:1779`) |
| Nested Partial Disclosure | H3 applied recursively, per branch, per level (`:1781`) |
| Full Disclosure | H1 throughout the disclosed scope — noting the spec's own warning that "Full" is scope-relative (`:1793`) |
| Selective Disclosure | disclosed elements at H1; undisclosed elements at H4 (SAID list in hand) or the whole section at H5 (AGID only) (`:1785`, `:672`) |
| Bulk-issued Instance Disclosure | H unchanged; X = bulk-instance (`:1787`) |

## (b) The matrix

Three columns per cell throughout: **knows** (the warranted claim), **cannot know** (the boundary of the warrant), **naive lie** (what an ordinary card design would wrongly imply — the point of the exercise).

### B1. Axis H — holdings, per addressable unit

| # | State | Viewer **knows** | Viewer **cannot know** | **Naive lie** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Content in hand** — the unit is expanded; labels and values present | The exact bytes; can recompute the unit's `d` and check it against every enclosing commitment; absence of an optional field *inside* an expanded block is issuance-time absence, committed by the SAID | Whether the content is *true* — authenticity ≠ veracity ([avv2025-orthogonality], `authenticity-vs-veracity.md:35`); whether anything else exists beyond this unit | Visible ⇒ fine. A card that renders values with no verification channel implies the display itself vouches for them; and it renders the *absence* of sibling data as nonexistence (see H9, X) |
| H2 | **Unblinded commitment** — bare SAID in hand for a block whose schema reserves no `u` (or whose `u` is absent) | Content-identity commitment; the block's shape via schema; the content is verifiable if later provided; crucially, the content **may be discoverable now** by rainbow/dictionary attack over the schema's value space, and the SAID is "a fixed point of correlation, potentially to the ACDC field values themselves in spite of non-disclosure" (`v1.1:171`) | The actual content (without guessing); the cause (axis C) | The gravest one on this axis: rendering an unblinded digest with a "private"/"redacted" treatment asserts a protection that does not exist. The spec's own verdict: such an ACDC "SHOULD be considered a public (non-confidential) ACDC" (`:171`) |
| H3 | **Blinded commitment** — bare SAID in hand, committed block carries a high-entropy `u` (per schema), `u` not disclosed | Commitment; shape via schema; content is computationally hidden — an adversary "is not able to discover the remaining contents... in a computationally feasible manner" (`v1.1:175`); the SAID is *not* a correlator to field values "unless and until there has been a disclosure" (`:175`) | Content; cause (withheld? erased? pending? — §c pair 1 and 3); whether disclosure will ever be offered | Rendering it as an error, a loading state, or empty. Also the inverse of H2's lie: conflating H3 with H2 under one "hidden" icon erases the only privacy distinction the format actually delivers |
| H4 | **Hidden aggregate element** — a bare SAID at some position in a disclosed `A` array | The count of elements; membership is provable if later given content+`u` ("Membership in the set can be verified against a set of SAIDs, one from each element", `:1785`); **nothing else — not even labels**, because "the field labels for a given block only appear within that blinded block" (`:705`) | Labels, values, which schema candidate each element instantiates; position tells nothing — the schema's `anyOf` order "is not correlated to the actual order of appearance... This prevents inference based on location" (`:705`) | Two lies. (1) Rendering hidden elements with candidate labels enumerated from the schema's `anyOf` — manufacturing information the format deliberately withholds. (2) Rendering them positionally aligned to the schema, implying an order-to-meaning mapping the spec explicitly severed |
| H5 | **Aggregate fully compact** — only the AGID in hand as the `A` field's value | A commitment to *some* list of elements exists | The count, the labels, the values — everything below the AGID. And the AGID is "an aggregate, AGID, (Aggregate ID) not a SAID" (`v1.1:672`) — it is a digest over an ordered list with a dummied placeholder (`:723-731`), not a content-address of a block | Rendering the AGID as a SAID pill. An entviz pill asserts content-identity of a block ([acdc-mtc-said-vs-membership]); an AGID is a different commitment object with a different expansion procedure, and dressing it as a SAID misstates what a match would prove |
| H6 | **Metadata decoy** — an artifact whose top-level `u` is present but *empty* (`v1.1:177`) | The Issuer, Schema, provenanced Edges, and Rules of a yet-to-be-disclosed private ACDC; the Discloser's commitment to this metadata; the real ACDC's `d` "will necessarily be different" (`:177-179`), by design | The real ACDC's SAID or attribute content; and — the sharp part — the Issuer stands behind *nothing yet*: "only cryptographic commitments from the Discloser are attached, not commitments from the Issuer" (`:183`) | Rendering a metadata ACDC with the visual authority of an issued credential — issuer name, schema title, edges drawn — when the issuer has committed to none of it as presented. This is a pre-contractual sketch wearing a credential's clothes |
| H7 | **Reference in hand, artifact not** — an edge's `n` SAID names a far node not in the payload; a `di` field names a delegator whose KEL is absent | The identity (SAID/AID) of what is missing; that the near artifact commits to it; verification of everything that depends on it is *unperformable with material in hand* (axis V) | Anything about the missing artifact's content or validity; the cause splits — viewer-side (not fetched / fetch failed: knowable from the viewer's own session log) vs remote-side (never provided: not knowable from the artifact) — §c pair 4 | Omitting the dangling edge or the named delegator, so the chain renders shorter and *more complete* than it is. The real vLEI chain has exactly this hole: "the root `EDP1vHcw...`'s KEL... is absent entirely. The chain's delegation claims cannot be fully verified from this payload alone" ([corpus-vlei-chain.md](corpus-vlei-chain.md) §3). Incomplete evidence is a normal state, not an error state |
| H8 | **Reference hidden** — a Compact Private Edge: the edge block is replaced by its SAID with a `u` inside, so even the far node's identity is withheld | An edge exists in a named schema slot; the presenter has made "a verifiable commitment to the ACDC attached to the Edge without disclosing any details of that ACDC, including the ACDC's SAID" (`v1.1:1176`) | Where the edge points; everything about the far node | Dropping the edge from the graph entirely. A DAG with a committed-but-blinded edge has different topology than a DAG without it, and hiding the hidden edge hides that the holder is withholding a relationship |
| H9 | **Nothing** — no field, no digest, no reference | If the *enclosing* block is at H1: this artifact, as issued, has no such field — absence is committed by the enclosing SAID and is an issuance-time fact. If the enclosing block is not at H1: not even that | That the information does not exist elsewhere; that the party lacks some other credential. The negative claim is a claim about a corpus the viewer does not have ([PLAN.md](PLAN.md), correlation-honesty principle), and EUDI *mandates* the ambiguity at whole-credential level: denial "SHALL behave towards the Relying Party as if the attestation did not exist" ([arf-topic-d-embedded-disclosure-policies] REQ 4, [prior-art/wallets.md](prior-art/wallets.md) §1) | Rendering absence as zero, as false, as "none", or as a withheld field. And the exhaustive-looking empty state: "no linked credentials found" reads as a fact about the world when it is a fact about the payload |

**A leakage asymmetry the matrix must carry (H1 interacting with H3 vs H4).** Expanding *any* sub-block of an `a` (attributive) section "exposes, at the very least, the labels of other fields in its enclosing block" (`v1.1:1791, 1793`), whereas disclosing an `A` element exposes nothing about siblings, "including their labels" (`:1791`). So after one field of an `a` block is disclosed, its still-hidden siblings are in a distinct sub-state — **label known, value hidden** — that exists nowhere in the `A` mechanism. A renderer that shows both as generic "hidden fields" understates what the verifier already learned in the `a` case (the holder may not realize the labels leaked) and overstates it in the `A` case. This is the mechanism-level difference the corpus makes concrete: the guardianship example reveals one boolean age flag "and nothing about which other thresholds hold — the aggregate form exists precisely so *which* flags are asserted stays hidden... unlike a labeled attribute set where a revealed field advertises its own name" ([corpus-keripy-examples.md](corpus-keripy-examples.md) §(b)2).

**The asymmetry as first drawn understated the strongest leak (amended 2026-09-11, PRV-F1).** The enclosing block of any `a` sub-block is the attribute section itself, whose reserved fields are `d`, `u`, `i` (the Issuee AID) and `dt` (`v1.1:274-282`), and `v1.1:1791`'s "at the very least, the labels" is a floor, not a ceiling: the reserved fields appear with *values*, not labels, in any uncompacted `a`. Disclosing any `a` sub-block therefore moves `a.i` and `a.dt` to H1 as a **forced transition**, not an independent one — and `a.i` is the strongest correlator in the artifact, joinable by equality across every verifier that receives any single field (§B5). The spec treats its disclosure as the Chain-Link-Confidentiality-gated event of the whole exchange: a disclosure "could be initiated on behalf of a different AID that has not yet been correlated to the Issuee AID and then only correlated to the Issuee AID after the Disclosee has agreed to the Chain-Link Confidentiality provisions" (`v1.1:355`). This is an explicit exception to axis H's per-unit independence, recorded in §a's orthogonality caveat; Rule 6 carries the rendering consequence, and §f question 7 is widened accordingly.

**H2 vs H3 is undecidable where the schema permits but does not require `u` (added 2026-09-11, SPC-F3).** The H2/H3 split as defined presumes the schema *decides* whether the committed block carries a `u`. The spec makes `u` permissive at every level — "MAY optionally appear in any block (field map) at any level of an ACDC" (`v1.1:102`), and for Edges, Edge-groups, Rules and Rule-groups, normatively and repeatedly, "optional, but when it appears, it MUST appear as the second field" (`v1.1:1173`; identically `:1104`, `:1289`, `:1325`) — so a conformant schema may list `u` as permitted without requiring it, and whether a `u` was present at issuance is exactly what compaction hides. Against such a schema a bare SAID is consistent with both H2 and H3, and no fetch can settle it. §c pair 11 records the indistinguishability; Rule 8's treatment set is widened to four.

**H6's discriminator is overloaded in the reference implementation (added 2026-09-11, SKP-F2).** The spec's discriminator is an *empty* top-level `u` — "An empty, top-level unique-entropy, `u`, field appearing in an ACDC indicates that the ACDC is a metadata ACDC" (`v1.1:179`) — with genuinely public ACDCs *omitting* `u` (the real vLEI credentials do — [corpus-vlei-chain.md](corpus-vlei-chain.md)). But keripy's ACDC field maps carry `u=''` as the unset default (`src/keri/acdc/messaging.py`, `alls`/`opts` — re-read directly for this revision at the checkout [presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) pins), and six upstream test fixtures serialize ordinary, fully-populated ACDCs with `'u': ''` (panel finding SKP-F2, verified by the panel against `upstream/main e2a1e35c`; re-confirmed at the local checkout, `tests/acdc/test_messaging.py:2378` et seq.). Absent, empty and populated are three states, and empty alone does not warrant the metadata reading against artifacts the reference stack emits today. Rule 12 is conditioned accordingly, and §e retiers H6 from "no artifact anywhere" to "artifact exists; discriminator ambiguous."

### B2. Axis C — cause of a unit not being fully in hand

The values, split by who could know them:

| Cause | Knowable by this viewer? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| pending-graduated — commitment made, fuller disclosure on offer in an ongoing exchange | only from exchange state (IPEX apply/offer/agree/grant), not from the artifact | Compact Disclosure "MAY be more fully disclosed later" (`v1.1:1775`); the exchange grammar is IPEX, whose `apply`/`offer` messages carry the now-normative Disclosure Paths, `dp`, field (`v1.1:1809-1994`, bound into `apply`/`offer` at `v1.1:2013-2017`; worked examples [corpus-keripy-examples.md](corpus-keripy-examples.md) §(b)1) |
| withheld-by-choice — holder decided not to disclose, this presentation | **no** — presents as a bare digest | [keri-primer-erasure-vs-hash], `keri-primer.md:223`; §c pair 1 |
| erased-for-compliance — "the payload is destroyed. The hash remains in the log to preserve the integrity of the chain" | **no** — presents identically to withheld | same source; §c pair 1 |
| viewer-not-fetched / fetch-failed — the far artifact was never retrieved, or retrieval failed | **yes** — from the viewer's own session history, never from the artifact | **Inference** from the structure of the problem; no source assigns this state, which is itself evidence it is viewer-local |
| never-provided — the presenter did not include the referenced artifact and no fetch route exists | no — indistinguishable from a fetch the viewer didn't attempt, absent a fetch log; §c pair 4 | [corpus-vlei-chain.md](corpus-vlei-chain.md) §3 (the absent root KEL) |
| never-issued — the field was absent at issuance | yes, iff the enclosing block is at H1 (committed absence) | SAID commits the enclosing map (`v1.1:20`) |

**Knows / cannot know / naive lie for the axis as a whole:** the viewer knows, at most, its own side (fetch history, exchange state) plus committed issuance-time absence; it cannot know the remote party's reason from the artifact, ever, for the withheld/erased/never-provided family; and the naive lie is any cause-specific label — "redacted", "withheld", "deleted" — attached without host-supplied evidence. Each of those words is an accusation or an excuse the artifact does not carry.

### B3. Axis V — verification, a vector of independent checks

Each check has a four-valued outcome — **pass / fail / unperformed / unperformable-with-material-in-hand** — plus a T-scope (§B4). The last two outcomes are different states and must render differently: *unperformed* is the viewer's choice or laziness — or, far more often, the honest description of a check whose result did not survive; *unperformable* is a fact about the evidence (the vLEI root-KEL hole makes delegation-to-root unperformable, not failed).

**Amended 2026-09-05, from the keripy audit ([keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md)): "unperformed" is the *default* truth for most cells of a merely-retrieved credential, not an edge case.** Eight of the nine checks in the reference implementation are verify-or-throw gates whose pass is recorded only implicitly as "the object exists"; "there is no API, stored record, or status object of the shape 'for credential X, checks 1-9 were {pass, fail, unperformed, unperformable}' anywhere in `src/keri/`" ([keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md) §(d)). The one exception is revocation: `Tever.vcState(vcid)` (`src/keri/vdr/eventing.py:1144-1192`) is "the one genuinely separable, re-queryable check" (§(a) check 8). So a renderer handed a credential from storage, with no freshly-computed vector from its host, must show checks 1–7 and 9 as unperformed *from its own point of view* — not because nothing happened, but because nothing survived the moment it ran. This strengthens rather than weakens the vector framing: the substrate itself does not track the distinctions a single "verified" badge would claim to summarize.

An original design premise of this section is hereby weakened in one specific place. This document treated fail vs unperformed as always distinguishable in principle. **For checks 6 and 7, against a stock keripy substrate, they are not: those checks can never honestly report "fail" — only "not-pass".** `verifyChain` "returns `None` both when the far node genuinely fails a constraint (e.g. `op == 'I2I' and issuer != farIssuee`, `verifying.py:478-479`) and when the far node simply is not in the store yet (`verifying.py:418-420,471-472,475-476`)", and its caller treats both identically — the same `MissingChainError`, the same escrow ([keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md) §(a) check 6; the full pair is §c pair 10). A host built directly on keripy "cannot honestly report 'fail' for these two checks at all today; it can only report 'unperformed/unperformable' or 'pass'" (§(e)). Rule 2 carries the rendering consequence.

**Amended 2026-09-11, from the doctrine panel (SKP-F1, KRT-F2, KRT-F4) — three corrections to this axis's framing, the vector idea intact.** (a) **The default outcome space is now {pass, unperformed, unperformable}; "fail" is an opt-in outcome a host must declare it can supply.** The four-valued default put the optimistic polarity in the one document whose thesis is that optimistic defaults are the bug: on the reference substrate "fail" is unreachable for checks 1–4 through a store-fed host — the eight verify-or-throw gates above mean SAID-invalid bytes never construct, schema failures never store, unanchored events never enter a KEL — so a renderer offering a fail-glyph that is merely "not asserted" invites the absent-flag-read-as-assurance failure Rule 2's own test forbids (SKP-F1). Rule 2 amendment (c) carries the inversion; a host that recomputes a check over raw material (arcviz's own Python path for checks 1–2, or the DI2I/NOT exception-type separator pair 10 notes) may declare fail for exactly those cells. (b) **The vector's index is per-(check, subject), not per-artifact (KRT-F2).** Edge constraints are evaluated per edge — a separate `verifyChain` call and far-node schema check for each ([keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md) §(a) check 6; `verifying.py:171-241`) — and delegation per KEL tier, so the subject is the artifact for checks 1–4 and 9, the edge for check 6, the KEL tier for check 5, and the registry for check 8. A per-artifact nine-cell vector cannot carry the blocking SAID Rule 10 demands, and a two-edge artifact with one far node in hand and one at H7 has no honest single value for check 6 — the scalar collapse this axis forbids, reproduced one level down. (Axis P adds a third arity, per render.) (c) **Checks 6 and 7 are one relation at two depths (KRT-F4).** Issuer bona fides is the edge-constraint walk applied transitively — the same code path, as pair 10 already concedes — so cell 7 is an aggregate over cell 6 at depth ≥ 2, i.e. a chain-level roll-up, and it now falls explicitly under Rule 11: a rendered cell 7 names the EGF rule it aggregates under, arcviz-default and host-overridable, never presented as spec-derived (validity aggregation is EGF-dependent, `v1.1:1123-1127`).

**And a scope statement axis P makes explicit: none of the nine checks is about the presenter.** Every check below concerns the artifact's issuance history; none establishes that the party who handed the credential over controls the AID it names as holder. That question is axis P (§a; [presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md)), not a tenth check — it applies once per render, not per unit, and does not degrade per-check.

**Correction (2026-09-05): the valid-then-vs-valid-now row is implementation-attested, not only paper-attested.** As first written, this axis rested the "two different queries the substrate can answer" claim on the papers alone, and the Phase 2D-b survey initially concluded no surface serves TEL event history. That conclusion was wrong at the scope it implied ([keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md), Correction section): keripy's **witness** exposes `GET /query?typ=tel` with `reg` (registry prefix) or `vcid` (credential SAID) — `QueryEnd`, `src/keri/app/indirecting.py:1191`, mounted in `setupWitness` at `indirecting.py:104` — serving `reger.clonePreIter()`, "the whole event stream for that prefix", as CESR: "genuine event history, not a state snapshot". It is live in `WebOfTrust/keripy` `main` at 2.1.0-dev0. Additionally, the V2 registry layer addresses TEL events **by sequence number** (`RegistryStore.seqEvent(regk, sn)`, `src/keri/acdc/registering.py`), and `WebRegBaser` "sets up **browser** sub databases for public ACDC registry TELs" (`webregbasing.py`) — IndexedDB — so valid-then-vs-valid-now may become *re-performable client-side* rather than taken on an agent's word. What is **not** fixed: the KERIA agent API still exposes only latest-state snapshots (`status`, `credentials().state()` — [keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md) §(b), §(d) pair 6), so whether this axis is servable in practice depends on **which surface the host talks to** — a witness or browser-local registry store serves it; the KERIA agent surface, today, does not.

**Caveat added 2026-09-11 (PRV-F3): the witness surface's capability comes with a correlation cost the comparison above originally omitted.** A witness operates on the issuer/controller side. The spec places a Validator's point-of-validation query at a verifier-side **Observer** precisely so the issuer's infrastructure cannot see usage: "A validator queries its Observer, not the Registrar, at a point of validation... This protects against forced validator-to-issuer correlation of ACDC usage, i.e., no forced phone home validation" (`v1.1:1707`). A host — or worse, the renderer itself — that queries the issuer's witness when a card is opened re-creates the OCSP-style leak at per-view granularity, and nothing on the resulting card says that viewing it told the issuer's side about it. So the §c pair 6 freshness interface names the *surface class* (verifier-side Observer vs issuer-side witness/Registrar) as part of the input, and Rule 17 forbids arcviz from fetching as a side effect of rendering.

**Decided 2026-09-05 (was a flagged inference):** the staleness threshold is **host-supplied, per credential** — not owned by arcviz and not a single global window, because "different credentials may have different revocation-freshness profiles" ([PLAN.md](PLAN.md) decisions, second set), presumably keyed by schema, registry, or governance framework — consistent with the LAPSED precedent's governance-defined threshold ([hardman-proga-lapsed]). Where no policy is supplied for a credential, "no freshness policy supplied" is its own visible state, not a silent default. §f question 2 is closed by this.

## (c) Indistinguishable states

States not separable from the artifact alone — plus one (pair 10) not separable from the reference *substrate* either. For each pair: the observable they share, and the out-of-band information that would separate them — which is, collectively, the interface arcviz needs from its host. The honest rendering names the ambiguity; it never picks a side.

**Baseline stated explicitly (added 2026-09-11, SKP-F3): arcviz is fully conformant and honest with every one of these host-interface channels empty.** Each channel's honest default for absence is already specified — cause renders unknown (Rule 5), no-freshness-policy is a visible state (Rule 4), an unresolved schema renders protection-unknown (Rule 8), duplicity is always scoped (pair 7), presentation provenance defaults to unknown (Rule 18). The channels the pairs enumerate are therefore one optional annotation vocabulary with defined absent-semantics, not separate contracts a host must implement; a host supplies what it can, and the render without it is degraded in *precision*, never in honesty. Exactly one channel had a decided owner at the time of the panel (the per-credential freshness policy) and one a demonstrated provider (witness TEL replay, with §B4's correlation caveat); the rest are named so that the ambiguities stay addressable, not because Phase 3/4 owes seven API surfaces.

1. **Erased-for-compliance vs withheld-by-choice.** Both present as a bare digest with no recoverable payload: "the payload is destroyed. The hash remains in the log to preserve the integrity of the chain" ([keri-primer-erasure-vs-hash], `keri-primer.md:223`; flagged as a genuine spec-level ambiguity in [prior-art/papers-acdc.md](prior-art/papers-acdc.md) §(e)1). **Separator:** an attested statement of cause — e.g. a dossier Annotation Edge marking the artifact's effective state in a later version ([acdc-inventory.md](acdc-inventory.md) §7, "Verifiers MUST process the dossier by traversing the graph to resolve the 'effective state'"), or issuer/holder metadata the host supplies. **Host interface: attested-cause metadata per unit, with its own provenance** (an unattested cause string is a claim, not a fact, and should render as such).
2. **Default vs explicit edge operator.** Two vLEI edges omit `o` (defaulting to `I2I` per `v1.1:1209-1213`) and one writes `I2I` explicitly, for the identical relationship ([corpus-vlei-chain.md](corpus-vlei-chain.md) §2). **Separator:** none is needed **when the far node is in hand and targeted** (narrowed 2026-09-11, KRT-F1) — then the states are semantically identical and the honest rendering is *identical rendering*, with the syntactic difference visible only in a raw-bytes view. The narrowing matters because the injected default is a function of the *far node's* content — targeted ⇒ `I2I`, untargeted ⇒ `NI2I` (`v1.1:1209-1213`) — so with the far node compact, absent (H7) or hidden (H8), the unspecified edge's effective operator is undetermined while the explicit edge's is not, and the two are no longer identical; Rule 9 carries the conditional and the undetermined state. This pair stays in the list because its resolution is opposite to every other pair: within its stated scope the renderer must *suppress* a visible difference rather than surface an invisible one, and "specified vs unspecified" must never become a badge.
3. **Compact-pending vs withheld-final.** A bare SAID may be an interim state in a graduated exchange ("MAY be more fully disclosed later", `v1.1:1775`) or the holder's final answer. **Separator:** the exchange state — IPEX apply/offer/agree/grant position and the message's Disclosure Paths, `dp`, field, a normative construct on the authoritative branch: a full section of path syntax, closures and worked examples (`v1.1:1809-1994`), carried in the query section of `apply`/`offer` (`v1.1:2013-2017`), with executable keripy examples ([corpus-keripy-examples.md](corpus-keripy-examples.md) §(b)1,3). (A panel finding, SPC-F4, held `dp` to be "an unresolvable reference: an unmerged construct from a keripy discussion"; it was withdrawn — the panel read the `main` branch, where `dp` appears zero times ([reviews/2026-09-06-operator-semantics-adjudication.md](../../reviews/2026-09-06-operator-semantics-adjudication.md), 2026-09-10 correction). This pair's separator is better grounded than this document originally claimed, not worse.) **Host interface: negotiation context, when the render happens inside an exchange — a `dp` value, or its closure, is the authoritative statement of which units a given view is entitled to show as disclosed rather than blinded ([acdc-inventory.md](acdc-inventory.md) §9).**
4. **Never-provided vs not-fetched vs fetch-failed** (for an H7 referenced artifact). From the artifact, all three are one state: the far thing is not here. **Separator:** the viewer's own session history — did this viewer attempt retrieval, and what happened. Two are self-knowledge the host/component already holds; only never-provided-vs-unfetchable remains genuinely open after logging. **Host interface: a fetch/attempt log per referenced SAID.** (Fetching itself is an intent boundary and a disclosure to third parties — [prior-art/papers-intent.md](prior-art/papers-intent.md) affordance 4 — so the log will often legitimately read "not attempted.")
5. **Blinded vs unblinded commitment (H3 vs H2) when the schema is not in hand.** The distinction rests on whether the committed block reserves a `u` (`v1.1:171,175`), which is read from the schema; with the schema itself compact or unresolvable, a bare SAID is just a bare SAID. **Separator:** the schema. **Host interface: schema resolution — noting that resolving it over the network is itself a correlating fetch (pair 4's caveat applies).** Until resolved, the honest state is "hidden; protection unknown." **Precondition added 2026-09-11 (SEC-F2): a resolved schema is admissible input only if it verifies against the committed schema SAID.** The static-schema mandate is normative — "all Schemas MUST be static, i.e., Schemas MUST be SADs and therefore verifiable against their SAIDs" (`v1.1:211`), and non-local URI schema references "MUST NOT be used because they are not cryptographically end-verifiable" (`v1.1:217`) — and Rule 8 derives its gravest distinction from this one input, so a host that resolves schemas without SAID verification can be induced, by a substituted schema that reserves a `u`, to assert H3's protection on an H2 unit *through Rule 8's own input*. A host that cannot demonstrate SAID-verified resolution supplies "hidden; protection unknown" instead. (Stock keripy verifies — `CacheResolver.add` refuses a schema whose SAID mismatches its key, `src/keri/core/scheming.py:47-48`, per SEC-F2's verification — which is exactly why the requirement belongs in the host interface rather than an assumption: a browser-side or KERIA-side host has no such gate by construction.)
6. **Not-revoked vs revoked-beyond-the-horizon.** Absence of revocation events in the material in hand ([corpus-vlei-chain.md](corpus-vlei-chain.md) §3) is consistent with both. **Separator:** fresher TEL events; never fully separable — only the as-of horizon moves ([acdc-mtc-revocation-reconciled]). A concrete source of fresher events exists in the substrate: the witness TEL-replay endpoint (`GET /query?typ=tel` — §B4 correction), though not on the KERIA agent surface. **Host interface: event-stream updates plus an explicit freshness policy — now decided to be host-supplied per credential, with "no policy supplied" a visible state** ([telco-revocation-freshness-explicit]; [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) decisions 2026-09-05, second set).
7. **No-duplicity-detected vs no-duplicity-exists.** Structurally never separable: KERI is duplicity-*evident*, and detection is relative to the witnesses/watchers consulted ([wtbo-duplicity-evident-not-resistant], `wtbo.md:109-113`). **Separator:** none complete; consulting more independent watchers shrinks the gap without closing it. The rendering must therefore always scope the claim ("none detected by N watchers"), never state the universal.
8. **Non-possession vs declined presentation** (whole-credential level). Deliberately indistinguishable outward: the wallet "SHALL behave towards the Relying Party as if the attestation did not exist" ([arf-topic-d-embedded-disclosure-policies] REQ 4, via [prior-art/wallets.md](prior-art/wallets.md)). **Separator:** none, by design of the presenting ecosystem — and arcviz must not imply one exists. This is the strongest external confirmation that H9's "cannot know" column is load-bearing.
9. **AGID vs SAID, as strings.** Both are CESR digest primitives; the derivation code names the algorithm, not the commitment kind. What makes a value an AGID is its structural position (the compact value of an `A` field) and the schema — "the value of a compact variant of the selectively disclosable Aggregate section is an aggregate, AGID, (Aggregate ID) not a SAID" (`v1.1:672`). **Separator:** structural/schema context, which the renderer must consult before choosing a pill treatment. **Inference:** that the strings are otherwise indistinguishable follows from AGID's construction as an ordinary digest over the serialized list (`:723-731`); no spec sentence states it outright. Flagged. *Kind-caveat added 2026-09-11 (outside-model panel):* this pair is a different kind from the others — its separator is *in the artifact* (structural position, available to anything that parsed the document), where every other pair's separator is genuinely out-of-band. It stays on the list because a pill component handed a bare string has lost that context, but that is an API-design footgun, not an artifact-level ambiguity, and grouping it with the rest without this caveat overstated the depth of the ambiguity.
10. **Edge-constraint failed vs far-node-not-yet-checkable** (added 2026-09-05 — the one pair found in the implementation rather than the spec, and indistinguishable from the *substrate* rather than from the artifact). In keripy, `Verifier.verifyChain` "returns `None` both when the far node genuinely fails a constraint (e.g. `op == 'I2I' and issuer != farIssuee`, `verifying.py:478-479`) and when the far node simply is not in the store yet (`verifying.py:418-420,471-472,475-476`)"; `processCredential` routes both to the same `MissingChainError` and the same escrow table (`reger.mce`, `verifying.py:187-191`), and expiring escrows are "simply removed and logged" with "no persisted distinction between 'the far node arrived and the constraint was violated' and 'the far node never arrived at all'" (`verifying.py:318-361`; all quotes [keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md) §(a) check 6, §(c); source `src/keri/vdr/verifying.py:400-490`). The reference implementation therefore cannot distinguish *failed* from *not-yet-checkable* for edge constraints — or for issuer bona fides, which it implements only as a special case of the same code path (§(a) check 7). **Separator: none exists in the substrate today.** A host could create one only by instrumenting or re-implementing `verifyChain`'s internals so the two `None` paths report differently — nothing in keripy does this bookkeeping ([keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md) §(d)). Until a host does, the honest outcome space for checks 6 and 7 is {pass, not-pass}, per the §B3 amendment and Rule 2. *Open lead flagged 2026-09-11:* two outside-model seats argued a host can build the separator itself — query its own store for the far SAID (absent ⇒ not-yet-checkable) and re-evaluate the constraint from the two artifacts when present — and the refutation run could not adjudicate it without tracing the cue mechanism at `verifying.py:188-189`, so it handed the lead off rather than ruling ([reviews/2026-09-05-outside-model-refutation.md](../../reviews/2026-09-05-outside-model-refutation.md) §(b)5). Until that trace is done, "no separator exists in the substrate" stands as written, with this flag on it.
11. **Blinded vs unblinded commitment when the schema permits but does not require `u`** (added 2026-09-11, SPC-F3). Distinct from pair 5: there the schema is not in hand; here it is in hand, verified, and *does not decide* — `u` is optional for the block class (`v1.1:102`, `:1104`, `:1173`, `:1289`, `:1325`; §B1's undecidability note), so the compact unit is consistent with both H2 and H3, and whether a `u` was present at issuance is exactly what compaction hides. **Separator: none by any fetch** — pair 5 is fixed by schema resolution; this cannot be fixed by resolution at all, only by fuller disclosure of the block itself. The honest treatment is "protection undecidable (permissive schema)", kept distinguishable in detail from pair 5's "protection unknown (schema unresolved)" because the remedies differ. Rule 8 carries both.
12. **Legitimate presentation vs replay** (added 2026-09-11 — the axis-P pair). A credential presented by the party who controls its holder AID, and the same bytes replayed by a party who does not, are one observable: every artifact-side check passes identically, the default transport strips the delivering exchange from the credential object, and the reference IPEX verifier never compares a grant's sender AID against the disclosed credential's holder field ([presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(a),(b),(c)). **Separator:** host-fetched exchange evidence *plus* host-authored reconciliation — retrieve the grant exn by SAID, verify its signature and KEL anchoring, reconcile its sender against the credential's holder field; no library call in the traced keripy/KERIA/signify-ts surface does any of this automatically, so the reconciliation is host work of the same kind as keripy's own `_verify_representation` test helper ([presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(a-cont)). **Host interface: presentation-provenance evidence carrying its own scope — who attested, over what signature, addressed to whom, as of when; absent it, P renders unknown (Rule 18).**

## (d) Rendering rules

Written to be testable: each rule names a violation a reviewer can look for. "Renders distinctly" means distinguishable by discrete symbol/shape/text — never by color alone ([PLAN.md](PLAN.md) principle: security-relevant state rides on discrete symbols; [amp-diff-principle-5]).

- **RULE 1 — no shared rendering across H-states.** Each of H1–H9 renders distinctly from every other and from any "fine" default. *Test:* produce fixtures for all nine; any two visually identical cells (ignoring content text) fail. In particular H2 vs H3 (guessable vs blinded), H4 vs H5 (count known vs unknown), H7 vs H9 (missing referent vs committed absence) must be pairwise distinct.
- **RULE 2 — verification renders as a vector, never a scalar.** Every check in §B3 gets its own indicator with four distinguishable outcomes: pass, fail, unperformed, unperformable. *Test:* find any single element whose presence summarizes more than one check; find any rendering where unperformed and unperformable share a glyph; find any rendering where unperformed is invisible (absence of a positive must not read as fine — the COIA rule: "An application MUST NOT render an absent flag as a positive assurance", [prior-art/local-codebases.md](prior-art/local-codebases.md) §4). *Amended 2026-09-05, per the keripy audit — the rule survives and is reinforced, with two changes.* (a) **Unperformed is the default truth, not an edge case**: since eight of nine checks leave no retrievable record past the moment they ran (§B3 amendment; [keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md) §(d)), a credential merely retrieved from storage renders with most cells at unperformed unless the host re-ran verification at render time or instrumented its own ingestion to persist a vector; the only substrate-re-queryable cell is revocation (`Tever.vcState`). The honest claim store presence itself supports is R0's — "the substrate's ingestion gate did not reject this" — never a pass on the other cells. (b) **The host interface must let a host declare a check's outcome space degraded to {pass, not-pass}**, and a renderer must not claim to distinguish fail from unperformed for a check where its host's substrate cannot make that distinction — on stock keripy that is checks 6 and 7, which can never honestly report "fail" (§c pair 10). *Additional tests:* a fixture retrieved from a bare store rendering anything other than unperformed for checks 1–7 and 9 without a host-supplied vector fails; a rendering that shows "fail" for an edge-constraint or issuer-bona-fides check backed by an undegraded stock-keripy host fails. *Amended again 2026-09-11 (SKP-F1; outside-model findings 4 and 7; KRT-F2) — three changes.* (c) **The default outcome space is inverted: {pass, unperformed, unperformable} is the default, and "fail" is the opt-in outcome a host declares it can supply** (per §B3's 2026-09-11 amendment (a)); arcviz may itself declare fail where it recomputes a check over raw material. This subsumes amendment (b): a host that declares nothing gets no fail-glyph anywhere, so checks 6 and 7 need no special-case degradation to avoid the absent-fail-read-as-meaningful lie, and (b)'s declaration mechanism survives as the general opt-in. (d) **The rule's own tests are re-scoped to match its amendments,** because as written a compliant degraded-space renderer failed the rule's literal test: the "unperformed and unperformable share a glyph" test applies within the *declared* outcome space — a host-declared collapse is visible as a declaration and does not fail it — and the "single element summarizing more than one check" test is scoped per (check, subject) and explicitly does not catch Rule 11's *labeled* whole-chain aggregate, which is the one sanctioned summary element. (e) **"One indicator per check" reads "one indicator per (check, subject)"** per §B3 amendment (b) — which is what Rule 10's blocking-SAID requirement already assumed. *Further tests:* a bare-store fixture rendering any fail glyph without a host fail-declaration fails; a two-edge fixture with one far node in hand and one at H7 rendering a single check-6 indicator fails.
- **RULE 3 — every positive verification claim carries a visible as-of scope.** *Test:* find a pass indicator with no reachable as-of; find a revocation state phrased as "not revoked" rather than "no revocation as of ⟨horizon⟩"; find a duplicity state phrased without naming the consulted-watcher scope.
- **RULE 4 — stale is a first-class symbol.** When the as-of scope exceeds the freshness policy — host-supplied, **per credential**, per the 2026-09-05 decision ([PLAN.md](PLAN.md), second set) — a distinct staleness marker appears without user action; a credential for which the host supplied no policy renders "no freshness policy supplied" as its own state, never a silent default. *Test:* age a fixture past the threshold and diff the render; no visible change fails. Render two credentials under different per-credential policies; a single global threshold applied to both fails. Render a no-policy fixture; anything other than a visible no-policy state fails.
- **RULE 5 — cause renders as unknown unless attested.** For any unit at H2–H5/H8, the cause label is "unknown" unless the host supplied attested cause metadata, in which case the label carries the attestation's own provenance. *Test:* find "redacted", "withheld", "deleted", or "private by choice" on any cell whose cause input is empty.
- **RULE 6 — the `a`/`A` leakage asymmetry is drawn.** A hidden sibling in a partially-disclosed `a` block renders as *label-known-value-hidden*, showing the leaked label; a hidden `A` element renders as an opaque element with no label, positioned without schema alignment; a fully-compact `A` renders with count unknown. *Test:* R2 fixture showing any candidate label on a hidden element fails; R3 fixture hiding the leaked sibling labels fails.
- **RULE 7 — an AGID is never rendered as a SAID.** No SAID pill, no content-address affordance, on an `A`-compact value; its treatment names it an aggregate commitment. *Test:* R2/R4 fixtures; an entviz pill on the AGID fails.
- **RULE 8 — H2 never renders with a privacy-asserting treatment.** An unblinded commitment renders as "collapsed, contents recoverable/guessable", never with the lock/private/blinded treatment reserved for H3. *Amended 2026-09-11 (SEC-F2, SPC-F3): the schema input is gated and the treatment set is four.* The H2/H3 classification may be computed only from a schema that verified against the committed schema SAID (§c pair 5's precondition; `v1.1:211`) — an unverified schema is not input, because a substituted schema reserving a `u` would otherwise make this rule assert protection that does not exist, through its own input. The four treatments: the lock (schema verified, `u` required); guessable (schema verified, `u` forbidden or absent); "protection undecidable (permissive schema)" (schema verified, `u` optional — §c pair 11); "hidden; protection unknown" (schema unresolved or unverified — §c pair 5). *Test:* four fixtures (H2, H3, permissive-schema, schema-unresolved); fewer than four distinct treatments fails; a fixture whose supplied schema does not verify against the committed SAID and which renders anything but "protection unknown" fails.
- **RULE 9 — default and explicit `I2I` render identically *when the effective operator is determined*, and an effective operator always renders as derived, never as read.** *Rewritten 2026-09-11 (KRT-F1); the original rule rendered a value the disclosure state does not always determine.* The injected default is a function of the far node's content — targeted ⇒ `I2I`, untargeted ⇒ `NI2I`, with a present `E1E` suppressing injection like the other three named operators on the authoritative branch (`v1.1:1209-1213`; table `v1.1:1203-1207`). So: (a) when the far node is in hand and its targetedness established, the syntactic presence of `o` is suppressed and the effective operator is shown, marked as *derived* and naming the far node it was derived from; (b) when the far node is compact, at H7, or at H8 — R1, this document's own normal case — the unspecified edge's effective operator is **undetermined**, renders as "operator undetermined — far node unresolved", and check 6 reports unperformable with the far SAID named (Rule 10); (c) an effective operator computed by the host's verifier is host-attested, not artifact-derived, and renders with that provenance, since a host and the spec rule can disagree. *Test:* render vLEI edges `le` (implicit) and `auth` (explicit) with far nodes in hand — any visible difference outside raw bytes fails; render an `o`-less edge whose far node is at H7 — an "I2I (default)" badge fails.
- **RULE 10 — missing referents render as holes, not trimmed edges.** An H7 unit (dangling `n`, absent delegator KEL) appears in the graph as an explicitly-unresolvable node, and every check it blocks reports unperformable with the blocking SAID named. *Test:* the vLEI fixture must show the root delegator; a 3-tier render of the 4-tier delegation, or a "delegation ✓" without reach, fails.
- **RULE 11 — no chain-level verdict roll-up.** Per-node status is per-node; any aggregate view labels itself as arcviz's own EGF-default rule, not spec-derived (validity propagation is EGF-dependent, `v1.1:1123-1127`), and remains overridable by the host. *Amended 2026-09-11 (KRT-F4):* check 7 (issuer bona fides) is itself an aggregate over check 6 at depth ≥ 2 (§B3 amendment (c)) and falls under this rule — a rendered bona-fides cell names the EGF rule it aggregates under, arcviz-default and host-overridable, never presented as spec-derived. *Tests:* find an unlabeled whole-chain badge; find a check-7 pass with no reachable aggregation-rule label.
- **RULE 12 — a metadata ACDC renders as issuer-uncommitted.** H6 artifacts carry a persistent, non-dismissable marker that no issuer commitment attaches as presented (`v1.1:183`). *Amended 2026-09-11 (SKP-F2): the trigger is conditioned, because the discriminator over-fires on reference-implementation artifacts.* keripy's builders carry `u=''` as the unset default and upstream fixtures serialize ordinary issued credentials that way (§B1's H6 note), so an empty top-level `u` alone MUST NOT fire the marker. It fires on empty-`u` *plus* corroboration: absence of Issuer commitments — the property the spec actually names, "only cryptographic commitments from the Discloser are attached, not commitments from the Issuer" (`v1.1:183`) — or host-supplied IPEX offer position. On empty-`u` with no corroborating signal either way, the honest render is the named ambiguity ("possibly a metadata ACDC"), per §c's own principle — never the full marker on a fully-issued credential, which would be a new lie manufactured by an anti-lie rule. *Tests:* R5 fixture rendered with the same authority chrome as an issued credential fails; a keripy-built issued credential with `u=''` and Issuer commitments attached that renders the non-dismissable marker also fails.
- **RULE 13 — no *corpus-level* negative claims, anywhere; committed absence is the one warranted negative and renders as such.** *Rewritten 2026-09-11: two outside-model seats independently caught the original "no negative claims, anywhere" contradicting H1/H9's committed-absence carve-out — the rule as written forbade exactly the claim the holdings axis calls warranted.* The scoping distinction the rule lacked: absence *within a block at H1* is cryptographically committed — the enclosing SAID proves this artifact, as issued, has no such field — and renders as exactly that issuance-time fact ("not present in this credential as issued"). Absence *from a corpus* — no linked credentials found, no revocation events seen, an identifier appearing nowhere — is a claim about material the viewer does not have, and renders with in-this-material scoping ("none *in what was presented*"); nothing corpus-level renders as exhaustive. *Test:* find a corpus-level empty state readable as "does not exist" ([hardman-wbca-negative]; §c pair 8) — the H9-under-H1 committed-absence render is exempt from this test and governed by Rule 1's distinctness (H9 vs H7) instead.
- **RULE 14 — correlators are surfaced, not manufactured.** Identical SAIDs/AIDs *within the rendered material* render identically (the entviz pill is a correlator-surfacing device by design — [PLAN.md](PLAN.md)); residual correlators of a partially-disclosed artifact are enumerable on demand; and **no join is drawn across presentations at all**. *Amended 2026-09-11 by Daniel Hardman's scope ruling:* arcviz renders one loaded presentation at a time and correlates only within it, so the earlier exception for an explicit, distinctly-styled, logged cross-presentation join is withdrawn along with the affordance it governed ([affordances.md](affordances.md) AF14, retired). *Test:* load two presentations sharing an AID; any edge drawn between them fails, explicit or not; an inferred edge sharing a disclosed edge's stroke fails. *Supersedes the note added earlier on 2026-09-11 (PRV-F4):* with no join and no cross-presentation retention, the correlation store that note worried about does not come into being, and the join-log half of open question 11 lapses.
- **RULE 15 — static output degrades honestly.** Print/screenshot/export renders every V indicator — and any P (presenter-control) claim — as a historical record ("checked ⟨scope⟩ by ⟨tool⟩"), never as live state. *Split 2026-09-11 (SKP-F5):* the former second half, "carries what a re-verifier needs," was an unbounded export-payload obligation welded onto a rendering rule — no named consumer, no stated scope, no fixture that could fail it — and is moved to open question 10 pending a demand test; the historical-record half stands, falls out of Rule 3's as-of scoping, and for P is the direct rendering-side answer to the mDL ecosystem's "flash pass" prohibition ([presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(e)). *Test:* diff live vs print CSS media output; identical verification chrome fails; a static export carrying a live-styled presenter-control badge fails. (No audited ecosystem designed for this moment at all — [prior-art/wallets.md](prior-art/wallets.md) §5 — so this rule is arcviz's own, flagged as new per [PLAN.md](PLAN.md)'s affordance-scope decision.)
- **RULE 16 — terms of use travel with the render (added 2026-09-11, PRV-F2).** Any unit whose enclosing ACDC carries a non-empty `r` section renders with a persistent indication that terms attach, the terms reachable and the `r` SAID shown; and any static export (Rule 15) carries the `r` section — or its SAID plus a resolution hint — alongside whatever else it carries. Ground: the reader of a render is a Disclosee, and Chain-Link Confidentiality's propagation is normative — "The terms-of-use of the original disclosure as applied to the original Disclosee MUST be applied by each subsequent Discloser to each subsequent Disclosee" (`v1.1:1804`) — while the governance stratum's only enforcement is that the recipient knew and accepted the terms; a render that shows encumbered data as unencumbered is the point where the chain silently breaks, and the break is invisible because the export still verifies. *Test:* render a credential whose `r` carries a chain-link-confidentiality clause; no visible terms indication fails; an export carrying attribute values with no terms reference fails.
- **RULE 17 — no network resolution as a side effect of rendering (added 2026-09-11, PRV-F3).** All material arrives from the host; an unresolved unit renders as H7/unperformable rather than triggering a fetch. A render-time fetch is a per-view disclosure to third parties (§c pair 4's caveat), and against an issuer-side surface such as a witness it re-creates the point-of-validation leak the Registrar/Observer split exists to delete (`v1.1:1707`; §B4's 2026-09-11 caveat). Where the *host* fetches, the freshness input names the surface class (verifier-side Observer vs issuer-side witness/Registrar) so the render can scope the claim to a horizon whose acquisition cost is known. *Test:* open a fixture with a dangling referent while monitoring network activity; any arcviz-initiated request fails.
- **RULE 18 — presentation provenance renders per axis P, and holder-facing views show no presenter indicator at all (added 2026-09-11).** In a verifier-facing view — any render reached by "a credential was disclosed to me" — the P state renders before, and at least as prominently as, the V vector: a green artifact-side vector with the presenter question silently unasked is this axis's naive lie (R9). P = unknown is the default and the truth of the default transport ([presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(c)); P = attested renders with Rule 3's full scoping — who attested, over what signature, addressed to whom, as of when; and in a holder-facing view (P = self) no presenter-control indicator appears, positive or negative — the viewing context forecloses the question, and any glyph would imply it is live. *Tests:* a verifier-facing fixture whose V vector is reachable without passing the P state fails; any "presenter verified" backed only by store presence fails (store presence warrants only R0, and a replayed credential's bytes satisfy it identically); a wallet-browse render carrying any presenter glyph fails.
- **RULE 19 — reserved-label hygiene: `_` is structure, never data (added 2026-09-11, from the `v1.1` rebase).** The bare label `_` is a virtual DAG-hop component that "appears only in an expansion of a DAG of chained ACDCs and MUST NOT appear as a field label in an ACDC" (`v1.1:55`, `:65`); a renderer walking an expanded DAG renders it as the hop it denotes — the traversal an Edge's `n` designates — never as a field named "_". And a `-` inside what the renderer takes to be a field label means a mis-parsed compact path or an invalid ACDC, since labels MUST NOT contain `-` (`v1.1:63`); it renders as a parse-level error state, not as data. *Tests:* render an expanded-DAG fixture; a field row labeled "_" fails; feed a label containing `-`; rendering it as an ordinary field fails.

## (e) Cells reasoned from spec rather than observed artifacts

The real vLEI chain is fully disclosed throughout and "cannot exercise arcviz's redacted/blinded rendering states" ([corpus-vlei-chain.md](corpus-vlei-chain.md) §5); its §4 inventory lists what production never exercises. Confidence tiers for this matrix's cells:

**Corpus-attested (real production data):** H1, H7 (absent root KEL), H9; the default-vs-explicit operator pair; V unperformable-with-material-in-hand; the no-`rev`-events revocation horizon; blinding *machinery* present but unexercised (leaf `u`).

**Worked-example-attested (keripy tests — engineered, but executable):** H3/R3 (attributive compaction), H4/R2 (aggregate selective disclosure), bulk-instance X (all three registry topologies), IPEX graduated exchange and `dp` grammar (the `dp` grammar is now also spec-attested — normative at `v1.1:1809-1994`; the panel finding that tiered it as unresolvable, SPC-F4, was withdrawn), negative/attenuation cases, and `E1E` (normative at `v1.1:1206`/`:1223`, exercised by both guardianship worked examples, no `corpus/` fixture yet).

**Reference-implementation-attested (added 2026-09-05 — direct source reads of keripy/KERIA/signify-ts, cited via [keripy-verification.md](keripy-verification.md) and [keria-signify-surface.md](keria-signify-surface.md)):** R0's store-presence guarantees and non-guarantees; pair 10 and the checks-6/7 {pass, not-pass} degradation; the "unperformed is the default" amendment to §B3/Rule 2. One cell moved up a tier in this revision: §B4's valid-then-vs-valid-now capability was previously paper-attested only, and is now grounded in a live witness endpoint (§B4 correction) — though scoped by which surface a host talks to.

**Fixture-attested (added 2026-09-11): a purpose-built corpus now exists at [`corpus/`](../../corpus/).** Twenty-nine fixture files — 25 ACDCs plus four registry-inception messages, each with a `.meta.json` sidecar naming the matrix cell and rule it exercises — every one a real, self-SAIDed artifact computed via keripy (SAIDs recompute; not hand-edited — [corpus/README.md](../../corpus/README.md)). Cells moved up from the spec-only tier: **H5** (`bare_agid`), **H6** (`metadata_acdc` — and per SKP-F2 this cell is simultaneously retiered from "no artifact anywhere" to "artifact exists; discriminator ambiguous", since keripy's own upstream test fixtures also emit empty-`u` on ordinary issued credentials, §B1's H6 note), **H8 and blinded edges/edge-groups/rules** (`compact_private_edge`, `blinded_edge_group`, `blinded_rule_group`), **committed absence at H9** (`optional_edge_absent`, with its present-edge control), the **`DI2I` and `NOT` operators** (`di2i_operator`, `not_operator`), **edge weights and `AVG`/`WAVG`** (`edge_group_operators`), and a **working edge-group** with two real co-endorsers (`working_edge_group` — generated directly via `acdcmap`/`Compactor`, not round-tripped through `Verifier.processCredential`, which cannot traverse edge-groups; its structure is real, its verifier-survival unproven — [corpus/README.md](../../corpus/README.md) "Known limitations"). Rules 1, 7, 9, 12 and 13 now have named exercising fixtures. **The tier's scope, stated honestly: these fixtures reproduce *structure*, not KEL/TEL facts.** No KEL or TEL events are simulated — no inception/delegation/rotation, no signing, no witnesses; AIDs are deterministic digest placeholders, and the registry files are inception messages only ([corpus/README.md](../../corpus/README.md)). So every signature, anchoring, delegation and revocation cell — checks 3–5, 8 and 9, R0's warrants, R8's staleness, pairs 6 and 7 — remains exercised by no fixture. One caveat inherited from SKP-F4: a stock-keripy-fed host will never deliver a `DI2I`- or `NOT`-bearing credential from its store, because the verifier refuses them at ingestion with a diagnosable `ValidationError` (panel SKP-F4 and the SEC-F6 verification note), so those two fixtures test arcviz's rendering for direct-feed or non-keripy hosts only.

**Spec-only — still no observed or constructed artifact. These cells are the least tested and reviewers should weight them accordingly:**

Phase 2C's original instruction — target the spec-only list first — is now largely discharged by the corpus. What remains: the erasure chain, a real-CLC rules fixture (Rule 16), and above all the KEL/TEL-dependent cells, which need event simulation rather than structural fixtures and are the corpus's stated out-of-scope.

## (f) Open questions

Logged rather than decided, per the brief. Questions since closed by recorded decisions are marked **Closed** in place, keeping their numbers so cross-references stay valid.

1. **Is there, or should there be, an ecosystem convention for attesting erasure?** §c pair 1's separator (attested cause) has a dossier-spec mechanism (Annotation Edges) but no ACDC-level convention. Already put to Daniel as [prior-art/papers-acdc.md](prior-art/papers-acdc.md) §(e)1; the host-interface shape in §c is this document's proposal, not an answer.
2. **Who owns the staleness threshold?** **Closed 2026-09-05:** the host supplies it, **per credential** — different credentials have different revocation-freshness profiles, so the interface cannot be a single global window; a credential with no supplied policy renders "no freshness policy supplied" as its own visible state ([PLAN.md](PLAN.md) decisions 2026-09-05, second set; §B4, Rule 4 updated accordingly).
3. **What may the renderer claim about revocation "freshness"?** **Rewritten 2026-09-05.** An earlier version of this question claimed a tension between "real-time status checks" ([keri-primer-real-time-status], `keri-primer.md:269`) and "reconciled over time" ([acdc-mtc-revocation-reconciled], `acdc-and-mtc.md:134`), unresolved between Daniel's own papers. **That claim is retracted: both passages were read directly on 2026-09-05 and they do not conflict** — the primer's "real-time status checks without privacy leaks" compares against X.509/OCSP (checking costs no privacy), while `acdc-and-mtc.md` compares against Merkle Tree Certificates and describes the *mechanism*, event reconciliation rather than a live query; achievable currency and the means of achieving it are not competing claims ([PLAN.md](PLAN.md) decisions 2026-09-05, second set). The "no revocation as of ⟨horizon⟩" rule (Rule 3) stands on the simpler ground that reconciliation has a horizon **by construction**: "Freshness here is about continuity rather than immediacy" (`acdc-and-mtc.md:132`), so a viewer knows only as much as the events it has replicated. What remains open is only how much fresher a horizon a given deployment can reach — a question about surfaces (§B4 correction), not about the papers.
4. **AGID composition into the top-level SAID** ([acdc-inventory.md](acdc-inventory.md) OQ5) — blocks confident rendering of H5's relationship to the enclosing artifact's identity; needs a keripy reference-implementation check before Rule 7's treatment is finalized.
5. **`E1E` and unknown operators — reopened and re-closed 2026-09-11 on corrected ground.** The 2026-09-05 closure ("unknown operators render as **unevaluated, never as invalid**") rested on two premises now stale: that `E1E` was "not in the published spec," and that "keripy's coerce-and-reject behavior" was the naive alternative. Both were `main`-branch readings. On the authoritative branch `E1E` is normative — in the unary operator table (`v1.1:1206`), with full identity-relation semantics (`v1.1:1223`), and named in the amended default-injection clause so its presence suppresses injection like `I2I`/`NI2I`/`DI2I` (`v1.1:1209`) — and upstream keripy implements it (SKP-F4's verification; the pre-#1527 "wrongly rejects" characterization is retired). Two panel dispositions land here: SEC-F1, which held the old closure contradicted default injection, was **withdrawn** — the panel read `main` ([reviews/2026-09-06-operator-semantics-adjudication.md](../../reviews/2026-09-06-operator-semantics-adjudication.md), 2026-09-10 correction) — and the adjudication's own interim replacement, a host-supplied "governing operator profile" input, was **retracted in the same correction**; this document never adopted it and adopts none now (there is one declared spec version and one operator table — an unreconciled repository is not a protocol ambiguity). What survives is the ruling re-scoped three ways, replacing the flat closure: (1) an operator normative on `v1.1` but unimplemented by the viewer's stack renders **unevaluated, never as invalid** — the original instinct, now correctly scoped, since a meaning exists that this verifier cannot compute; (2) a token outside the `v1.1` table (e.g. `I1I`) triggers default injection (`v1.1:1209`) and yields a **determinate verdict under a substituted operator** — rendered as that verdict *plus* the substitution, the declared token shown verbatim, never as unevaluated, which would suppress a real negative; (3) an m-ary operator minted by a sibling spec on an edge *group* (the dossier spec's `MxN`/`RMxN`/`MxQ`/`RMxQ` — [acdc-inventory.md](acdc-inventory.md) §7) sits outside the unary injection rule entirely — the group default is `AND` (`v1.1:1121`) — and renders under its own spec if the host knows it, else unevaluated with the token shown. This disposes of SKP-F4 (the stale motivating case) alongside the withdrawn SEC-F1.
6. **Does the renderer get exchange (IPEX) context?** §c pair 3's separator assumes the host can supply negotiation state. Whether arcviz's API should accept it, and what the render does without it, is a Phase 3/4 interface decision.
7. **Rendering the unrendered leakage of `a`-block disclosure to the *holder*.** Rule 6 shows the verifier what leaked. Whether arcviz (in holder-facing use, per [prior-art/papers-intent.md](prior-art/papers-intent.md) Q4) should *warn* pre-presentation is an affordance question, not a state question — parked for Phase 4. **Widened 2026-09-11 (PRV-F1):** the warning's subject is not only sibling labels but the forced disclosure of `a.i` and `a.dt` — the strongest correlator in the artifact goes out with the first disclosed field and cannot be withheld (§B1's forced-transition note), which is the larger fact a holder deciding what to disclose is not being shown.
8. **Validity-propagation default.** Rule 11 forbids an unlabeled roll-up but permits a labeled arcviz-default EGF rule. What that default should be (strict AND? nothing?) is deliberately not decided here.
9. **Whether H2's guessability deserves an active indicator** (§B5 inference) — surfacing "this hidden value is recoverable by dictionary attack over its schema" is correlation-honest but aggressive; no precedent exists in any audited product.
10. **Who re-verifies from a static export, and what do they need in hand?** (Added 2026-09-11 — split out of Rule 15, SKP-F5.) The historical-record half of that rule stands; the obligation to carry "what a re-verifier needs" (raw CESR? the anchoring KEL slice? the TEL? the schema? far nodes?) has no named consumer, no stated scope, and no fixture that could fail it. Parked until a demand test names the consumer and the material they actually re-verify from.
11. **Export retention.** (Added 2026-09-11 as "retention and join scope" per PRV-F4; **narrowed the same day** by Daniel Hardman's scope ruling.) The join half of this question lapsed with the retirement of cross-presentation joining: arcviz loads one presentation at a time, so there is no join log and no set of presentations retained in joinable form — the artifact the finding worried about is never created. What survives is Rule 15's other durable artifact: an **export** has a lifetime nothing currently bounds, and a holder who exports a view has produced something that outlives the presentation it came from. Export lifetime and per-credential export permission remain undecided, with no-persistence the default posture.
12. **Presentation provenance's own open questions** (added 2026-09-11) live in [presenter-control.md](presenter-control.md) §(g): whether KERIA's escrow/notification layer deduplicates replayed grants (an absence-of-evidence, not a confirmed absence), whether the `_verify_representation` binding pattern generalizes to presenting pre-existing credentials, and whether "holder binding" is safe terminology given ACDC's Issuee/Holder distinction. Axis P and Rule 18 do not depend on their resolution — P = unknown is honest under every answer.
